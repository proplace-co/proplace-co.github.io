Three dynamics are at play today: sovereign digital infrastructure is gaining momentum in France, the pharmaceutical supply chain continues to attract European private equity at sustained valuations, and deep tech fundraisings — chips, drones, medical imaging — confirm that capital is now seeking assets with real-world foundations as much as software promise. Orange and Morrison: €3 Billion to Keep Infrastructure in European Hands

Source: latribune.fr → · Sector Developer & IT Infrastructure — 📬 subscribe to the Developer & IT Infrastructure newsletter

Orange is partnering with New Zealand investment firm Morrison to create a joint venture dedicated to data centers in France, backed by an investment program of €3 billion targeting 400 MW of capacity — nearly ten times the group's current capacity in France. Five data centers across four campuses (Chevilly-Larue, Aubervilliers, Chartres, and a fourth site) will be expanded. Completion is expected in the first quarter of 2027.

The immediate takeaway: Orange is catching up with American hyperscalers and responding to enterprise cloud demand that exceeds local capacities.

But what's at stake is more structural: by bringing in a specialized infrastructure partner rather than an American cloud provider, Orange is building a sovereign toll position — an operator that leases computing power without relying on a foreign provider to own it. Morrison brings the financial discipline of an infrastructure fund (long-term, predictable returns); Orange brings strategic land, fiber, and enterprise customer relationships. For a French CIO looking to host sensitive data without using AWS or Azure, this type of offering genuinely changes the landscape of available options.

Apax Acquires Gerresheimer's Packaging Businesses for €1.5 Billion

Source: privateequitywire.co.uk →

Apax Partners is acquiring the two pharmaceutical plastic packaging divisions of German company Gerresheimer — the global Primary Packaging Plastics division and the US subsidiary Centor — in a carve-out valued at €1.5 billion including debt. Together, these entities generated approximately €570 million in revenue in the last fiscal year and employ 2,400 people across 16 sites in nine countries. Gerresheimer will use the proceeds to deleverage its balance sheet, following a difficult period marked by accounting reporting delays and a BaFin audit.

The classic pharmaceutical carve-out here conceals a dual logic: Gerresheimer sells what is good to save itself, and Apax buys what is good to value it separately. Pharmaceutical plastics — vials, closures, dosing systems — is a niche with high regulatory barriers and a captive customer base (laboratories rarely change primary packaging suppliers mid-product cycle). Apax is acquiring a profitable, cash-generating asset that Gerresheimer was offloading for balance sheet reasons rather than strategic ones. For an industrial acquirer or another fund looking to build a pharma packaging platform, the assets that will emerge from this portfolio in five years will likely be more expensive.

Bawag – PTSB: 75% Vote to Close Irish Privatization

Bawag, an Austrian banking group, has submitted a €1.6 billion takeover bid for Permanent TSB (PTSB), an Irish bank in which the state holds a 57.4% stake. Shareholders are voting today at an extraordinary general meeting in Dublin. The transaction requires 75% acceptance — a high threshold, especially since advisory firm Glass Lewis recommends rejection (valuation deemed stretched given the rising share price), while ISS supports the transaction. The offer of €2.97 per share remains below the year-end net book value.

The sticking point is arithmetic: the state, with 57.4%, cannot alone reach the required 75%. It needs to convince a portion of minority shareholders who, in turn, are looking at a rising share price and a discount to net assets. Bawag is buying an Irish deposit franchise and a position in a concentrated banking market with three players — a distribution annuity that the current price may not yet fully reflect. If the vote fails, PTSB remains in limbo, and Bawag will have to raise its offer or walk away.

Schneider Electric in Talks to Acquire Shelly Group

Source: zonebourse.com →

Schneider Electric is reportedly in advanced negotiations to acquire Bulgarian company Shelly Group, a specialist in connected devices for smart homes and IoT, with an indicative offer of around €70 per share valuing Shelly at approximately €1.3 billion. Shelly has confirmed the existence of discussions; the stock jumped 10% to €64 on the announcement. Negotiations also cover the founders' majority stakes.

Schneider is not acquiring a competitor: it is acquiring a distribution channel and a consumer software layer that it cannot build internally. Shelly is the most influential brand on Swedish social networks in 2025 in its segment — which means an already established community of installers and prosumers. For Schneider, whose strategy revolves around energy management in buildings, integrating Shelly means moving one step closer to the end-user and locking in an ecosystem of connected objects before Asian players do it instead.

Air France-KLM and Lufthansa Submit Binding Offers for TAP

Source: famagusta-gazette.com →

Air France-KLM and Deutsche Lufthansa have both submitted binding proposals to Parpublica, the Portuguese state holding company, for the acquisition of a 44.9% stake in TAP Air Portugal. Air France-KLM's offer covers all activities (passengers, cargo, loyalty, MRO); Lufthansa positions Lisbon as a strategic hub for the South Atlantic. The Portuguese government has 30 days to review the dossiers, with a decision expected in September.

TAP is one of the few direct gateways to Brazil and Lusophone Africa from Europe — a network that neither candidate can replicate organically. The winner is not buying planes; they are buying traffic rights and a loyal diaspora clientele. For Air France-KLM, the stakes are also defensive: allowing Lufthansa to establish itself in Lisbon would strengthen the Frankfurt hub on transatlantic routes to the detriment of Paris and Amsterdam.

Legrand Makes its Seventh Acquisition in 2026

Source: lepopulaire.fr →

Legrand announces a new acquisition, its seventh since the beginning of the year. The target and amount are not disclosed, but the pace — seven transactions in seven months — confirms the Limoges-based group's systematic build-up strategy in electrical and digital building infrastructure. A named acquirer, a consistent sectoral thesis, details to follow.

BC Partners Acquires Co-Controlling Stake in InfoRLife

BC Partners is acquiring an approximately 50% stake in Swiss company InfoRLife, the only pure-play specialist in the development of dossiers and contract manufacturing of ready-to-use intravenous infusion bags. The valuation is reportedly around $1.5 billion according to sources. ACS Dobfar, the Italian parent company and founder of the group, retains the remainder. This is the second investment from BC Partners Fund XII, which is currently raising towards €5 billion.

Ready-to-use IV bags are a structurally growing segment: hospitals are adopting them to reduce dosing errors and improve logistical efficiency. InfoRLife is the only pure-play B2B player in this niche in Europe — a specialization annuity that BC Partners will seek to expand through external growth, as it has done with Biogaran and Synthon. For a French healthcare industrialist, this type of specialized CDMO platform is exactly what large laboratories are looking to outsource more in the coming years.

Relais Group Acquires 70% of Tershine

Source: finanznachrichten.de →

Relais Group, a Finnish group specializing in vehicle parts and products, is acquiring 70% of Swedish company Tershine AB, a premium brand of car care products, from its founder Tobias Ericson for €14 million. Ericson remains at the helm and retains a direct stake. Tershine will be consolidated into Relais' Products & Solutions division starting August 2026.

Disciplined build-up in specialized automotive distribution: Relais acquires a brand with a strong community (the second most influential brand on Swedish social networks in its segment in 2025) and retains the founding entrepreneur — a classic formula for acquiring an affinity brand without killing what makes it thrive.

Greif Acquires Envaplast in Spain

Source: greif.com → · Sector Industrial Tech & Manufacturing — 📬 subscribe to the Industrial Tech & Manufacturing newsletter

US company Greif (industrial packaging, NYSE: GEF) is finalizing the acquisition of Envaplast, a Spanish manufacturer of small polymer containers based near Valencia, primarily serving the agrochemical industry. The amount was not disclosed. The transaction strengthens Greif's Customized Polymer Solutions division and gives it a foothold in Spain to accelerate in the EMEA market.

Disciplined bolt-on in a resilient niche market: agrochemicals consume specialized containers in regular volumes, little sensitive to economic cycles. Greif is plugging a geographical blind spot.

The Exploration Company in Talks to Raise $300 Million

The Exploration Company, a French startup specializing in launchers and orbital services, is reportedly in advanced discussions to close a $300 million (approximately €276 million) funding round. No investors have been officially named at this stage.

In a sector where the costs of accessing orbit have fallen but where orbital service infrastructure (refueling, in-orbit logistics) remains to be built, The Exploration Company is playing the next frontier card: no longer the launch, but what is done once in orbit. A round of this size, if confirmed, would position the company among the rare European players capable of keeping pace with American and Emirati investments in the segment.

Mistral AI: Microsoft Contract and Samsung Entry in Negotiation

Source: maddyness.com →

Mistral AI secures a structuring contract with Microsoft and is simultaneously negotiating Samsung's entry into its capital. Amounts are not disclosed in either case.

Two seemingly distinct moves, one common logic: Mistral seeks to multiply its industrial anchor points — a dominant cloud client for distribution, a chip and device manufacturer for hardware integration. Microsoft distributes, Samsung embeds. For a company whose value relies on model quality and European sovereignty, forging these two links without losing editorial independence is the central balancing act for the coming months.

AB Tasty: Social Plan, Nearly 80 Positions Cut in France

Source: maddyness.com → · Sector B2B Software & Cloud — 📬 subscribe to the B2B Software & Cloud newsletter

AB Tasty, a French publisher of digital experience optimization solutions, is cutting nearly 80 positions in France as part of a social plan. Amount and timeline are not specified.

Conversion optimization is a market under pressure: generative AI internalizes functions that specialized SaaS tools previously billed separately. AB Tasty is not alone in this position — it is the entire intermediate martech segment that is recalibrating its workforce in the face of algorithmic competition that it cannot absorb as quickly as it deploys.

Legend Media in Negotiations to Bring in an Investor

Source: maddyness.com →

Legend, a French media company, is reportedly in discussions to welcome a new investor into its capital. Neither the investor's identity nor the amount has been disclosed. In a French media sector constantly seeking viable economic models, the operation remains to be followed upon confirmation.

ChipAgents Raises $60 Million to Design Chips with AI

Source: boursorama.com →

ChipAgents closes a $60 million (approximately €55 million) funding round to accelerate its AI agent platform dedicated to semiconductor design.

Chip design is one of the last bastions where human engineering remains the bottleneck: a design cycle can take years and involve hundreds of engineers. If AI agents can compress this cycle, the cost of entry into custom chip design drops — which interests hyperscalers who want their own processors as much as industrialists who want ASICs without going through large design foundries. ChipAgents is betting that the next semiconductor battle will be fought as much in design software as in manufacturing itself.

Agon Raises $30 Million for Virtual Anti-Drone Battlefields

Source: tech.eu →

British defense startup Agon raises $30 million (approximately €28 million) to develop virtual simulation environments for training and testing counter-drone systems.

The war in Ukraine has transformed drones into a primary weapon and counter-drones into an urgent market. Simulation is the cheapest and fastest way to iterate on neutralization tactics without destroying real equipment. Agon sells not weapons but training — a recurring, scalable model that armies and defense industrialists can buy without the export constraints that weigh on hardware.

Qureight Raises $20 Million in Series B for AI-Powered Clinical Imaging

Source: tech.eu → · Sector HealthTech & Digital Health — 📬 subscribe to the HealthTech & Digital Health newsletter

Qureight closes a $20 million Series B (approximately €18 million) to expand its AI-powered medical imaging platform to clinical trials.

Imaging analysis in clinical trials is a slow, costly process subject to human reading biases. A platform that standardizes and accelerates this reading creates directly measurable value for trial sponsors — reducing time and cost per patient. In the context of today's dominant news (Dassault Systèmes consolidating its positions in life sciences, BC Partners investing in pharmaceutical injectables), Qureight illustrates the same bottom-up movement: capital seeks assets that fit into the pharma value chain where friction is greatest.

Passionfroot Raises $15 Million in Series A for B2B Creator Marketing

Source: startupresearcher.com → · Sector Horizontal & Productivity SaaS — 📬 subscribe to the Horizontal & Productivity SaaS newsletter

Passionfroot, a Berlin-based startup, closes a $15 million Series A (approximately €14 million) led by Insight Partners, to fund its expansion in the United States and Brazil. The platform manages the entire B2B creator marketing campaign cycle — from discovery to payment — via an AI agent named Zest, backed by a proprietary creator data graph.

B2B creator marketing is the answer to classic advertising saturation: when professional buyers trust an independent expert on LinkedIn more than an advertisement, the brand has an interest in funding the expert. Passionfroot is building the infrastructure for this market — not the content, but the pipeline between the brand and the creator — a potentially very defensible platform-toll position if the data graph becomes rich enough.

NGE Acquires Stake in Gaïa Energy Systems

Source: lechodusolaire.fr → · Sector Climate & Energy Tech — 📬 subscribe to the Climate & Energy Tech newsletter

NGE, France's fourth-largest construction group, is acquiring a stake in Gaïa Energy Systems, a Marseille-based developer and operator of renewable energy (wind, hydropower, geothermal, photovoltaic). The amount was not disclosed. The transaction, completed on July 7, gives NGE immediate access to an operational portfolio (five hydroelectric plants, one ground-mounted PV plant, two rooftop portfolios) and aims for 200 MW of capacity by 2030. Gaïa has also acquired three 13 MW floating photovoltaic plant projects in Occitanie and Nouvelle-Aquitaine.

NGE is not diversifying: it is vertically integrating. By becoming both co-financier, constructor, and operator of renewable energy plants, the group transforms each construction site into a recurring asset rather than a one-off contract. This is the concession model applied to the energy transition — and for a construction group, it is a way to smooth its revenues over much longer horizons than the traditional public procurement cycle.

Reboat Raises €3 Million to Industrialize Sailboat Refurbishment

Source: renseignementeconomique.fr →

Reboat (Lorient) closes a €3 million round with the entry of Seventure Partners (a subsidiary of Natixis Investment Managers) into its capital, alongside historical investors and founders of Click&Boat and Virtual Regatta. The transaction includes €1 million in convertible bonds. The objective: to standardize and industrialize the refurbishment of sailboats with a guarantee, following the model of what refurbished goods have done for consumer electronics.

The used boating market is fragmented, without quality standards or guarantees — exactly the terrain where an industrialization platform can create a trust premium. Seventure's entry signals that the model is considered scalable, not artisanal. For an investor seeking circular economy with strong local roots, Reboat is a bet on market structuring rather than technological innovation.

Midipile Launches €2 Million Fundraising for its Electric Micro-Utility Vehicle

Source: renseignementeconomique.fr →

Midipile is opening a €2 million fundraising round to finance the distribution of its one-meter-wide electric micro-utility vehicle. The vehicle targets constrained urban uses — last-mile delivery, interventions in historic city centers — where conventional utility vehicles can no longer pass.

Seed round in a structuring urban utility mobility segment. The one-meter-wide format is a direct response to increasing traffic restrictions in European city centers: less a disruptive innovation than a precise adaptation to a regulatory constraint that will only strengthen.
