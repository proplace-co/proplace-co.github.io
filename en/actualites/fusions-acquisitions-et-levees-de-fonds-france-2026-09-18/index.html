September 18 illustrates a clear bifurcation of capital: on one hand, considerable sums are pouring into AI infrastructure and new physical frontiers—computation, space, robotics—with nine-figure rounds that would have seemed improbable three years ago; on the other, a myriad of more modest rounds are funding precise verticals, from women's health to industrial security to acoustics for robots. Today's M&A is discreet in volume but reveals two opposing logics: French industrial consolidation in construction, and high-dose pharmaceutical alliances in bispecifics. No forced common thread—but a sectoral density that deserves to be read in detail.

    CIC Conseil accompagne la cession de Braja-Vésigné à Spie batignolles

    M&AFrance

    Braja-Vésigné joins Spie batignolles: the silent consolidation of French construction

    Source: news.google.com → · Sector Horizontal & Productivity SaaS — 📬 subscribe to the Horizontal & Productivity SaaS newsletter

    Spie batignolles acquires Braja-Vésigné, a transaction advised by CIC Conseil, for an undisclosed amount. Braja-Vésigné is a civil engineering and specialty works player, a segment where critical mass is a prerequisite for accessing large public markets and infrastructure tenders.

    The obvious takeaway: a classic build-up in a fragmented sector. But what's at play here is more structural. French construction is going through a paradoxical period—residential contraction, but a revival of public infrastructure spending (networks, energy, defense). Groups capable of rapidly absorbing specialized SMEs are positioning themselves to capture this flow of public contracts before the window closes—consolidation is not defensive, it is offensive on the cycle.

  • Dualitas

    Roche rachète Dualitas pour 1 Md$ en accord bispecific

    M&A920 M€Exited (Acquired / Buyout)

    Roche bets a billion on Dualitas: bispecifics as the new oncological standard

    Source: news.google.com → · Sector Biotech & Pharma — 📬 subscribe to the Biotech & Pharma newsletter

    Roche signs a licensing agreement with Dualitas for $1 billion (approximately €920 million) for a bispecific molecule—an antibody capable of simultaneously targeting two distinct receptors on a tumor cell.

    The obvious reading: Roche is buying external pipeline to offset the erosion of its historical blockbusters, a classic Big Pharma strategy in "open innovation" mode.

    But the choice of bispecifics says something more precise. This therapeutic format is redefining oncology: where classic antibodies target one, bispecifics orchestrate two attack mechanisms. This billion isn't funding a drug; it's funding a position in what could become the standard of care for the next decade in oncology—and Roche is paying dearly to avoid having to license technology from its competitors in five years. For French capital active in biotech, this is a valuation signal: independent European bispecific platforms have rarely been so attractive.

  • Crusoe

    Crusoe lève 3,9 Md$ pour ses data centers IA modulaires

    FundraisingUnited States3,6 Md€Series E+

    Crusoe raises $3.9 billion: when AI infrastructure becomes an asset class in its own right

    Source: techcrunch.com → · Sector Developer & IT Infrastructure — 📬 subscribe to the Developer & IT Infrastructure newsletter

    Crusoe raises $3.9 billion (approximately €3.6 billion), bringing its valuation to $30.9 billion. The purpose: to build massive data centers and modular "AI factories"—dense computing units, rapidly deployable, designed for model training and inference.

    The surface reading: another AI infrastructure player raising huge sums in an overheated market.

    But Crusoe carries a more interesting thesis than average. The company was built on an original idea—using flared natural gas (flaring from oil sites, burned to waste by operators) to power servers. What was a niche energy arbitrage has become a differentiating supply strategy at a time when the battle for cheap electricity has become the real bottleneck for AI.

    What this round reveals: competition in AI infrastructure is no longer just about GPUs or software—it's about access to energy. Crusoe isn't raising to buy chips; it's raising to secure a resource its competitors don't have. For a European investor, this is a reminder that AI infrastructure projects that haven't solved the energy question upstream are structurally fragile.

  • Impulse Space

    Impulse Space lève 308 M$ en série D à 5,4 Md$ de valorisation

    FundraisingUnited States283 M€Series D

    Impulse Space: $308 million for orbital propulsion, valuation at $5.4 billion

    Source: news.google.com →

    Impulse Space adds $308 million (approximately €283 million) to its Series D, bringing its valuation to $5.4 billion. The company develops orbital transfer vehicles—"space tugs" capable of moving payloads from one orbit to another after separation from the main launcher.

    This segment is still not very visible to the general public, but it is strategically crucial. The launcher puts you in low Earth orbit; the transfer vehicle takes you where your satellite needs to operate. With the proliferation of constellations (communications, observation, defense), the demand for precise orbital mobility is exploding. Impulse Space bets that the space value chain will become even more verticalized—and that the "last mile orbital" link will become as indispensable as the launcher itself. The $5.4 billion valuation for a company that does not yet have large-scale revenues indicates that investors are betting on position, not on current cash flows.
