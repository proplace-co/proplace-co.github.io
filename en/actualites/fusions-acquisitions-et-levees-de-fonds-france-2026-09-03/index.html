  • Alan

    Alan accélère son expansion en Afrique avec l'acquisition de Tanel

    M&AFrance600 M€Series E+

    Alan acquires Tanel: the first African foothold for a unicorn that understood its saturated market is at home

    Source: lefigaro.fr → · Sector InsurTech — 📬 subscribe to the InsurTech newsletter

    Alan finalizes the acquisition of Tanel, a Senegalese health insurance startup operating in Senegal and Ivory Coast, for an undisclosed amount. Tanel has 10,000 insured members and over 200 corporate clients; Alan now claims 1.2 million in Europe.

    The immediate interpretation: a French unicorn diversifying geographically, attracted by a market estimated at €600 million in French-speaking Africa, strong economic growth, and still very low health insurance penetration rates.

    What's more interesting is the sequence: Alan had taken a minority stake in Tanel two years ago, before converting this investment into a full acquisition when the founders wanted to scale up. This is an exercised option logic, not opportunism—Alan observed, validated the local model, then absorbed. For an insurtech whose value relies on health data and prevention, Africa is not a fallback market: it's a territory where medical consumption habits are forming now, before traditional players establish themselves. Arriving in 2026 with a mature technological platform and experienced local teams means laying down data infrastructure on a continent of 400 million French speakers before the battle begins. The announcement of an imminent entry into Saudi Arabia, in the same week, confirms that Alan is building a deliberate geography: markets where private health insurance is still to be structured.

    For a French investor or executive looking at Africa: the acquisition-option (minority first, control then) is the sequence that minimizes execution risk in complex regulatory markets. Alan did it in two years. The window on several French-speaking African markets remains open, but it is closing.

    CPP Investments et Equinix finalisent l'acquisition d'atNorth pour 4 milliards de dollars

    M&AIceland3,7 Md€Exited (Acquired / Buyout)

    CPP Investments takes control of atNorth: Canadian pension fund bets on Nordic cold

    Source: peinsights.substack.com →

    The Canadian pension fund CPP Investments acquires a controlling stake of approximately 51% in atNorth, a data center operator and developer in the five Nordic countries, in a transaction valued at approximately $4 billion. Equinix co-invests approximately 34%, and Partners Group—the former shareholder—reinvests approximately 10%. The entire deal is supported by $4.1 billion in financing structured to fund both the transaction and the development pipeline.

    atNorth's pipeline covers eight operational data centers and several mega-sites under construction in Finland, Denmark, Sweden, Norway, plus a metropolitan site in Stockholm.

    What deserves attention is the location logic. The Nordic countries offer three simultaneous advantages that few regions combine: abundant and renewable electricity (hydroelectric in Norway and Sweden, geothermal in Iceland), natural cold that reduces cooling costs, and political and regulatory stability. For AI data centers with massive energy consumption, these three factors make the Nordic region a rare asset. CPP Investments, which manages the retirement savings of millions of Canadians, is not doing venture capital: it is looking for assets with predictable returns over twenty years. That a fund of this nature takes control of computing infrastructure reveals that AI data centers are migrating towards the category of real assets—just like highways or water networks. The reinvestment by Partners Group, which could have exited, is an additional signal: even the former owner believes the valuation still has room to grow.

    Lottomatica fusionne avec Cirsa (jeux et paris) pour créer géant mondial

    M&ASpain2,8 Md€Exited (Acquired / Buyout)

    Lottomatica merges with Cirsa: birth of the world's second-largest listed gaming operator

    Source: theigaming.eu → · Sector B2B Software & Cloud — 📬 subscribe to the B2B Software & Cloud newsletter

    The boards of Lottomatica (Italy, listed in Milan) and Cirsa (Spain) have approved an all-share merger by absorption. Cirsa shareholders will receive 0.668 Lottomatica shares for each Cirsa share. Upon completion, Lottomatica's historical shareholders will hold 67.5% of the new entity, and Cirsa's 32.5%. Blackstone, Cirsa's main shareholder since its acquisition in 2018, will hold approximately 24% and become the largest individual shareholder of the combined company.

    The pro forma adjusted EBITDA is approximately €2 billion and revenue exceeds €4.4 billion, with number one positions in Italy and Spain, plus Cirsa's operations in Latin America.

    The all-share structure is significant: Blackstone is not exiting—it is converting its Cirsa stake into a stake in the listed entity, with an implicit discount of 6x 2026 EBITDA for Cirsa. This is a bet on synergies and on the future liquidity of a larger, more tradable stock. For Blackstone, which took Cirsa private in 2018, this is an elegant partial exit via indirect listing, without having to organize a separate IPO in a difficult market for new introductions. The result is a European champion in regulated gaming, with balanced exposure between mature markets (Italy, Spain) and growth markets (Latin America).

The chosen structure (WHP for licenses, G-III for operations) is a clean separation between the brand as an intangible asset and the brand as a commercial flow. Marc Jacobs retains artistic direction: he remains the guarantor of desirability, while the buyers manage distribution.

  • Utility Innovation Group

    Vertiv va racheter Utility Innovation Group pour 2,6 Md$ (1,45 Md$ base)

    M&AUnited States2,4 Md€Exited (Acquired / Buyout)

    Vertiv acquires Utility Innovation Group: when power supply becomes the strategic asset for AI data centers

    Source: boursorama.com → · Sector Data & Analytics — 📬 subscribe to the Data & Analytics newsletter

    Vertiv acquires Utility Innovation Group, a specialist in microgrids and advanced power control systems, for $1.45 billion in cash, with an earn-out of up to $1.15 billion based on performance—totaling up to $2.6 billion.

    The official reading: Vertiv, already a leader in power and cooling equipment for data centers, adds the ability to guarantee electricity through hybrid sources (grid + on-site generation).

    What is at stake is more structural. The bottleneck for AI data centers is no longer computing—it's electricity. A new-generation data center can consume as much as a medium-sized city; grid connection times often exceed four to five years in most Western countries. The ability to guarantee power upon commissioning, without waiting for connection, has become the limiting factor in the race for compute. By acquiring UIGroup, Vertiv is no longer just selling power cabinets: it is selling time—the possibility for a data center operator to go from site selection to commissioning without being blocked by grid constraints. Vertiv's CEO explicitly stated: "competitive advantage depends on how quickly you can go from site selection to commissioning." The $1.15 billion earn-out indexed to future profits reveals that UIGroup is still in the early stages of revenue generation—Vertiv is betting on a growth in microgrid demand that is not yet fully visible in the accounts.

    For any player building or financing data centers in Europe: the electrical constraint is now the primary project risk, ahead of regulation or land.

Cogedis-Icoopa: when two Breton cooperatives create France's leading independent accounting firm

Source: bretagne-economique.com →

Cogedis (€90M revenue, 960 employees, 80 agencies) and Icoopa (20 agencies, 250 employees) merge on September 1, 2026, to form a combined entity with €110M in revenue, 1,200 employees, 100 agencies, and 23,000 clients—the leading independent national accounting group.

The surface reading: defensive consolidation in the face of digital transformation and mandatory electronic invoicing.

The reality is more precise. Both entities share the same cooperative history and the same clientele—artisans, farmers, rural SMEs—which is exactly what large national firms have never served well. The merger does not seek to compete with the Big Four: it creates critical mass to invest in digital tools (electronic invoicing, data exploitation) without diluting them in an all-out external growth strategy. The result is a solid niche player, rooted in the real Breton and Norman economy, which can now absorb the technological infrastructure costs that neither could bear alone. This is the same logic as the regional Crédit Agricole banks: independence as a competitive advantage, size as a condition for survival.
