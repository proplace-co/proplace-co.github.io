    Merkur acquires Casigrangi and triggers a tender offer for SFC: 195% premium for seven French casinos

    Source: sccgmanagement.com →

    Merkur Casinos (a subsidiary of MERKUR.COM AG, Gauselmann family foundation, over €2 billion in revenue in 2024) acquires 95% of Casigrangi — a holding company that controls 81.21% of Société Française de Casinos — from Groupe Philippe Ginestet and DOFA for €31.5 million, or €6.19 per SFC share in transparency.     What Merkur is buying is a regulated geographical presence in France: seven casinos, three on the Mediterranean coast and one in Puy-de-Dôme. Casino licenses in France are contingent, non-reproducible, and their value is structurally disconnected from current operating results. Merkur — which has exceeded 15,000 employees and €2 billion in revenue in Europe — follows the same logic as Novomatic and Vikings before it: consolidate the French independent casino market, whose regulatory assets are worth far more than their income statements suggest.

    For Philippe Ginestet, who entered at €1.70 in 2020, the exit at €6.19 represents 3.6 times his investment in five and a half years — a performance that illustrates that the value of a physical gaming license in France lies in regulatory scarcity, not in EBITDA.

  • HR Path

    M&AFrance500 M€

    HR Path: when buying once a month becomes the industrial model

    Source: lenouveleconomiste.fr →

    HR Path, an HR consulting and HRIS integration firm backed by Ardian, has completed 22 acquisitions in two years to reach €360 million in revenue and 2,600 employees across 30 countries.     For a digital consulting or IT services player considering international expansion, HR Path illustrates that the window for acquiring mid-sized targets in Europe is closing: private equity-backed aggregators are sweeping the market faster than organic growth can.

    Decathlon acquiert 50% de Sport 2000 pour renforcer sa présence rurale et montagne

    M&AFrancePublic

    Decathlon takes 50% of Sport 2000: not an acquisition, but a takeover of independent retailers' supply chain

    Source: laprovence.com →

    Decathlon announces the acquisition of 50% of Sport 2000 France's central purchasing body, owned by the Céraclès cooperative, for an undisclosed amount — the transaction is expected to close in 2027, subject to approval from the French Competition Authority.

    The immediate interpretation: the French leader in sports equipment is partnering with a network of independent retailers to gain more leverage against Intersport, which absorbed Go Sport in 2023. Two major brands joining forces to counter a third bloc.

    But let's look at what Decathlon is actually buying. Sport 2000 France's central purchasing body had a turnover of €98 million in 2025, with a net profit of only €215,000 — an asset almost worthless on paper. What Decathlon is acquiring is not a stream of profits: it's the influential position over the purchasing, logistics, and marketing of several hundred independent points of sale. By entering the central purchasing body, Decathlon is not buying stores — it is embedding itself at the heart of the purchasing decisions of retailers who will remain legally independent, but whose supplies will now pass through a structure where Decathlon holds an equal stake.

    For a sports supplier or a foreign competitor looking to enter France, the window is closing: the distribution flows of the independent segment are being absorbed into Decathlon's orbit, without the affected brands losing their identity.

The logic is clear: acquire raw volume from Shell (which is divesting its non-strategic renewable assets) while monetizing already developed assets with an institutional investor — which partially finances the acquisition. TotalEnergies retains operations and recurring revenues, KKR provides patient capital.

This is the financing model for large-scale energy transition: majors develop, insurers and pension funds finance through stable minority co-investments. TotalEnergies positions itself as a developer-operator rather than a full owner — which frees up capital for the next acquisition cycle.
