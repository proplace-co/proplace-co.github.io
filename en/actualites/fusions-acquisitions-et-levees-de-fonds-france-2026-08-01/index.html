August 1st opens with a dual movement: on one side, French and European industrialists are strengthening their positions—in submarine warfare, in metals, in fleet mobility—with a long-term conviction that contrasts with the usual summer caution; on the other, a flow of modest but precise fundraisings, from robot-mapped skin to battery-free sensors, which outlines the technological layer of the next decade. In between, Couche-Tard finally achieves its big move in Central Europe after its Japanese failure, and Europe soberly counts its unicorns.

In focus — the deals we decoded

Thales + Exail: 3.9 billion to close the submarine entry point

Source: lavoixdefrance.fr → · Sector The Physical World — 📬 subscribe to the The Physical World newsletter

Thales finalizes the acquisition of Exail for 3.9 billion euros, securing the French leader in underwater drones and inertial navigation systems.

The immediate interpretation is one of defense consolidation: a large system absorbing a niche gem to bolster its catalog. But what is at stake here is more structural. Inertial navigation—the ability to orient oneself without GPS, without signal, underwater—is one of the rare technologies where dependence on external infrastructure can cost a mission. In a context where submarine cables have become targets and where submarine warfare is experiencing its first revolution since the 1940s, Exail is not just another product in a catalog: it is a building block of strategic autonomy.

What Thales is buying for 3.9 billion is not revenue—it's the ability to offer its sovereign clients a complete chain, from detection to navigation, without a foreign link; in a sector where the buyer is the state and technological dependence is a political vulnerability, this vertical integration is worth far more than its apparent multiple.

Arval + Athlon: BNP creates the European co-leader in fleet management

Source: fr.finance.yahoo.com →

BNP Paribas, through its subsidiary Arval, finalizes the acquisition of Athlon—formerly a subsidiary of Mercedes-Benz Group—for a balance sheet impact of approximately 200 million euros in expected net contribution by the end of the third year, with a CET1 ratio affected by approximately -13 basis points.

The immediate result: a combined fleet of 2.3 million vehicles, positioning Arval as the European co-leader in long-term leasing.

The operation deserves a moment's consideration. Mercedes is divesting Athlon not because long-term leasing is a bad business—it's precisely the opposite—but because owning it ties up regulatory capital at a time when the manufacturer needs liquidity to finance its electric transition. BNP, for its part, has a banking balance sheet that naturally absorbs this type of long-duration asset: fleet leasing is, for a bank, a form of credit backed by a physical asset with a predictable return. The expected return on invested capital of 18% signals that the operation was structured with discipline.

What BNP is buying here is also a position in the energy transition of corporate fleets: whoever manages 2.3 million vehicles in long-term leasing controls renewal decisions, and thus the flow towards electric vehicles—a consulting and infrastructure annuity that goes far beyond simple automotive financing.

Icade + Comet: a real estate company enters coworking

Source: maddyness.com → · Sector Construction & PropTech — 📬 subscribe to the Construction & PropTech newsletter

Icade takes a majority stake in Comet, a coworking space operator. Amount undisclosed.

A real estate company acquiring a flex-office operator is the structural response to tertiary vacancy: rather than leasing bare square meters to companies that no longer want them on 3-6-9 leases, Icade is internalizing the operator that transforms these square meters into a service. The logic is consistent—and it says something about the state of the Parisian office market: long-term leases are dead for a growing fraction of demand, and the only way to monetize assets is to become the operator oneself.

The symmetrical risk is real: Icade is moving from the business of landlord, which it masters, to that of service operator, which requires a very different customer culture and operational responsiveness—the question is not whether coworking has a future, but whether a listed real estate company is the right operator.

Couche-Tard + Zabka: 8 billion to erase the Japanese failure

Source: rte.ie → · Sector Retail & E-commerce Tech — 📬 subscribe to the Retail & E-commerce Tech newsletter

Alimentation Couche-Tard launches a voluntary public tender offer for Zabka, the Polish convenience store giant, for approximately 8 billion euros—its largest acquisition to date. Shareholders CVC Capital and Partners Group have already indicated their intention to tender their shares, representing approximately 57% of the capital.

The 9.4% premium over the last closing price is modest—a sign that the market had already anticipated an exit by the PE fund shareholders.

The context is important: Couche-Tard had attempted to acquire Seven & I (7-Eleven) for 46 billion last year, before abandoning the effort. Zabka is a significant consolation, but it is also a strategically coherent operation: approximately 13,000 convenience stores in Poland and Romania, a proven franchise model, and a position in Central Europe where consumption continues to converge towards Western European standards. The combined group would reach 30,300 points of sale, with Europe increasing from 30% to 60% of the total base.

The abandonment of fuel as a margin driver—explicitly cited by management—is the real signal: Couche-Tard is betting that the urban convenience store, without pumps, is the retail format that best resists e-commerce and the energy transition; Zabka, designed for pedestrians and passing customers, is the purest thesis of this bet.

  • nasz sklep s.a. — Their local network in Poland would consolidate their post-Żabka presence and diversify their local offering

Derichebourg + Scholz Recycling: 480 million to change scale

Source: lesechos.fr → · Sector Climate & Energy Tech — 📬 subscribe to the Climate & Energy Tech newsletter

Derichebourg finalizes the acquisition of the German company Scholz Recycling for 480 million euros, financed entirely from cash and existing credit lines. Scholz employs 3,500 people, operates over 180 sites worldwide, and generated 1.6 billion euros in revenue last year. The pro forma revenue of the group jumps by 50% to approximately 5 billion.

The operation deserves a note on its history: Scholz was owned by the Chinese group Chiho Environmental Group, which attempted to block the sale through a lawsuit in Hong Kong challenging the validity of the independent agents who led the divestment. Derichebourg maintained its course after legal analysis.

What Derichebourg is buying is a network for collecting scrap iron and non-ferrous metals in Germany and Europe—in other words, a position in the supply chain for recycled steel, whose structural demand will grow as the European industry decarbonizes its production; scrap metal is to steel decarbonization what lithium is to batteries—a critical raw material that no one talks about.

Haudecoeur + Natur Food + Heliadis: ethnic grocery consolidates on a European scale

Source: lsa-conso.fr →

Haudecoeur simultaneously acquires the German company Natur Food and the Belgian company Heliadis for a total valuation of 350 million euros, accelerating its expansion in ethnic grocery distribution in Europe.

The consolidation of specialized food distribution follows a classic logic of network densification: ethnic grocery stores have long been a fragmented, local market with low formalization. The emergence of a player that structures the logistics chain on a European scale creates purchasing economies and the ability to reference brands continent-wide—an advantage difficult for a local operator to replicate.

Orkla + The European Candy Group: Nordic confectionery establishes itself in continental Europe

Source: just-food.com → · Sector Knowledge & Media — 📬 subscribe to the Knowledge & Media newsletter

Orkla acquires The European Candy Group (also known as Continental Candy Industries) from funds managed by Bencis Capital Partners and management shareholders, for 207 million euros. The stated objective: to give the confectionery brand Bubs the production capacity needed for its expansion outside the Nordic markets.

A classic industrial acquisition for capacity growth, with a well-articulated brand-as-production-tool logic.

Engine + Options Travel: Permira builds a corporate travel booking tool

Engine, a business travel startup backed by Permira, acquires Options Travel, an American agency with approximately 408 million dollars in annual booking volume, for 129 million euros.

Engine had built its reputation by serving business travelers who book independently, without going through a dedicated travel agency. By acquiring Options Travel—precisely this type of agency—Engine is changing its model: it is no longer just a direct booking tool, it is becoming a complete travel management operator, capable of negotiating rates and managing corporate travel policies.

The bet is to build an AI-native tool that makes established platforms like Concur or Navan obsolete—but to achieve this, Engine needs the contractual data and supplier relationships that a traditional agency holds; Options Travel is less an acquisition of revenue than an acquisition of data and access licenses.

Verisure: first dividend after a turnaround in results

Source: escudodigital.com → · Sector Media & Entertainment — 📬 subscribe to the Media & Entertainment newsletter

Verisure reports a net profit of 65.7 million euros in the first half of 2026, compared to a net loss of 86.8 million a year earlier, and announces its first dividend. Revenue reached 2.043 billion euros (+10.6%), with adjusted EBITDA up 11.3% to 939.9 million.

The return to net profitability after years of losses—driven by heavy acquisition debt—and the announcement of a first dividend signal financial normalization. For a group whose capital is largely held by PE funds, this dividend is also a signal sent to debt markets and potential future acquirers.

McCabes Pharmacy: consolidation of Irish pharmacies

Source: irishtimes.com → · Sector HealthTech & Digital Health — 📬 subscribe to the HealthTech & Digital Health newsletter

McCabes Pharmacy, a network of 31 pharmacies in Ireland, is sold for 58 million euros to the German group that owns the Lloyds Pharmacy chain, merging the two brands in the Irish market.

Consolidation of retail pharmaceutical distribution in an island market with low critical mass: the logic is network densification to amortize central costs (referencing, logistics, IT systems) over a broader base of points of sale.

Béaba Suavinex Group: handover between PE funds

Source: investorsinhealthcare.com → · Sector HealthTech & Digital Health — 📬 subscribe to the HealthTech & Digital Health newsletter

Bluegem Capital Partners and Portobello Capital partner to support the next phase of development of the Béaba Suavinex Group, a childcare specialist, for 20 million euros.

A PE co-investment operation on a well-established Franco-Spanish childcare brand, with no further details on the control structure or the use of funds.

Aeffe (Moschino): Oxy Capital attempts to save a struggling fashion group

Source: wwd.com → · Sector Knowledge & Media — 📬 subscribe to the Knowledge & Media newsletter

Oxy Capital submits a firm offer to acquire Aeffe, parent company of Moschino, Alberta Ferretti, and Pollini, listed in Milan, for 12 million euros—an amount that alone speaks to the group's deteriorated state.

Aeffe is undergoing restructuring as part of an amicable settlement with its creditors. Oxy Capital would operate with a Chinese industrial partner and Invitalia, the development agency of the Italian Ministry of Economy, which explains the approval of unions concerned with preserving artisanal jobs.

At 12 million, Oxy Capital is not buying brands—it's buying an option on their revival, with a Chinese partner likely providing access to the Asian market and Invitalia politically securing the operation; the real price will be that of the industrial plan, not the entry ticket.

BMW withdraws from Mondial de l'Auto, Chery confirms its presence

Source: x.com →

BMW announces its withdrawal from the Paris Mondial de l'Auto, citing poor financial results, while the Chinese group Chery, through its subsidiary Omoda & Jaecoo, confirms its participation.

Not a financial transaction per se, but a clear sectoral signal: when a German premium manufacturer scales back its marketing spending at European auto shows while a Chinese challenger invests there, it indicates a redistribution of commercial ambitions in the European market. Established manufacturers rationalize; entrants seek visibility.

European Unicorns H1 2026: 53 new entries, 40% in deep tech

Source: x.com →

According to Dealroom, Europe added 53 new unicorns and exits valued at over a billion in 2026, across 15 countries, with the UK leading (23 additions). 40% of the total falls under deep tech.

The most instructive figure is this 40%: in previous cycles, European unicorns were predominantly B2C platforms or fintechs. The shift towards deep tech—defense, biotech, materials, energy—reflects both the maturity of European academic ecosystems and the growing sovereign demand in these sectors.

🚀 Fundraisings today · 19

In focus — the deals we decoded

SquareMind (Swan): 17 million to robot-map skin

Source: lavoixdefrance.fr →

SquareMind raises 17 million euros (18 million dollars) for Swan, its skin imaging robot capable of mapping the entire skin surface in minutes.

The value proposition is precise: a dermatologist cannot monitor all of a patient's moles at every consultation. Swan automates this exhaustive mapping, creating a documented and comparable baseline over time—a melanoma early detection tool that integrates into the care pathway without replacing the doctor.

The value is not in the unique image, it is in the temporal comparison: it is the longitudinal patient database that constitutes the defensible asset, and which makes the system increasingly precise as it accumulates data—a learning dynamic that creates a progressive barrier to entry.

Huelco (Orioma): 2 million for a wireless, battery-free IoT sensor

Source: lavoixdefrance.fr →

Huelco raises 2 million euros to deploy Orioma, its IoT sensor that operates without wires and without batteries, and aims for European expansion.

An industrial sensor that requires neither power nor wired connectivity solves the main obstacle to the massive deployment of IoT in harsh industrial environments: battery maintenance and cabling. The target market—factories, infrastructure, logistics—is vast, but the modest fundraising suggests a commercial validation phase still underway.

Apolownia: 1 million for coastal restoration and blue carbon

Source: eu-startups.com →

Apolownia (Paris) raises 1 million euros to develop coastal restoration and blue carbon projects—the sequestration of CO₂ by marine ecosystems (seagrass beds, mangroves, tidal flats).

Blue carbon is a still underdeveloped carbon asset class, with biodiversity co-benefits recognized by international standards. At this funding stage, Apolownia is in its seed phase: the fundraising finances the structuring of initial projects and the validation of measurement methodologies.

European university spinouts H1 2026: 4.9 billion raised

According to Sifted, European universities produced 222 spinouts that collectively raised 4.9 billion euros in the first half of 2026.

An aggregated figure that confirms the growing power of university technology transfer in Europe—long behind the United States and the United Kingdom. The missing data, as always, is the fine sectoral and geographical breakdown: 4.9 billion concentrated on ten British biotech spinouts does not have the same meaning as the same sum distributed across 200 continental deeptech projects.

Atomic One: 5.6 million for an AI e-commerce platform

Source: startuprise.co.uk → · Sector Retail & E-commerce Tech — 📬 subscribe to the Retail & E-commerce Tech newsletter

Atomic One (Málaga) raises 5.6 million euros in two tranches to develop its AI platform dedicated to e-commerce brands, with the support of Arcano Partners (via its Impacto Andalucía fund, co-financed by the EIB and ERDF) and international private investors.

The platform aggregates operational data from an online store, makes optimization decisions, and executes them autonomously—pricing, inventory, campaigns. The positioning is that of an operational co-pilot, not an analytical tool. The participation of regional public funds (EIB, ERDF) in a venture round illustrates how European cohesion mechanisms now co-finance tech startups in emerging ecosystems like Andalusia.
