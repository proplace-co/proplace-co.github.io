    Palo Alto Networks Acquires Console for $500 Million

    Source: techcrunch.com → · Sector Horizontal & Productivity SaaS — 📬 subscribe to the Horizontal & Productivity SaaS newsletter

    Palo Alto Networks has acquired Console, an IT service automation startup backed by Thrive, for $500 million (approximately €460 million).

    The acquisition positions Palo Alto in AI-powered IT operations automation — a segment adjacent to cybersecurity where the lines between "securing a system" and "operating a system" are blurring.   • Doctrine

    LexisNexis rachète Doctrine pour intégrer la chaîne de l'IA juridique

    M&AFranceExited (Acquired / Buyout)

    LexisNexis Acquires Doctrine: When the Tool Becomes the Entire Chain

    LexisNexis finalizes the acquisition of Doctrine, a French legal intelligence platform, for an undisclosed amount. The new entity represents 800 employees and approximately €200 million in revenue in France — a notable fact: the two companies had faced each other in court just over a year before one bought the other.

    The immediate interpretation is that of a classic consolidation: a leading publisher absorbs a digital challenger to avoid being left behind. But the move is more precise than that. Doctrine is not simply a database of case law — it is a layer of analysis and research that fits between raw text and a lawyer's reasoning. By integrating it, LexisNexis is not buying a competitor: it is closing the loop. It now controls the raw material (texts), the search engine, and the assisted interpretation layer. In a sector where legal AI will redistribute fees between firms that master their tools and those that suffer from them, owning the entire information processing chain becomes a toll booth position — and the fact that the two parties first fought in court before merging says something about the real value of what Doctrine had built.

    For a French law firm or legal department: the consolidation of this market will accelerate, and independent tools will become scarcer. The question of who owns your legal knowledge infrastructure deserves to be asked now.

    M&AFranceSeries C

    Space Cargo Takes a Stake in Novespace

    Source: frenchweb.fr →

    Space Cargo makes a strategic investment in Novespace, a subsidiary of CNES led by Thomas Pesquet, for an undisclosed amount. Space Cargo CEO, Nicolas Gaume, joins the board of directors. Novespace's parabolic flights — which offer microgravity sequences of approximately 22 seconds — serve as a bridge between terrestrial experiments and the orbital factories that Space Cargo aims to develop.

    Vertical integration is logical: if one wants to produce in space, one must first test under accessible microgravity conditions. Novespace is the most accessible testing infrastructure in Europe for this type of experimentation. Space Cargo is not buying a supplier — it is securing privileged access to a rare infrastructure, while gaining institutional legitimacy through CNES.

  • Crusoe

    Crusoe lève 3 milliards de dollars pour son infrastructure de données cloud

    FundraisingUnited States2,8 Md€Series E+

    Crusoe and Fluidstack: AI Infrastructure Raises Billions, Margins Remain the Question

    Source: news.crunchbase.com → · Sector Data & Analytics — 📬 subscribe to the Data & Analytics newsletter

    Crusoe, a cloud and data center provider, has closed $3 billion in funding. Fluidstack, a distributed GPU infrastructure platform, has raised $1.5 billion. Together, the two operations represent the bulk of a weekly total of €2.8 billion for AI infrastructure.

    Crusoe was built on an original idea — using flared natural gas at oil sites to power data centers, transforming an energy waste into a computing resource. At this scale of funding, the originality of the origin is no longer enough: it requires contracts, margins, and the ability to compete with cloud operators whose balance sheets are incomparable. Fluidstack aggregates distributed GPU capacity — a model that bets on market fragmentation rather than concentration.

    The real test for these two players is not the fundraising, it's the operating margin in twelve months: building AI infrastructure is capital-intensive, and customers who pay for compute fiercely negotiate their prices as soon as supply exceeds demand. Oracle charges for its AI cloud services at margins much lower than its historical margins — Crusoe and Fluidstack will start even lower.

LumaApps, a French digital workplace platform, announces that its founder Sébastien Ricard will hand over operational management on December 1 to Sébastien Cano, former president of Gemalto North America and cybersecurity executive at Thales. The new CEO will lead from Austin a group transformed since Bridgepoint's entry into its capital in 2024, after the merger with Beekeeper.

This is not a CEO change — it's a shift in the center of gravity. LumaApps built its credibility in France and Europe; it chooses to monetize it in the United States, where digital workplace adoption budgets are deepest. Cano's profile — an executive experienced in infrastructure sales cycles to large American accounts — confirms that the ambition is not organic growth but aggressive commercial penetration of a market where decisions are made in Austin, New York, and San Francisco. Bridgepoint, a European fund, is financing Americanization: this is the classic move of a European scale-up that has exhausted its local market and must seek valuation where multiples are more generous.

The Fragility of ARR in the Age of AI

A study published this week documents that startups' annual recurring revenue is structurally less stable than before: corporate buying cycles have been broken by AI, customers re-evaluate their subscriptions more frequently, and multi-year contracts are becoming scarcer. Not an operation per se, but a fundamental signal for any acquirer or investor who values a target based on its ARR: the recurrence premium that the market mechanically granted to subscription figures now deserves to be verified contract by contract.
