    Source: akm.ru →

    Lufthansa has finalized the acquisition of 41% of ITA Airways for €325 million, with the remaining stake held by the Italian Ministry of Economy, and an option to increase its capital share later. Italy is the fourth-largest European aviation market, structurally under-consolidated on the national supply side since Alitalia's bankruptcy. For French companies operating Rome-Paris or Milan-Paris routes, ITA's rise within the Lufthansa network means increased competition on business routes within eighteen months.

PureHealth Holding (United Arab Emirates) has finalized the acquisition of 60% of the Greek hospital group Hellenic Healthcare (HHG) from CVC Capital Partners for €800 million, valuing the entire group at €1.3 billion. CVC retains 35%, the founder 5%.

Source: sapians.com → · Sector FinTech — 📬 subscribe to the FinTech newsletter

Sapians publishes an interview with Paul Bougnoux (Largillière Finance) on the OBO (Owner Buy-Out) mechanism, illustrated by transactions between €20 million and €300 million.

OBO allows a leader to monetize part of their company's value without losing control — by selling to a holding company they co-own with a fund, financed by debt. In a context where valuation multiples have shrunk since 2023 and interest rates have quadrupled since 2022, OBO is often more attractive than a total sale at a degraded price: the leader secures wealth today while retaining exposure to the future value of their company. A tool to know, particularly for leaders of tech companies whose valuation remains strong despite the economic climate.

    FedEx et Advent finalisent rachat InPost pour 7,8 Md€

    FundraisingPoland7,8 Md€Exited (Acquired / Buyout)

    FedEx and Advent acquire InPost: when last-mile logistics are worth more than airplanes

    Source: reuters.com →

    FedEx and Advent International, leading a consortium, have crossed the 89% acceptance threshold in their takeover bid for InPost, the Polish automated parcel locker operator, for €7.8 billion. Trading on Euronext Amsterdam will be delisted upon completion of the transaction.

    The immediate takeaway: FedEx is buying European coverage; InPost brings it nine countries and tens of millions of lockers, infrastructure that the American giant would have taken a decade to build alone.

    But what is at stake here is deeper. InPost is not a delivery company — it's a network of fixed points where consumers pick up parcels, which reverses the costly logic of "last mile" (one address, one slot, one failed attempt). The locker is a rent-generating infrastructure: once installed in a building lobby or in front of a supermarket, it generates traffic with no marginal delivery cost. FedEx, whose model relies on trucks and drivers, is actually acquiring an asset that consumes less human labor per parcel processed than any other form of delivery. For a French or European logistics operator, the message is clear: value in this sector is migrating to whoever owns the fixed physical network, not whoever manages fleets.

    • FOXPOST SRO. — Their automated locker network in Hungary would extend their presence in Eastern Europe, capitalizing on InPost's expertise.
