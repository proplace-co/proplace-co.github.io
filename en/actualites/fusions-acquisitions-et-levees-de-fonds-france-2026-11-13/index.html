The day is dominated by two overlapping yet distinct dynamics: on one hand, European venture capital is massively shifting towards autonomous defense and physical robotics — with unusually large tickets for the Old Continent; on the other, industrial and services consolidation is methodically advancing, acquisition after acquisition, in technical installations, logistics, and business services. AI spans both registers, sometimes as a disruptive technology in military drones and humanoid robots, sometimes as a tool for measuring ROI for companies that have already spent without counting.

  • PATRIZIA SE

    PATRIZIA SE: Partial offer with 30% premium for gradual delisting

    M&AGermanyPublic

    PATRIZIA SE: Partial offer with 30% premium for gradual delisting

    Source: eqs-news.com →

    First Capital Partner GmbH, based in Gräfelfing (Germany), launches a voluntary partial public tender offer for shares of PATRIZIA SE, an Augsburg-listed real estate asset manager, at €8.20 per share — an approximate 30.2% premium over the previous day's closing price. The objective is to increase the total stake to 73% of the capital (before cancellation of treasury shares). The offer has no minimum acceptance threshold. Concurrently, PATRIZIA is requesting its transfer to the Scale segment of the Frankfurt Stock Exchange (downlisting), and First Capital Partner excludes any domination or profit transfer agreement for three years.

    The combination of a high premium, a partial offer without a threshold, and a simultaneously announced downlisting outlines a smooth delisting: shareholders who want to exit do so at a good price, while those who remain accept reduced liquidity but a stable reference shareholder. For PATRIZIA, the operation secures a capital anchor without the constraints of a company listed on the main market.

    🔮 What First Capital Partner could buy next

    • Cinerius Financial Partners — First Capital Partner could seek to consolidate its position in asset management, with this target strengthening its network of independent managers after a delisting.
    • ATHELIOS Vermögensatelier — First Capital Partner would consolidate its post-delisting positioning by integrating a model for acquiring managers and client portfolios, offering succession solutions.

    Agile Robots raises €450 million: European physical robotics finds its champion

    Source: maddyness.com →

    Agile Robots, a Munich startup founded in 2018 by Zhaopeng Chen, develops humanoid robots and automated arms equipped with artificial intelligence designed to modernize industrial factories — its clients cover the automotive, consumer electronics, healthcare, and services sectors. The company raises €450M, bringing its valuation to approximately €7.6 billion. Investors in this round include SoftBank, Nvidia, Hitachi, and T.Capital (Deutsche Telekom's investment vehicle).

    Agile Robots now claims the top valuation in robotics and AI in Europe, ahead of its national competitor Neura Robotics (valued at approximately €6.3 billion in June). The funds will finance expansion into the United States, increased production capacity in Germany, and entry into new markets. German automakers are cited as early adopters of humanoid solutions as early as 2027.

    The joint presence of Nvidia and SoftBank deserves a precise reading. Nvidia invests in robot manufacturers because these robots run on its GPUs — each deployed humanoid is a unit of compute sold. SoftBank, for its part, has made physical robotics one of its structuring axes since the acquisition of Boston Dynamics. The two investors are not just buying a stake: they are financing an outlet for their own infrastructure. Agile Robots receives capital and, with it, privileged access to the most strategic technological supply chain of the moment.

    For a French industrialist looking to automate, this fundraising announces a credible humanoid offering in Europe within eighteen months — with a German anchor and local production capacity.

  • SE3

    SE3 raises $30 million: spatial intelligence for military drones emerges from the shadows

    FundraisingGermanySeries A

    SE3 raises $30 million: spatial intelligence for military drones emerges from the shadows

    SE3, a Munich startup founded by Lukas Köstler (formerly of Tesla's Autopilot team and Nvidia Research), develops AI software enabling military drones and unmanned systems to understand the three-dimensional space around them. Its technology fuses video and sensor feeds from multiple drones and ground vehicles into a unified 3D map of the battlefield, with a semantic layer allowing soldiers to query the environment in natural language and issue commands in the same way. The company has contracts with the Bundeswehr and is already deploying its technology in Ukraine.

    SE3 emerged from its confidential phase less than five months ago with €5.5 million in seed funding — this Series A therefore occurs on an extremely compressed timeline, a sign of operational demand that cannot wait.

    The technical architecture is notable: SE3 does not use large general-purpose language models to reason about physics or terrain. It relies on classic computer vision algorithms to distill sensor data into a structured database, which the language model can then query with conventional tools — trajectory calculation, obstacle detection, mission planning. The architectural choice reflects a military engineering discipline: reliability takes precedence over flexibility, and a deterministic flight planning algorithm is better than a probabilistic model in a cluttered environment.
