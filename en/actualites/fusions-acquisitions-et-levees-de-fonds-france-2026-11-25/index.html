  • Fibank

    Eurobank acquires Fibank: Bulgaria's leading bank built from Athens

    M&ABulgaria1 Md€Public

    Eurobank acquires Fibank: Bulgaria's leading bank built from Athens

    Eurobank, a listed Greek banking group, is set to acquire Fibank, one of Bulgaria's main banks, for approximately €1 billion. The transaction positions Eurobank as the leading bank in Bulgaria with a market share of about one-fifth.

    The obvious interpretation: a Greek bank strengthening its presence in the Balkans, a natural market for its regional expansion.

    What is less often said: Bulgaria is on its way to joining the Eurozone, which mechanically transforms the value of local banking assets — a Bulgarian bank operating in euros tomorrow is structurally worth more than a bank operating in lev. Eurobank is not just acquiring market share; it is positioning itself before the revaluation that monetary integration will produce. For a French acquirer or investor looking at Central and Eastern Europe, the Eurozone accession calendar remains one of the few predictable value catalysts in the banking sector.

    🔮 What Eurobank could buy next

    • tbi bank — Eurobank could seek to extend its leadership in Bulgaria via a mobile-first challenger bank, capitalizing on the upcoming Eurozone integration.
    • DSK Bank — Eurobank would consolidate its leading position after the acquisition of Fibank thanks to their strong retail presence and extensive network in Bulgaria.
  • Baker Ing

    Coface absorbs Baker Ing: complex debt collection enters the global network

    M&AUnited KingdomExited (Acquired / Buyout)

    Coface absorbs Baker Ing: complex debt collection enters the global network

    Source: ideal-investisseur.fr →

    Coface, a French credit insurer active in credit insurance, debt collection, factoring, and business information services, is acquiring 100% of Baker Ing, a London-based company founded in 2015, specializing in the management and collection of complex, high-value B2B debts internationally. The amount was not disclosed.

    Baker Ing is not a generalist debt collection firm: its positioning is explicitly on difficult debts, those that standard processes cannot handle. It has built a client portfolio including several multinationals and an experienced credit management team.

    For Coface, the operation strengthens an existing service line in a specific geographical market — the UK — rather than opening up a new business. The logic is symmetrical: Baker Ing gains access to Coface's international network to develop its business outside the British market.

    Strategic interpretation: in a context of rising corporate insolvencies in Europe, complex debt collection is one of the few businesses whose demand mechanically grows with the deteriorating cycle. Coface is consolidating its capacity to handle the end of the chain — the debt that credit insurance could not prevent — at a time when this end of the chain is becoming more congested.

    🔮 What Coface could buy next

    • Hoopiz Credit Management — Coface could seek to further digitize and automate its complex debt collection processes, capitalizing on the acquisition of Baker Ing.
    • GEVO — Coface would extend its European debt collection coverage with their Sekundi network, capitalizing on the integration of Baker Ing for complex receivables.
  • Countingup

    Taxfix acquires Countingup: from tax declaration to financial platform for freelancers

    M&AUnited KingdomSeries B

    Taxfix acquires Countingup: from tax declaration to financial platform for freelancers

    Source: startbase.de → · Sector FinTech — 📬 subscribe to the FinTech newsletter

    Taxfix, a Berlin-based fintech offering digital tax declarations for individuals, is acquiring Countingup, a London-based fintech founded in 2017 that combines business accounts, accounting, invoicing, and tax functions for freelancers and small businesses. The price was not disclosed.

    Countingup will continue to operate under its own brand; its 26 employees will join the Taxfix group, which has over 400 people across several European locations. Countingup's banking partnership with Griffin, a licensed UK institution, will be maintained.

    The obvious interpretation: Taxfix is diversifying its offering and reducing its dependence on the annual tax cycle by adding recurring banking services.

    What the operation reveals more precisely: Taxfix is building a comprehensive financial platform for the European freelancer — a segment whose needs (pro account, accounting, declaration) are currently served by three or four distinct applications, and whose aggregation on a single interface creates a daily customer relationship where tax declaration only creates one per year. Countingup's real asset is not its transaction volume: it's the daily usage habit of 100,000 UK micro-businesses. For a French or European player targeting freelancers, the question posed by this deal is one of timing: the consolidation of this segment is accelerating.

    🔮 What Taxfix could buy next

    • Accountable — Taxfix could seek to expand its financial platform for freelancers in Europe, strengthening its presence and accounting service offerings.
    • Indy — Taxfix would extend its geographical reach and strengthen its integrated service offering post-Countingup thanks to their all-in-one platform for freelancers in France.

  • Mythos AI

    Saab acquires Mythos AI: maritime autonomy leaves the lab

    M&AUnited StatesExited (Acquired / Buyout)

    Saab acquires Mythos AI: maritime autonomy leaves the lab

    Source: svenskverkstad.se →

    Saab, a Swedish defense and technology group, is finalizing the acquisition of Mythos AI, a 15-person American company specializing in autonomous navigation and intelligent piloting software for ships. The price was not disclosed.

    Mythos AI develops software for automatic piloting, autonomous navigation, and automated seabed inspections. Its technology has been tested over 30,000 autonomous nautical miles in busy waters, with nearly 40,000 avoidance maneuvers of other vessels. Saab and Mythos AI already had a technical partnership: in 2025, the two companies demonstrated pilot assistance and remote operation functions on Saab's Enforcer development platform in San Diego Bay.

    The integration aims to combine Mythos AI's autonomy software with Saab's marine platforms, sensors, and system integration capabilities, for both civil and military applications.

    What the operation illustrates: the transition from demonstration to industrialization in autonomous defense does not happen through additional fundraising — it happens through absorption into a group that already has the customers, certifications, and distribution channels. Mythos AI had solved the technical problem (30,000 miles of real data is a serious entry barrier); Saab solves the market entry problem. For deeptech defense startup founders, this deal illustrates an increasingly common trajectory: exit via the industrial partner, not via IPO.

    🔮 What Saab could buy next

    • AI UNIVERSAL — Saab could seek to extend its maritime autonomy capabilities to commercial operations and decarbonization, complementing the acquisition of Mythos AI.
    • Sthenos AI — Saab would complement its acquisition of Mythos AI with their expertise in C2 and sovereign AI for naval defense, strengthening its systems.

  • Withings

    Withings regains independence: the founder's return as a strategic signal

    M&AFranceExited (Acquired / Buyout)

    Withings regains independence: the founder's return as a strategic signal

    Source: ticpharma.com →

    Withings, a French company specializing in connected consumer health devices (scales, blood pressure monitors, health watches) and medical monitoring solutions, is returning to the control of its co-founder Eric Carreel and his family, who collectively hold 90% of the capital. The remainder is shared between BNP Paribas Développement, 2050, Adelie, and ACE & Company (7%) and employees and former employees (3%). Withings announces revenue growth of over 20% in 2026 and states it is profitable, without disclosing amounts. The B2C connected devices business accounts for 81% of sales.

    The history of Withings is that of a company founded in 2008, acquired by Nokia in 2016, resold to Eric Carreel in 2018, then refinanced by several rounds bringing in Bpifrance, Eurazeo, Gilde Healthcare, and others. This repurchase of the majority by the Carreel family settles these participations.

    What this deal says, beyond Withings: the trajectory — founder → large group → founder repurchase → institutional investors → family repurchase — is rare and says something about the project's maturity. A profitable company, growing at double-digit rates, whose founder buys back shares from its investors, no longer needs external capital to grow: it generates the necessary resources. It's a clean exit for the funds, a regaining of sovereignty for the Carreel family, and a signal that Withings has solved its economic equation. For investors in digital health, it's also a reminder that connected consumer health devices remain a difficult market — and that those who master it rarely do so by remaining within a large group.

    Cosmo N.V. takes 23% of Scialytics: surgical AI emerges from the university

    Source: tradingview.com →

    Scialytics, a Strasbourg-based startup founded in September 2025 as a spin-off from IHU Strasbourg and the CAMMA research team at the University of Strasbourg, develops AI software that analyzes video images of laparoscopic and robotic surgeries to assist surgical teams in real-time and facilitate post-operative case reviews. Its primary application targets laparoscopic cholecystectomy (gallbladder removal, performed between 750,000 and one million times per year in the United States) to help surgeons systematically verify critical safety steps and prevent bile duct injuries. The company has not yet obtained clinical certification.

    Cosmo N.V., a listed Irish group specializing in medical technologies, led Scialytics' seed funding round and acquired a minority stake of 23%. Financial terms were not disclosed. For Cosmo, this investment is part of its "Vision 2030" strategy to extend its real-time medical AI footprint into the operating room.

    Scialytics will use the funds to expand its engineering, clinical, and regulatory teams, build the necessary clinical evidence, and obtain European and American certifications.

    Strategic interpretation: the choice of cholecystectomy as the initial application is not insignificant — it is one of the most frequent surgeries, with a well-documented, costly, and legally sensitive risk of complication (bile duct injury). Software that measurably reduces this risk has a clear selling point to hospitals and insurers. Cosmo's early-stage investment positions the group as a potential industrial partner for commercialization — not just as a financial investor.

    Bcomp raises €20M from EIB to industrialize flax fiber in Romania

    Source: eu-startups.com →

    Bcomp, a Swiss startup based in Fribourg founded in 2011 by Christian Fischer and Julien Rion, develops high-performance composite materials based on flax fibers as a substitute for carbon fiber, glass, and conventional plastics. For industrial investors and corporates sourcing materials, Bcomp is one to watch: if the Timișoara factory delivers its volumes, high-performance flax fiber ceases to be a niche material.

Pliant raises over €200M in debt: financing credit lines before granting them

Source: ffnews.com → · Sector FinTech — 📬 subscribe to the FinTech newsletter

Pliant, an international fintech specializing in modular B2B payment solutions — corporate credit cards, expense management, customizable payment infrastructure for businesses — secures over €200 million in debt financing capacity from Ascory Bank AG and Viola Credit. The operation comprises three distinct components: a securitization structure backed by receivables from its European corporate card portfolio, a Visa pre-financing solution to manage card settlement liquidity, and an extension of the existing debt facility with Viola Credit. This debt raising comes shortly after an $80 million Series C equity round.

The raison d'être of this structure is mechanical: a company that issues corporate credit cards must finance its clients' credit balances before collecting them. The more it grows, the more this need for intermediate liquidity grows. Debt backed by receivables is the standard instrument to finance this position — it is not development debt, it is the operational fuel of the model.

What this operation says about the B2B payments market in Europe: Pliant is building a corporate payment infrastructure that requires, to grow, banking partners capable of financing the underlying credit cycle. The presence of Ascory Bank AG as a partner for over six years suggests a co-construction relationship rather than a simple financing relationship. For B2B payments fintechs looking to scale in Europe, access to this type of structured facility is often the real bottleneck — not the technology.
