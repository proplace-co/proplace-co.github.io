Analysis of M&A and Fundraising Transactions as of September 1, 2026 SUBTITLE=== Defense, energy, digital sovereignty, and sector consolidation: a busy back-to-school period where French and European capital repositions its lines — the complete breakdown for decision-makers and investors. ===BODY=== The back-to-school period opens with a broad spectrum: frigates signed in Stockholm, a supercomputer assembled in Angers for Finland, autonomous drones changing hands, and silent consolidation in insurance, perfumery, oenology, and consulting. At the European level, energy — storage, renewables, hydrogen — accounts for a significant portion of the activity. Meanwhile, in the United States, middle-market insurance is restructuring around a mega-deal. No single logic governs this day, but a constant runs through French deals: building sovereign entities, piece by piece, in markets where size is starting to be a decisive factor. # 🤝 M&A Transactions ## Naval Group: four frigates and an industrial alliance worth €4.3 billion **Naval Group** signs a **€4.3 billion** contract in Stockholm for the delivery of four defense and intervention frigates to **Sweden**, with the first unit expected in 2030. The ceremony, presided over by Macron and the Swedish Prime Minister, is accompanied by a framework agreement for industrial and operational cooperation. The immediate takeaway: a great export contract for the French defense industry, in a context of NATO expansion and European rearmament post-2022. *What deserves attention is the structured reciprocity: France had chosen Saab's GlobalEye for its aerial surveillance in December, and Sweden chose French frigates in May. This is not just a tender won — it's an industrial exchange between states, a form of sovereign barter where each order anchors a mutually agreed dependence. For Naval Group and the French BITD, this means a secure order book for a decade, but also a maintenance, training, and MRO relationship that links the two navies for thirty years. The real product sold is not the frigate: it's interoperability.* ## Bull wins its largest contract: an AI supercomputer for Northern Europe **Bull** — the former subsidiary of **Atos**, acquired by the French state at the end of March — has been selected by **EuroHPC** and the **Lumi-AI Factory** consortium (Finland, Czech Republic, Denmark, Estonia, Norway, Poland) to design and assemble a **€387.8 million** AI supercomputer, deliverable in the second half of 2027 from the Angers factory. This is the largest contract in Bull's history. The machine is designed for training and inference of large-scale AI models, as well as for scientific simulations. *The timing is significant: Bull has just emerged from a state rescue procedure, and immediately wins the most important contract in its existence. This is not an industrial coincidence — it demonstrates that nationalization was precisely aimed at keeping alive an actor capable of responding to this type of European tender. EuroHPC is the instrument through which Europe is trying not to outsource its computing infrastructure to American or Asian players alone. Bull is now one of its key builders. For an investor or industrial partner, the question is not "Is Bull viable?" but "Will the French state accept that it is not?" — the answer is now clear.* ## Synov merges ALTEC-ATLS and ADETEC to create A3Tech **Synov** (approximately **€160 million** in revenue, over 1,000 employees), an Auvergne-based electronic systems group located near Le Puy-en-Velay, is merging two of its subsidiaries — **ALTEC-ATLS** and **ADETEC** — to create **A3Tech**, a new entity dedicated to security, transmission, and connected alerts. The operation finalizes a rapprochement initiated in October 2025 with the acquisition of ALTEC-ATLS. ALTEC-ATLS brings thirty years of French manufacturing of security equipment (transmitters, GSM/IP interfaces, sirens); ADETEC brings industrial electronics, IoT, and hardware/software development. *The merger aims to control a complete technological chain — from design to secure transmission — to address integrators and industrialists as a single entity. A classic internal rationalization operation, but one that reveals an ambition to position itself in connected building security, a rapidly growing market in the wake of regulatory obligations.* ## Foncière Volta acquires the Marco Polo building in Clichy for €17.5 million **Foncière Volta**, alongside the **Distrimo** group (50/50), acquires the "Marco Polo" office building in Clichy — approximately 8,100 m² over seven levels — for **€17.5 million** excluding duties, or approximately **€2,200/m²**, financed by a real estate leaseback from **Arkea Banque Entreprises & Institutionnels**. *At €2,200/m² for an asset delivered in 2005, served by line 14 (Saint-Lazare in a few minutes), line 13, and the RER C, in a transforming area at the gates of Paris, the price speaks for itself: first-ring office space remains under valuation pressure, and those with liquidity and patience are now buying what the market is neglecting. For Foncière Volta, the operation is consistent with an opportunistic real estate strategy — entering quality assets at depressed prices and waiting for the normalization of the office market to do its work.* ## Decathlon takes 50% of Sport 2000 France: the distributor becomes a network architect **Decathlon** signs a letter of intent to acquire **50%** of **Sport 2000 France**'s central purchasing body from **Céraclès Coopérative**, its cooperative shareholder. The amount is undisclosed, with finalization expected in mid-2027, subject to approval by the Competition Authority. The central body (€98 million in revenue, €215,000 in net profit in 2025) pools purchasing, logistics, and marketing for franchised stores, without owning them. The obvious takeaway: Decathlon is consolidating the French sports retail market, already shaken by Intersport's acquisition of Go Sport stores in 2023. *But what is at stake is more subtle. Decathlon is not buying stores — it is entering the network head of a network of independent franchisees. By controlling 50% of the central body, it gains access to the purchasing flows, offer construction, and logistics of several hundred points of sale that will remain legally autonomous. This is a form of control without ownership: Decathlon captures influence over the network without bearing the operational risk store by store. For Sport 2000 franchisees, the equation is ambivalent — they gain the purchasing power of the French leader, but their central body, soon to be co-managed by their main competitor, will gradually orient their assortment. The line between partner and prescriber will quickly blur.* ## Jamespot acquires Alinto: messaging, the last link in the sovereign suite **Jamespot** acquires **Alinto**, a French specialist in professional messaging (over 1,000 corporate clients, nearly one million users, over 100 million messages processed daily). The amount is undisclosed. This is Jamespot's ninth acquisition since 2014. With Alinto (messaging), Jamespot.pro (collaboration), and SafeBrain (AI), the Lyon-based group now has a complete digital suite under one sovereign roof — common identities, unified security rules, single point of contact. *The logic is clear and well-executed: against Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace, digital sovereignty cannot be partial. A collaborative platform without integrated messaging remains a complement, never a substitute. By adding Alinto — whose open-source SOGo technology is already used by public and sensitive actors — Jamespot crosses the threshold that transforms a niche offering into a credible alternative for organizations that do not want to depend on American clouds. The question that remains open: size. 70 employees after nine acquisitions is still modest to compete with global suites. The ambition is there; the scale, not yet.* ## Vivelys acquires Lamouroux: precision oenology integrates thermoregulation **Vivelys** (precision oenology specialist, 50 employees, Villeneuve-lès-Maguelone), a subsidiary of the **Oeno** group, is set to acquire all the capital of **Lamouroux** (Gironde-based SME, 40 employees, **€10 million** in revenue in 2025), a specialist in automated thermal control systems for winemaking since 1988. Finalization is expected in the coming months. *Vertical consolidation operation in the wine sector: Vivelys now controls precision analysis and thermal regulation, two complementary links in winemaking management. In a sector where climate change makes temperature control during fermentation increasingly critical, integrating this expertise internally rather than outsourcing it is a solid industrial decision.* ## Drone Volt acquires Azur Drones' assets: a proven system taken over in court **Drone Volt**, through its subsidiary **InAirTech**, has been designated by the Bordeaux Commercial Court as the acquirer of all industrial and operational assets of **Azur Drones** (Mérignac). The main asset is the **Skeyetech** system — an autonomous "Drone in a box" surveillance drone, with over 62,000 BVLOS flights recorded, deployed in 11 countries, compatible with major video management systems on the market, ANSSI certified for cybersecurity. *Azur Drones had developed mature and operationally proven technology but did not survive financially. Drone Volt is acquiring an asset at a judicial sale price that would have cost years and tens of millions to rebuild. This is the classic mechanism of distressed asset acquisition: industrial value survives financial failure, and whoever has the cash and strategic complementarity seizes the opportunity. For Drone Volt, Skeyetech directly fills the long-duration autonomous surveillance segment — a rapidly growing civil and military market, particularly for critical infrastructure protection.* ## Clean Cells acquires Anaquant: mass spectrometry bioanalysis comes to Vendée **Clean Cells** (a subsidiary of the Nantes-based **Clean Biologics** group, Montaigu-Vendée) acquires **Anaquant** (Lyon, founded in 2014), a specialist in mass spectrometry bioanalysis. The amount is undisclosed. Anaquant has developed standardized mass spectrometry methods applicable from the early stages of biopharmaceutical development, allowing regulatory dossiers to be built on solid data from the outset. *For Clean Cells, the acquisition strengthens protein characterization capabilities — a central element of quality control and compliance in the biopharmaceutical sector. A rare skill acquisition, in a market where European and American regulations require increasingly early and robust analytical data.* ## TechnicoFlor acquires Azur Fragrances: Grasse as an address and label **TechnicoFlor** (perfume composer, Allauch) acquires **Azur Fragrances** (Mouans-Sartoux, founded in 1978, approximately 40 employees, **€6.2 million** in revenue in 2025), an independent family-owned company based in Grasse. The amount is undisclosed. The operation, completed in two months after an unsuccessful first attempt in 2024, brings TechnicoFlor a Grasse production site, R&D activity, and the "Parfum de Grasse" label — and consolidates a presence in the German market that Azur Fragrances had developed. *In perfumery, Grasse is not just an address: it is a certification of origin that conditions access to certain premium segments and certain clients. TechnicoFlor is buying an industrial tool as much as geographical legitimacy.* ## Implid integrates HiConnect to address foreign subsidiaries in France **Implid** (Lyon-based consulting and expertise group) acquires **HiConnect**, an independent firm specializing in social and HR support for foreign companies established in France — over 150 international companies, approximately 1,000 employees supported. The amount is undisclosed. *HiConnect occupies a precise and difficult-to-replicate niche: being the interface between a foreign headquarters and its French employees, managing not only payroll but also regulatory, cultural, and organizational complexities. For Implid, the acquisition opens up a client segment — subsidiaries of international groups — that requires expertise that generalist firms do not master. A rare skill acquisition in a defensible niche market.* ## Althays acquires Pure Illusion: the digital chain closes in Haute-Savoie **Althays** (Haute-Savoie group, management solutions, information systems, and cybersecurity) acquires **Pure Illusion** (an agency expert in digital strategy, web, and marketing). The operation was signed on July 15. The amount is undisclosed. *Consistent build-up move: Althays completes its chain by adding website creation, e-commerce, and digital marketing to its infrastructure and security foundation. The stated objective — to be a complete player in the digital chain — is clear, even if the group's size remains modest to compete with national integrators.* ## Netalis acquired by NetR SAS: Xavier Niel discreetly enters Besançon telecoms **Netalis**, a B2B telecoms operator founded in Besançon in 2015, placed in receivership at the end of April, was acquired at the beginning of July by **NetR SAS** — a company formed for the occasion. The Besançon court validated the sale on June 24, effective July 1, with all jobs preserved. Among NetR's investors are **Sipartech** (a European fiber infrastructure operator) and, unexpectedly, **NJJ Capital**, Xavier Niel's personal holding company. *The presence of NJJ Capital in a judicial takeover of a modest-sized regional B2B telecoms operator is the signal that deserves attention. Xavier Niel does not enter this type of deal out of industrial philanthropy: NJJ Capital is methodically building a portfolio of telecoms infrastructures in Europe, and judicial takeovers are a way to acquire customer bases and network assets under conditions that the normal market does not allow. Netalis survives — and perhaps becomes a brick in a larger assembly.* ## APRIL acquires Alexis Assurances and launches its specialized P&C division **APRIL** acquires **Alexis Assurances**, an independent broker located near Lyon, specializing in renewable energy and construction insurance — over 1,000 clients, from SMEs to key accounts, covering civil liability, all-risk construction, and machinery breakdown for photovoltaic and wind installations. The amount is undisclosed. The operation marks the launch of APRIL's specialized P&C division, which is expected to gradually aggregate expertise in highly technical markets. *The timing is strategic: renewable energies generate a rapidly growing demand for technical insurance, but the risks are complex (intermittency, equipment lifespan, ten-year guarantees on still-young technologies). Generalist brokers do not master these specificities. By acquiring Alexis Assurances, APRIL is not consolidating for size — it is buying rare expertise in a structurally expanding market, and positioning it as the spearhead of a division that will grow through successive acquisitions.* ## Moeve and Galp negotiate the merger of their downstream activities: a European energy player in the making **Moeve** (formerly Cepsa, Spain) and **Galp** (Portugal) are in negotiations to combine their downstream activities — fuel distribution, mobility, energy — to create a leading European energy and mobility platform. **€600 million** in investment by Moeve in 30 biomethane units in Spain provides the context for an already underway strategic transformation. The transaction amount is undisclosed. *The logic is one of transition: separately, Moeve and Galp are two Iberian oil players in reconversion; together, they form a multi-energy distribution network (fossil fuels, electric, hydrogen, biomethane) dense enough to negotiate with car manufacturers and charging operators. Size creates credibility in tomorrow's mobility. The regulatory challenge — a merger between the two main players in the Iberian Peninsula — will be decisive.* ## Aon acquires USI for $17 billion: US middle-market brokerage consolidates **Aon** (Dublin/NYSE) announces the acquisition of **USI** from **KKR** and other shareholders for **$17.0 billion** (approximately **€15.6 billion**). USI is the tenth largest US broker, with approximately **$3 billion** in annual revenue and over 10,500 employees in nearly 200 offices. The transaction is expected to be accretive to adjusted EPS in 2028, with **$395 million** in annual synergies expected at cruising speed. Aon had already acquired NFP in 2024 to establish itself in the US middle market. USI is the second piece of the same puzzle. *What this operation reveals is the platform logic applied to brokerage: Aon does not sell policies, it sells access to data, analysis, and networks that its clients cannot reconstruct alone. USI brings its proprietary analysis platform (USI ONE®) and access to the Excess & Surplus segment, one of the most dynamic in US commercial insurance. By consolidating the middle market around a differentiating data infrastructure, Aon is building a quasi-monopoly position in a segment that represents tens of thousands of American companies. For independent middle-market brokers, the window of survival is narrowing as the data platforms of large groups become indispensable.* ## Apollo sells Kelvion to SLB for $4.1 billion: data center cooling becomes a strategic asset **Apollo** sells **Kelvion** — a German manufacturer of cooling solutions for data centers and industrial clients — to **SLB** (formerly Schlumberger) for **$4.1 billion** (approximately **€3.8 billion**). *The price says it all: SLB, formerly a specialist in oilfield services, is paying a significant premium for a heat exchanger manufacturer. Data center cooling has become one of the most concrete bottlenecks to AI growth — GPUs generate considerable heat, and the ability to dissipate it efficiently directly determines the deployable computing density. SLB, which has mastered industrial thermodynamics for decades in the oil context, is repositioning this expertise towards digital infrastructures. This is a transfer of skills from a declining sector to an overheating sector — literally and figuratively.* ## Samsung launches a tender offer for PolyPeptide Group for €2.5 billion **Samsung Peptide AG** publishes a tender offer prospectus for **PolyPeptide Group AG** (Switzerland/Germany), a specialized manufacturer of peptides for the pharmaceutical industry, for approximately **€2.5 billion**. *Therapeutic peptides — including GLP-1 analogues (Ozempic, Wegovy, and their successors) — have transformed peptide CDMO into a rare and coveted asset. Samsung is consolidating a position in the global pharmaceutical production chain at the precise moment when demand for peptide synthesis capacity structurally exceeds supply. Acquiring PolyPeptide means buying production capacity that is impossible to build quickly, in a market where regulatory compliance lead times are measured in years.* ## Shell sells Sonnen to Tiven: a residential storage pioneer changes hands at a discounted price **Shell** finalizes the sale of **Sonnen** — a German pioneer in residential battery storage — to **Tiven**, a Munich-based asset manager and family office, for an amount "in the hundreds of millions of euros" according to Handelsblatt, well below the **~€500 million** paid by Shell during the 2019 acquisition. Tiven, whose central figure is Gert Purkert (founder of Aurelius, former McKinsey), plans a restructuring with a view to a future resale. *Shell had bought Sonnen to make it the foundation of its integrated home energy offering — a bet on the convergence of solar production, storage, and residential demand management. This bet was not profitable: Sonnen required an additional capital injection in 2024 to maintain its operations. The loss-making sale illustrates the difficulty of monetizing residential storage technology in a still fragmented market under price pressure. The amount is undisclosed. *Neura's ambition is precise: Adlatus is not being bought for its cleaning robots, but for its hundreds of field deployments and its knowledge of real cleaning processes. The goal is to connect these machines to the "Neuraverse" — Neura's shared AI infrastructure — to transform a robot that follows a program into an agent that understands its environment. Industrial cleaning is a massive market, with high labor intensity and low social valuation: exactly the type of market where intelligent automation can quickly create value if the technology is up to par.* ## Orrön Energy merges its Nordic assets with Cloudberry: a 2.1 TWh renewable IPP **Orrön Energy** (Sweden) contributes the majority of its Nordic renewable assets to **Cloudberry Clean Energy** (Norway), creating an independent power producer (IPP) with an annual generation of approximately **2.1 TWh** (wind, hydro, batteries) across all Nordic price markets. Cloudberry takes over **€93 million** of Orrön's debt; Orrön becomes Cloudberry's main shareholder and retains a **12 GW** European development portfolio. *Orrön is carrying out a balance sheet operation as much as a strategic one: by contributing its operational assets to Cloudberry, it clears its debt and transforms itself into a pure developer — less capital tied up in operating assets, more capacity to finance the 12 GW pipeline. This is the classic separation between the value of the operational asset (carried by Cloudberry, which has the size to optimize it) and the optional value of development (carried by Orrön, which retains flexibility). In a Nordic market where renewable electricity prices are under pressure, operational size matters more than ever.* ## Ballard acquires GeoPura: fuel cell integrates hydrogen production and distribution **Ballard Power Systems** (Canada/NASDAQ) finalizes the acquisition of **GeoPura** (United Kingdom, founded in 2019), a provider of zero-emission power solutions based on hydrogen — autonomous HPU™ units, green hydrogen production by electrolysis, the UK's largest compressed hydrogen distribution fleet. The amount is undisclosed. *Ballard manufactured the fuel cell modules that GeoPura already used in its HPU™s — the acquisition formalizes a vertical integration that already existed in practice. The transition from a component manufacturer to a "clean energy as a service" (EaaS) solution provider is strategically significant: value in the hydrogen chain is gradually shifting from the component to the turnkey solution operator. The amount is undisclosed. *Large-scale battery storage has become the indispensable flexibility infrastructure as the share of renewables in the European electricity mix increases. Alpiq is acquiring an immediately operational asset here — not a development project — which allows it to participate in system services markets in Germany today.
