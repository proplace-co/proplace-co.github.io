In focus — the deals we decoded

Genesis AI and its Eno robot: France enters the humanoid race

Source: x.com →

Genesis AI, a French startup, unveils Eno, its first humanoid robot equipped with hands offering 20 degrees of freedom — no funding amount communicated at this stage. The first degree: a product announcement in a sector saturated with promises. What deserves attention: the hand is precisely what industrial robots have never been able to do. An arm that grabs a box in a warehouse has been solved for decades; a hand that distinguishes a ripe fruit from a damaged one, untangles a cable, or turns a screw in a confined space is a problem of a completely different nature. Twenty degrees of freedom on a hand is the threshold from which robotics ceases to be a tool for repetition and potentially becomes a substitute for skilled manual labor — and this is where the next wave of automation in French industry and logistics will play out.

Orange and Morrison: 3 billion not to miss the train

Source: lavoixdefrance.fr →

Orange and Morrison announce a joint investment of 3 billion euros in five French data centers, for a total capacity of 400 MW. The obvious interpretation: two players responding to the explosive demand for compute related to AI. The reality is more structured than that. Orange brings the network, connectivity, and a captive enterprise customer base; Morrison brings infrastructure development expertise. Together, they target a specific segment: French and European companies that do not want to host their data with AWS, Azure, or Google — for regulatory reasons (GDPR, sensitive data) as well as strategic ones. At 400 MW, this is not a niche infrastructure: it is a position in the race for digital sovereignty, a bet that the demand for sovereign compute in Europe will be deep enough to justify heavy and long-to-amortize assets — a reasonable bet, but whose return depends entirely on Orange's ability to convert its customer base into tenants of these centers.

Geodis acquires Deret: SNCF recovers a weakened competitor

Source: lesechos.fr →

Geodis (SNCF subsidiary) enters into exclusive negotiations to acquire part of Deret group's logistics and transport activities, as well as all associated real estate assets. Deret, France's sixth-largest player in the transport-logistics segment, based in Saran near Orléans, is going through a period of judicial turbulence: its patriarch Lucien Deret has been indicted for misuse of company assets. This is Geodis's second major acquisition in the French market in a few months, after the acquisition of Caen-based transporter Malherbe in March. The mechanism is clear: a family business weakened by a governance crisis finds itself in a weak position at the precise moment when its main shareholder needs liquidity or stability. Geodis is not just buying trucks and warehouses — it is buying market share at crisis prices, and the real estate assets included in the operation constitute a tangible base that secures the balance sheet regardless of future operational performance.

OVH absorbs Gladia: the missing brick in the sovereign AI stack

Source: boursier.com →

OVH Groupe finalizes the acquisition of Gladia, a French expert in AI audio transcription (speech-to-text), announced on June 11. The transaction is carried out by share exchange: 1,807,186 newly issued OVH shares at a unit price of 15.66 euros, representing a value of approximately 28 million euros, with conditional warrants that could bring the issuance to more than 5 million additional shares depending on 2026-2027 performance. What is at stake here goes beyond technology acquisition. OVH is methodically building a complete AI stack — sovereign compute, models, and now modalities (voice). Audio transcription is not a peripheral feature: it is a fundamental building block for AI agents, automated meeting systems, call centers, healthcare, and legal. By internalizing Gladia rather than relying on OpenAI or Google APIs, OVH avoids building a sovereign offering whose critical layers would remain dependent on American providers — this is the difference between real integration and commercial dressing.

Groupe Wattez acquires Régal Marée: consolidation in the Boulogne-sur-Mer fish processing industry

Groupe Wattez acquires Régal Marée, a fish filleting company with 37 employees, based in Boulogne-sur-Mer. This is a sectoral consolidation operation in a sector — fish processing — where critical mass conditions relationships with large retailers and institutional catering. Boulogne-sur-Mer remains France's leading fishing port in terms of volume processed; consolidating processing capacities there strengthens a geographical and commercial position that is difficult to replicate elsewhere.

Orchestra acquires Afidium: French travel tech structures itself

Source: air-journal.fr → · Sector Mobility & Transportation — 📬 subscribe to the Mobility & Transportation newsletter

Travelsoft, through its French subsidiary Orchestra, acquires Afidium, a B2B travel software publisher founded in 2005. Afidium brings pricing optimization tools (FareOpt), quality control of booking files (QualityCheck), and multi-source management (Watabi). In a sector where agency and tour operator margins are structurally under pressure, process automation is no longer a competitive advantage — it is a condition for survival. By absorbing Afidium, Orchestra is not just buying features: it is buying the customer base and contracts of a direct competitor, accelerating a consolidation whose probable outcome is a duopoly or a narrow oligopoly in French B2B travel software.

IWG acquires Wojo: the end of a hotel interlude in coworking

Source: abcbourse.com → · Sector Construction & PropTech — 📬 subscribe to the Construction & PropTech newsletter

IWG (International Workplace Group) acquires Wojo, Accor's coworking business, making it a 100% subsidiary. Wojo integrates 186 flexible workspaces. Accor and Bouygues Immobilier held Wojo as a joint venture. The operation is accompanied by a long-term partnership with Accor to develop workspaces within its hotel network. What is really happening: Accor recognizes that coworking is an infrastructure and global network business, not a hotel service. IWG, already present in over 120 countries with more than 6,000 sites, is the only player capable of amortizing development costs at this scale. The post-acquisition partnership is the smart part: Accor sells the asset but retains the customer flow, IWG acquires 186 sites without development risks — it's a deal where both parties sell what they don't do well and keep what they master.

RWS acquires Acolad: AI translation consolidates Europe

Source: prnewswire.com → · Sector B2B Software & Cloud — 📬 subscribe to the B2B Software & Cloud newsletter

RWS, a UK group listed on AIM and positioned in AI solutions for content, announces an agreement to acquire Acogroup, the parent company of Acolad, a global provider of translation and content services, headquartered in the Paris region. Acolad employs approximately 1,200 people in 22 countries in Europe and North America, and offers localization, interpreting, transcription, and an AI content platform. Amount undisclosed. The translation sector is undergoing a complete transformation: generative AI tools have radically reduced the marginal cost of raw translation, which destroys the value of players positioned on volume and shifts it towards those who master quality, cultural context, and integration into customer workflows. By acquiring Acolad, RWS is not buying translators — it is buying a European customer base to which it can sell its AI platforms, transforming a service acquisition into a technology deployment accelerator.

Iberdrola acquires Finnish electricity distributor: 5 billion for the Northern network

Source: europ.info →

Iberdrola announces the acquisition of Finland's largest electricity distributor, valued at 5 billion euros, serving over 1.1 million customers. This is one of the largest movements in European energy infrastructure this year. Electricity distribution is a regulated asset: returns are capped by the regulator, but they are stable, predictable, and not subject to direct competition. In a context of still high interest rates and uncertainty in energy markets, this type of asset commands a significant premium. Finland is also at the heart of the Nordic energy transition — high penetration of renewable energies, smart grids, electrification of uses. Iberdrola is not paying 5 billion for meters and cables: it is paying for a distribution infrastructure that will be the necessary conduit for all the green electricity that Finland will produce in the next twenty years — a regulated annuity on the energy transition.

KKR takes 50% of TotalEnergies' European solar and onshore wind portfolio: 1.8 billion

Source: pehub.com → · Sector Climate & Energy Tech — 📬 subscribe to the Climate & Energy Tech newsletter

KKR acquires a 50% stake in TotalEnergies' European onshore renewable energy (solar and wind) portfolio for 1.8 billion euros. This is a classic capital recycling structure for an oil major: TotalEnergies builds the renewable assets, scales them up, then sells a share to an infrastructure fund to free up capital and finance the next development cycle. KKR obtains a stable cash-flow generating asset backed by long-term contracts. This is less a bet on the energy transition than a well-established financial mechanism: TotalEnergies plays the role of the developer turning its balance sheet, KKR plays the role of the holder of mature assets — the model works as long as renewable electricity prices remain high enough to justify entry valuations.

ASSA ABLOY acquires Gunnebo Entrance Control: physical access control consolidates

Source: finanznachrichten.de →

ASSA ABLOY announces the acquisition of Gunnebo Entrance Control, a British provider of physical access control solutions (security gates, turnstiles, secure doors), which generated 151 million euros in revenue in 2025 with a good operating margin. Amount undisclosed. ASSA ABLOY, already a global leader in opening and access systems, adds a complementary range in high-traffic environments (public transport, stadiums, secure buildings). The expected dilutive effect on EPS is described as low by the group. This is a position-strengthening operation in mature markets, consistent with the group's stated strategy.

Ceva Logistics acquires Paack in Spain and France: CMA CGM goes last mile

Source: boursorama.com → · Sector Mobility & Transportation — 📬 subscribe to the Mobility & Transportation newsletter

Ceva Logistics (CMA CGM subsidiary) acquires Paack, a last-mile e-commerce delivery specialist in Spain and France, for 49 million euros. Paack has positioned itself on precise time-slot delivery, popular with premium e-merchants and brands that want to avoid image damage related to missed deliveries. For CMA CGM, which has built a logistics conglomerate covering maritime, air, warehousing, and land transport in just a few years, the last mile was the missing link. At 49 million, Paack is a low-risk acquisition to test a complete vertical integration of the logistics chain — from the hold of the container ship to the consumer's door — which, if the model works, transforms CMA CGM into a direct competitor of Amazon Logistics on its own turf.

Pall-Ex Romania: management buyout with BoldMind above 25 million euros

Source: romania-insider.com →

The BoldMind fund and the management team of Pall-Ex Romania jointly acquire the company in a deal exceeding 25 million euros. Pall-Ex is a pallet network for groupage transport, active in Central and Eastern Europe. This is a management buyout operation in a logistics segment where regional consolidation is underway, driven by e-commerce growth and the industrialization of Eastern European markets.

In focus — the deals we decoded

Mistral AI at 20 billion valuation: the race for resources above all

Source: lavoixdefrance.fr →

Mistral AI is negotiating a 3 billion euro fundraising at a target valuation of 20 billion euros, according to Bloomberg — almost double its September 2025 valuation (11.7 billion). Discussions are preliminary. Mistral's annualized recurring revenue was 400 million dollars in January 2026, compared to 16 million at the end of 2024 — growth that justifies a revaluation. What this fundraising reveals is less Mistral's commercial health than the logic of the sector: the race for frontier models is above all a race for computing resources. Mistral aims for 1 GW of computing capacity by 2030, with 200 MW by 2027. A 3 billion fundraising in this context is not an ordinary growth round — it is a buy-in in a competition where whoever lacks compute in the next eighteen months incurs a structural delay that is difficult to overcome, regardless of the quality of their engineers.

OLIX raises 270 million at 2.8 billion valuation: rethinking the inference chip

Source: eu-startups.com →

OLIX, a British startup founded in 2024, raises 270.5 million euros in Series B at a valuation of 2.8 billion euros. The round includes Fundomo, Arm, and Hudson River Trading, with the participation of Reed Hastings (Netflix co-founder) and increased investment from all existing investors. OLIX designs AI inference chips based on a simple observation: a data center produces tokens like a factory produces parts, and each stage of this production has different hardware needs. Using the same general-purpose GPU for all stages is like running an entire industrial chain on the same versatile machine — efficient up to a certain point, then fundamentally limited. OLIX internally designs the chips, lasers, and the network that connects them. The presence of Arm as an investor is a strong signal: Arm is at the heart of the architecture of global mobile and embedded chips, and its investment in OLIX suggests a possible convergence between edge inference architectures and data center systems. Two years of existence, 2.8 billion valuation: this is the pace of the AI semiconductor race, where the window to establish oneself as an alternative to Nvidia is closing fast — and where investors are betting on architecture even before seeing the first revenues at scale.

Multiverse Computing raises 500 million in Series C: quantum computing enters the industrial phase

Multiverse Computing (San Sebastián) raises 500 million euros in Series C at a valuation of 1.5 billion euros, becoming the new Spanish unicorn. The company compresses large language models for deployment on cloud, edge, and on-device environments. This positioning is strategic: it directly addresses the inference cost constraint, which is one of the main obstacles to the profitability of large-scale AI models. A 500 million fundraising on this precise theme — compression and efficiency rather than raw power — suggests that the market is beginning to value computational sobriety as much as maximum performance.

Engo raises 5 million for sports smart glasses

Source: mesinfos.fr →

Engo, a Grenoble-based spin-off of MicroOled founded in 2025, raises 5.1 million euros from Ventech, Odyssée Venture, and Bpifrance Amorçage Industriel. Engo produces ultra-light smart glasses (less than 40 grams, up to 20 hours of battery life) integrating an augmented reality Micro OLED screen that projects performance data into the field of vision of endurance athletes. Where Google, Meta, or Samsung offer smart frames without an integrated screen, Engo bets on head-up display as a central differentiator. The display technology comes directly from MicroOled, giving Engo a miniaturization advantage that is difficult to quickly replicate. For a French investor, this is a bet on a premium niche (the endurance athlete who pays for performance) before a possible extension to professional or medical uses — the classic path for wearable technologies that first find their market in sports.

Féroce raises 3 million for regenerative proteins by subscription

Source: renseignementeconomique.fr →

Féroce, a Haute-Savoie startup founded in July 2024, closes 3 million euros, of which 1.48 million was raised via Crowdcube from over 800 customer-investors. The company offers meats and fish from regenerative farming, distributed in frozen boxes by subscription (Box M at €149, Box L at €249, deliveries every 4 to 8 weeks). What is notable in the round structure: almost half comes from customers. A customer who invests in a food brand does not just provide funding — they commit to staying, recommending, and defending the brand in tough times. Féroce has transformed its funding into a loyalty tool, which structurally reduces its future customer acquisition cost.

Dwelly raises 170 million in Series B: AI consolidates the UK rental market

Dwelly (London, founded in 2023) raises 170 million dollars in Series B led by EQT Growth, with the participation of General Catalyst and other funds. Dwelly acquires and automates UK rental agencies with AI, targeting the consolidation of the UK rental market estimated at 100 billion pounds. The model: acquire traditional agencies, integrate their operations onto a unified AI platform, reduce management costs while improving the tenant experience. This is the Dwelly-in-real-estate model that Veolia applied to environmental services or that PE consolidators apply to accounting firms: the technological platform as a lever for integrating fragmented assets — the risk being that the cultural integration of acquired agencies will be slower than expected.

Aiforia raises 20 million EIB for AI cancer diagnosis

Source: eu-startups.com →

Aiforia (Helsinki, listed), a specialist in pathological image analysis using deep learning, secures 20 million euros in venture debt from the European Investment Bank under the InvestEU program. The financing is structured in three tranches conditioned on revenue milestones, with a non-dilutive synthetic warrant mechanism for existing shareholders. Aiforia operates in cancer detection through histological image analysis — an area where AI can reduce diagnostic times and improve early detection. The tranche structure is a useful discipline: it forces the company to demonstrate commercial traction before unlocking subsequent funds.

metr raises 10.5 million in Series B for building management

metr (Germany) raises 10.5 million euros in Series B with the entry of SBG – Sächsische Beteiligungsgesellschaft and the participation of Kachel GmbH (a subsidiary of the WIKA group), IBB Capital, BRAWO GROUP, and Next Big Thing AG. metr develops a Building Management System platform. The funds will be used for technological development, operational expansion, and the opening of a hub in Saxony. The participation of an industrial player like WIKA (measurement instrumentation) as an investor is a signal of sectoral validation: metr addresses a market where established players are looking for technological partners rather than frontal competitors.

Warren raises 10 million in Seed to reinvent supplementary pensions in Belgium

Source: konabedandbreakfast.com → · Sector FinTech — 📬 subscribe to the FinTech newsletter

Warren (Belgium) raises 10 million euros in Seed to deploy a transparent and low-cost corporate supplementary pension platform. The company has obtained an IBP (Institution for Occupational Retirement Provision) license and manages its own pension fund, eliminating traditional intermediaries. The offering combines algorithmic management and human advice. The Belgian supplementary pension market is structurally opaque and underperforming — Warren is betting on increasing European regulation in favor of fee transparency to accelerate adoption.

InsectBiotech raises 8 million to transform olive waste into proteins

Source: europesays.com →

InsectBiotech (Spain) raises 7.2 million euros with the participation of Arcano Partners via the Impact Andalucía Innovation fund and undisclosed American investors. The company uses black soldier fly larvae to convert by-products from the Andalusian olive oil industry into proteins, oil, and fertilizer. The planned production site in Andalusia will process up to 7,500 tons of agricultural by-products per year. The logic is sound: Andalusia produces a massive amount of olive residues (pomace) and benefits from exceptional sunshine — InsectBiotech has found a geography where feedstock and energy, the two structural costs of insect farming, are naturally cheap.

ZuriQ raises 22.4 million in Seed for trapped-ion quantum processors

ZuriQ (Zurich) raises 22.4 million euros in Seed to develop 2D trapped-ion quantum processors. Trapped-ion quantum computing is one of the most promising architectures for achieving sufficient quantum coherence for useful calculations — but also one of the most difficult to industrialize. A Seed round of this amount for this technology reflects the growing conviction that the window for building positions in quantum hardware is closing rapidly, before the first commercial applications identify the winners.

Agon raises 30 million in Seed for AI anti-drone defense

Agon (London) raises 30 million dollars in Seed to develop AI training models for European defense infrastructure and anti-drone capabilities. A 30 million Seed round in deeptech defense is a sign of acceleration: generalist funds are entering segments they avoided three years ago, driven by real demand from NATO member states seeking sovereign solutions.

Greyparrot raises 27 million in Series B for waste intelligence

Greyparrot (London) raises 27 million dollars in Series B to deploy its AI platform for analyzing waste streams in sorting and recycling centers. The technology identifies and quantifies materials in real-time on sorting belts, improving recovery rates and traceability. In a context of increasing regulation on the circular economy in Europe, sorting operators have a real need for granular data on what they process.

Xeltis raises 20.5 million for living vessels

Xeltis (Netherlands) raises 20.5 million euros to develop artificial cardiovascular vessels and valves designed to be gradually replaced by the patient's own living tissues. This regenerative medicine approach avoids the long-term complications of permanent prostheses. This funding is part of the European medtech dynamic supported by the EIB and specialized life sciences funds.

Ominimo raises 20 million in Series B: AI car insurance becomes a unicorn

Ominimo (Budapest) raises 20.1 million euros in Series B at a valuation of 1.4 billion euros, becoming the new European InsurTech unicorn. The company offers AI-driven car insurance and is profitable two years after its launch.
