Fossil and renewable energy, asset management, medtech, specialized insurance, future food, quantum computing: July 28 aggregates movements covering almost the entire European industrial spectrum. Some operations stand out due to their size or logic — the Vår Energi-BlueNord merger, BNP Paribas' acquisition of AXA IM, Multiverse Computing's fundraising. But it's the overall picture that says something: capital is simultaneously deployed on the oldest physical infrastructures (gas stations, wind turbines, hospitals) and on the most speculative technologies (quantum, precision fermentation). Two speeds, one allocation logic.

In focus — the deals we decoded

BNP Paribas–AXA IM: when a bank decides not to let others manage its money anymore

Source: europesays.com → · Sector FinTech — 📬 subscribe to the FinTech newsletter

BNP Paribas enters into exclusive negotiations to acquire 100% of AXA Investment Managers from AXA, for €5.1 billion, with a long-term partnership in which AXA will continue to entrust a significant portion of its assets to the combined platform.

The immediate interpretation: BNP gains critical mass in asset management — approximately €1,500 billion in combined assets under management — to compete with Amundi and Nordic giants. A classic sectoral consolidation move, driven by economies of scale.

But the deeper mechanism lies elsewhere. What changes is not just size: it's the nature of revenue. A deposit and lending bank lives on intermediation — it takes balance sheet risk, it is exposed to cycles. An asset management platform collects fees on assets under management, regardless of market conditions, without committing its own capital. By absorbing AXA IM, BNP is not just growing: it is partially transforming into a rent-generating machine from financial flows. The announced CET1 impact — approximately 25 basis points — is remarkably low for a €5 billion operation, meaning the acquisition barely impacts regulatory capital. And the expected return on invested capital exceeds 18% from the third year post-integration.

For a French financial player seeking to understand where European banking profitability is headed, this operation is a clear signal: value is migrating from the balance sheet to delegated management, and those without a large-scale asset platform will increasingly pay more to rent one.

Vår Energi–BlueNord: geology as a strategic argument

Source: energiesmedia.com → · Sector Climate & Energy Tech — 📬 subscribe to the Climate & Energy Tech newsletter

Norwegian Vår Energi announces the acquisition of BlueNord, an operator on the Danish continental shelf, in a merger that would make the combined group the largest independent hydrocarbon producer in Europe.

The stated logic is geographical: to venture beyond the Norwegian shelf for the first time, adding Danish assets. But the operational rationale is more precise. Vår Energi argues that the two shelves share the same geological, fiscal, and operational characteristics — which concretely means that teams, processes, and service contracts are transferable without learning costs. This is not geographical diversification; it is a lateral extension into familiar territory.

In a context where access to capital for hydrocarbons is gradually tightening, size becomes a protection: a producer of this magnitude carries more weight in negotiations with states, banks, and license partners. The merger consolidates an extraction rent at a time when every additional barrel extracted in the North Sea is also an energy security argument for Europe.

Vusion acquires In-Store Media: transforming the label into an advertising panel

Source: fr.finance.yahoo.com → · Sector Retail & E-commerce Tech — 📬 subscribe to the Retail & E-commerce Tech newsletter

Vusion, a specialist in electronic shelf labels for large retailers, announces the acquisition of In-Store Media (ISM), an in-store advertising agency based in Barcelona, present in nine countries and working with 90 retailers and over 1,600 brands.

Vusion has equipped millions of shelves with connected labels. ISM knows how to sell advertising space inside stores. The combination of the two creates something that did not exist before: a physical advertising infrastructure that can be controlled in real-time, as close as possible to the moment of purchase.

The challenge is not so much in-store advertising as the monetization of the already deployed infrastructure. Each electronic label installed at a retailer becomes a display point potentially rented to a brand — the retailer gains a new revenue stream without additional capex. This is the same logic as Amazon with its logistics network transformed into an advertising platform, applied to physical retail.

Saint-Gobain in Asia-Pacific: two acquisitions, two geographies

Source: boursorama.com →

Saint-Gobain finalizes the acquisition of Phu My Innovative Materials (PMIM) in Vietnam and signs an agreement to take a majority stake in AGC Polymer Materials in Japan (a subsidiary of AGC), with closing expected in the first quarter of 2027.

Two discreet operations, but consistent with the group's long-term strategy: to strengthen its presence in high-growth construction markets (Vietnam) and to access advanced polymer materials through the partnership with AGC in Japan. Saint-Gobain is methodically building a local presence in areas where demand for construction and renovation materials is structurally growing.

Objectway acquires SLIB from BNP Paribas and Natixis

Source: fintech.global → · Sector FinTech — 📬 subscribe to the FinTech newsletter

Objectway, a European technology provider for wealth and asset management, enters into exclusive negotiations to acquire SLIB, a French specialist in capital markets software, from BNP Paribas and Natixis.

SLIB covers technical segments that Objectway did not: securities processing, trading, clearing, settlement, risk management. The acquisition extends Objectway's platform beyond wealth management to the entire financial markets value chain, and gives it a direct presence in France — the second-largest European financial market — as well as in Lisbon.

For French financial institutions using SLIB, the concrete question is one of continuity: the two current executives remain in place post-acquisition, which limits the risk of disruption. For Objectway, the operation is a shortcut to the technical complexity that banks now demand from their unified providers.

Goldman Sachs acquires Numantec for €700 million

Source: globalbankingandfinance.com → · Sector FinTech — 📬 subscribe to the FinTech newsletter

The private equity arm of Goldman Sachs acquires control of Numantec, an Italian manufacturer of medical devices and drug delivery systems based near Mantua, from White Bridge Investment, for an estimated value of €700 million.

Numantec was built by White Bridge through successive acquisitions since 2021, starting from a vascular access device manufacturer. Approximately 600 employees, seven production sites in Europe and the United States. Goldman is betting on revenue visibility — consumable medical devices generate recurring flows, little sensitive to economic cycles.

In a private equity environment where exits remain difficult (the KPMG report published today indicates the slowest pace of exits in over a decade), Goldman deliberately chooses an asset with predictable revenues rather than a speculative growth story. This is the choice of stability over speculative valuation.

Cinven and La Caisse acquire Optio, a specialized MGA platform

Source: newswire.ca → · Sector Mobility & Transportation — 📬 subscribe to the Mobility & Transportation newsletter

Cinven and La Caisse (formerly CDPQ) jointly announce the acquisition of Optio Group, a UK-based Managing General Agent platform specializing in niche insurance, with operations in 15 countries across six business lines (professional liability, transport, real estate-energy, health, transactional guarantees, surety-credit).

The MGA is a particular insurance model: the company designs and underwrites risks, but does not bear them itself — it cedes them to third-party capacity providers. The result: almost zero exposure to insurance risk, revenues based on commissions and management fees, growth by adding new lines or geographies. This is precisely the profile that PE funds are looking for right now: a financial activity with high intellectual added value, without the balance sheet of a traditional insurance company.

TÜV SÜD acquires SustainCERT: carbon certification as a strategic asset

Source: esgtoday.com →

German TÜV SÜD acquires SustainCERT, a Luxembourg-based climate impact verification platform founded in 2018 by the Gold Standard Foundation, specializing in digital verification of carbon credits and decarbonization of value chains.

While voluntary carbon markets have been experiencing a credibility crisis for several years, TÜV SÜD makes the opposite bet: it is precisely because the demand for independent and rigorous verification is increasing that the asset is worth something. SustainCERT brings a digital Measurement-Reporting-Verification (dMRV) capability that TÜV SÜD did not possess. The acquisition positions TÜV SÜD as a central player in the tightening carbon regulation — an infrastructure of trust in a market that lacks it.

Central Garden & Pet takes control of TRIXIE

Source: europesays.com → · Sector Developer & IT Infrastructure — 📬 subscribe to the Developer & IT Infrastructure newsletter

American Central Garden & Pet announces the acquisition of a majority stake in TRIXIE Heimtierbedarf, a European leader in pet accessories and snacks, founded in 1974 and based in Tarp, Germany, from its founder and current shareholders.

TRIXIE: approximately 600 employees, over 6,000 product references, 90% of revenue from own brands, presence in over 30,000 points of sale. For Central, this is entry into the European pet care market with an established brand and existing distribution — faster and less risky than organic growth. The founders retain a minority stake and management remains in place.

Enilive acquires 320 service stations in Central Europe

Source: biobased-diesel.com →

Eni's mobility subsidiary, Enilive, signs an agreement to acquire OIL! Tankstellen GmbH from Prax, an operator of 320 service stations in Germany, Denmark, Austria, and Switzerland.

Enilive already operates approximately 5,300 stations in Europe. The acquisition strengthens its presence in German-speaking markets and opens up the Danish market. The strategic interest goes beyond volume: these stations will be progressively integrated into the distribution network for renewable fuels (HVO, biofuel produced in Eni's biorefineries in Venice and Gela), fast charging stations, and the Enilive Café catering offer. The physical distribution network becomes the rare asset: it is harder to replicate than the technology itself.

Galp buys 361 MW of wind power from Acciona Energía in Spain

Source: renewablesnow.com →

Portuguese Galp finalizes the acquisition of a 361 MW onshore wind portfolio from Acciona Energía in Spain.

Portfolio densification operation: Galp consolidates its presence in Iberian wind power with assets in production, without development risk. For Acciona, the sale frees up capital for other projects. A clean transaction, with a portfolio rebalancing logic on both sides.

Global private equity at its lowest transaction level in five years

Source: ipe.com →

KPMG's quarterly report on global private equity (Q2 2026) documents a notable deterioration: the number of transactions over a rolling twelve-month period reaches its lowest level in twenty-one quarters, exits are at their slowest pace in over a decade (1,315 exits in the first half), and overall investment volume is declining.

The important nuance: the average deal value is holding up. Funds are abandoning small opportunistic operations to focus on high-value conviction bets — exactly what Goldman is doing with Numantec, Cinven with Optio. This is not a market that is uniformly contracting: it is a market that is becoming more selective. For quality targets with visible revenues, conditions remain favorable. For everything else, liquidity has withdrawn.

Alpha (Raise) acquires a Gironde roofing and waterproofing company

Source: cfnews.net → · Sector FinTech — 📬 subscribe to the FinTech newsletter

Alpha, a Marseille-based roofing and waterproofing group in LBO with Raise since 2024 (€81 million in revenue), makes a build-up acquisition in Gironde for €9 million.

Geographical densification operation in a fragmented sector. The logic is classic: consolidate a regional artisanal market under a national brand, pool support functions, improve margins. Nothing unusual, but consistent with the value plan announced during the LBO.

Multiverse Computing raises up to €570 million: quantum enters the unicorn league

Source: tech.eu →

Spanish Multiverse Computing, a specialist in quantum computing applied to finance, energy, and industry, announces a funding round of up to $570 million (approximately €524 million), co-led by Forgepoint Capital, which would value the company as a European unicorn.

Multiverse does not build quantum processors — it develops algorithms and software that run on existing quantum machines (and on classical simulators in the meantime). This is a deliberately pragmatic positioning: not betting on hardware, whose maturity timelines remain uncertain, but capturing application value now.

A round of this scale in European quantum is unprecedented. It signals that institutional investors — not just specialized venture funds — are beginning to treat quantum as a strategic infrastructure to be funded before the market is clear. This is the same movement we saw in generative AI in 2021-2022: capital precedes demand to lock in positions.

Standing Ovation raises $34 million with Bel and Danone as investors

Source: lavoixdefrance.fr →

Parisian deeptech Standing Ovation closes a Series B of $34.2 million (€31 million) to industrialize and deploy in the United States its casein produced by precision fermentation — a dairy protein made without cows, molecularly identical to the original. The round is co-led by the Ecotechnologies 2 fund (managed by Bpifrance on behalf of the State as part of France 2030) and Crédit Mutuel Innovation, with the participation of Danone Ventures as a new entrant alongside historical investors Groupe Bel, Astanor, and Seventure Partners.

The simultaneous presence of Bel and Danone as investors is not anecdotal. These two groups are both investors and potential customers — they validate the technology while ensuring preferential access if fermentation casein reaches industrial scale. This is a form of strategic insurance: by entering early, they avoid becoming dependent on a supplier they did not support. For Standing Ovation, it is a validation that dairy industrialists themselves consider precision fermentation a credible threat — and prefer to manage it from within.

Ominimo: a profitable insurtech unicorn built by a development bank

Source: thenextweb.com →

Ominimo, an automotive insurer operating in four European countries, reaches a valuation of €1.4 billion in its Series B, with the EBRD (European Bank for Reconstruction and Development) as an investor.

Two years after its first policy, Ominimo shows a rare profitability for an insurtech at this stage. The particularity of the model: the company does not bear the insurance risk itself — it cedes it to third-party capacity providers, exactly like Optio in the MGA segment. It collects management fees and commissions, without exposing its balance sheet to claims. This "asset-light" risk model allows for rapid growth without the solvency constraints that weigh on traditional insurers — and explains why the EBRD, whose mission is the development of efficient financial markets in Europe, sees it as a relevant investment.

Hekat Fluidics raises €2.4 million to detect cancers via blood tests

Source: europesays.com →

Bordeaux startup Hekat Fluidics raises €2.4 million in seed funding to finance the development of its exosome sorting instrument — nanometric vesicles released by tumor cells into bodily fluids, carrying information about the type of tumor, its aggressiveness, and its sensitivity to treatments.

The clinical challenge is real: exosomes are very early cancer biomarkers, detectable by a simple blood test, but unusable in routine practice due to the lack of a suitable instrument. Hekat Fluidics is developing precisely this instrument, at the initial request of the Pasteur Institute. At this stage, the fundraising finances the instrumental proof of concept before a next clinical stage.

Nuclear Turbines raises €17.5 million for compact reactors

Source: eu-startups.com → · Sector Climate & Energy Tech — 📬 subscribe to the Climate & Energy Tech newsletter

British startup Nuclear Turbines, an energy spin-off of BAE Systems, raises €17.5 million to develop compact and economical nuclear systems.

The positioning is that of small modular reactors (SMRs) applied to decentralized industrial or military uses. The link with BAE Systems gives the company access to a defense-heavy industry customer base that independent nuclear startups do not have. A modest-sized round for a sector that requires considerable capital — this is a technology validation round, not yet industrialization.
