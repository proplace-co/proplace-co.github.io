Analyse des opérations M&A & levée de fonds du 12 septembre 2026M&A & Fundraising Analysis for September 12, 2026 SUBTITLE=== New space, carbon footprint, vertical software, and charging infrastructure: European capital is repositioning itself on what cannot be dematerialized — a complete daily overview for decision-makers and investors. ===BODY=== Space and energy structured the fundraising side today, while vertical software and industrial automation concentrated M&A movements. France occupies an unusual space: between a European record in new space, an accelerated consolidation of regulatory carbon footprints, and discreet but methodical external growth operations in engineering and healthcare. In Europe, two take-privates of listed software companies in England signal that public markets continue to undervalue what private equity is willing to pay. # 🤝 M&A Transactions ## KEBA acquires Transtechnik: When industrial automation is bought through a trusted network **KEBA**, an Austrian group specializing in industrial automation, acquires **Transtechnik SAS**, a Dijon-based integrator founded in 1987, active in cosmetics, packaging, and agri-food. Amount not disclosed. The immediate interpretation: an international player strengthening its commercial presence in France, a market considered strategic for automation in Europe. *What is really at play is more subtle: Transtechnik is not a distributor; it is a project engineering firm that supports its clients throughout an industrial cycle — from consulting to commissioning. What KEBA is buying is less a order book than a thirty-five-year-old relationship of trust with demanding clients. In a market where French industrialists are hesitant to entrust their transformation to a foreign supplier without local roots, this relational depth is precisely what cannot be built quickly.* For a European automation player looking to establish itself in France, the shortest path is often to acquire someone who already knows the customers. ## Caudalie Group acquires Talm: The first acquisition by a house that never made any **Caudalie Group**, founded by Mathilde and Bertrand Thomas, acquires **Talm**, a young Parisian cosmetics brand founded five years ago by Kenza Keller. Amount not disclosed. For Caudalie, this is a first: the group had never before acquired an external brand. The operation was built on the continuity of a personal relationship between founders — Kenza Keller was looking for accelerated distribution, Caudalie was looking to broaden its spectrum without diluting its identity. *This type of founder-to-founder acquisition in premium cosmetics works when the seller chooses their acquirer as much as the other way around. Distribution is the real asset Talm is buying here — not a check, but access to a retail network and international infrastructure that five years of organic growth would not have been enough to build.* For emerging brands in the sector, the operation is a reminder that an exit to a family group aligned with their values can be worth more, in the long run, than an additional fundraising round. ## TotalEnergies buys back Plastic Energy's 35% stake in Grandpuits: Industrial sovereignty before profitability **TotalEnergies** increases its stake in the **Grandpuits** (Seine-et-Marne) chemical recycling plant to 100%, after acquiring the share held by the British company **Plastic Energy**, as part of the latter's capital restructuring. The plant, which started operations in March 2026, processes 15,000 tons of plastic waste per year through pyrolysis. The obvious interpretation: TotalEnergies consolidates a symbolically strong industrial asset — the conversion of an oil site into recycling infrastructure — and simplifies its governance. *The important detail lies in the circumstances: it was Plastic Energy's restructuring that made the sale possible, not an offensive strategic decision by TotalEnergies. The group therefore regains full control of an asset whose capacity represents less than 0.3% of household packaging placed on the French market — a symbolic volume in the face of criticism about the energy-intensive nature of the process. What TotalEnergies is buying here is primarily complete control of an industrial showcase at a time when European regulations on recycled plastics will create captive demand.* The question is not the immediate profitability of Grandpuits — it is whether the group will be in a position to scale up when regulations make this outlet mandatory. ## Qualitas Energy acquires Cero Generation's European platform: 5.8 GW change hands **Qualitas Energy** acquires **Cero Generation**'s European platform, previously owned by **Macquarie Group**, via its Qualitas Energy Fund VI. The portfolio covers 5.8 GW spread across the United Kingdom, Italy, and Spain — including over 2 GW in operation, construction, or ready-to-build, and 3.8 GW in the development pipeline. Amount not disclosed. Cero was created in 2021 by Macquarie as a vehicle dedicated to solar PV and battery energy storage systems (BESS). It retains its teams and offices in London, Milan, and Madrid under the Cero brand. *What Macquarie is selling is development pipeline as much as assets in production — and that is precisely what Qualitas is paying for: the ability to transform 3.8 GW of construction rights into future cash flows. In renewable energy, the real competitive advantage is no longer knowing how to build solar panels; it is controlling authorized land and grid connections in markets where permits are becoming scarce.* For a player like Qualitas, acquiring a pipeline of this size in three markets where it was already present is a way to buy ten years of organic development in a single transaction. # 🚀 Fundraisings ## The Exploration Company raises $450 million: European new space tests its maturity **The Exploration Company (TEC)**, founded in 2021 by Hélène Huby, closes a **$450 million Series C** (~**€414 million**), a European record for a space company at this stage. The fundraising brings the company's cumulative funding to approximately $680 million since its creation. Following this, the European Space Agency awards TEC a **€760 million** service contract for the next phase of the European reusable space cargo program. TEC develops the Nyx capsule at three sites: Munich (headquarters), Le Haillan near Bordeaux, and Turin. The immediate interpretation: a Franco-German startup establishing itself as a European new space champion, supported by Macron and Von der Leyen at the Paris summit. *Two elements deserve to be separated. First, the fundraising: at this stage of development, $450 million primarily finances heavy physical capital — engines, test infrastructure, assembly lines — with still modest teams. This is the financial structure of an industry, not software: almost all the money goes into machines and engineering, very little into payroll proportionally. This is not a flaw; it is the nature of the space sector. Second, the ESA contract: €760 million in guaranteed revenue over several years means public demand retroactively validates private infrastructure — exactly the mechanism that allowed SpaceX to survive its early years.* For European investors in industrial deep tech, TEC illustrates that the "sovereign contract + private capital" model can work without relying exclusively on NASA or the Pentagon. ## Logivolt raises €300 million: Funding charging stations or funding a network? **Logivolt** obtains a **€300 million green loan** via the **BPCE** group to deploy 1.7 million residential charging points by 2030. The obvious interpretation: supporting the rise of electric vehicles, which now account for 35% of new registrations. *The real issue is in the physics of the network, not in finance. 1.7 million residential charging stations connected simultaneously between 7 PM and 11 PM represent a load that, according to operator estimates, could be equivalent to several nuclear reactors during peak hours. The question that this funding does not solve — and that no one directly asks — is that of intelligent load management: without active coordination between charging stations, neighborhood transformers become the bottleneck. What Logivolt is actually funding is as much a network infrastructure problem as a charging station problem.* For local authorities and network managers, the challenge is not the number of charging stations installed but the ability to modulate their power demand — and that is where the true value of the deployment will lie. ## Mistral raises €105 million from European investors: The political signal behind the funding round **Mistral AI** closes a **€105 million** round, fully subscribed by European investors, in a context where its main €3 billion fundraising (announced in dominant news) marks a turning point for the French ecosystem. The operation is presented as a signal of digital sovereignty: demonstrating that Europe can finance its own foundation models without relying on American or Asian capital. *What is interesting about this specific round — distinct from the main fundraising — is precisely its composition: 100% European in a market where large American funds are actively seeking to take positions in AI labs. This is not a coincidence of timing; it is a deliberate demonstration of the continent's ability to finance its own artificial intelligence infrastructure. The real question remains open: the sovereignty of the model depends as much on the compute it runs on as on the identity of the shareholders.* For French companies evaluating their AI options, Mistral's rise as a fully funded player concretely changes the make-or-buy equation. ## Greenly acquires Normative and Watershed: Regulatory consolidation accelerates **Greenly** simultaneously announces two acquisitions: **Normative**, a Swedish carbon footprint player valued in an operation described at **€75 million**, and **Watershed**, a British player in the sector (amount not disclosed, operation valued at approximately **€52 million** according to sources). This brings Greenly to over 4,000 clients in more than 30 countries. Both operations respond to the same logic: the CSRD directive will require some 50,000 European companies with more than 250 employees to publish a complete and audited carbon footprint by 2028. *What changes with the CSRD is the very nature of the market: the carbon footprint ceases to be a voluntary tool and becomes a legal, audited obligation. In this context, fragmentation — four or five different tools depending on subsidiaries, scopes, and standards — becomes a compliance risk, not just an operational inconvenience. Greenly is not buying competitors to eliminate them: it is buying emissions databases, scientific methodologies (Normative), and industrial integration teams (Watershed, 35 engineers). What the market will pay for tomorrow is the platform that covers Scope 1, 2, and 3 in a single auditable environment.* For CSR and CFOs of groups affected by the CSRD, market consolidation means that the tool choice made today will likely commit them for five years. ## Galeon refuses €60 million and raises €6 million: Refusal as a strategic signal **Galeon**, an Annecy-based publisher of AI-native patient records, refuses a **€60 million** acquisition offer and instead raises **€6 million** from its community of hospital pioneers and **BPI France**. The company equips 19 hospitals, employs about forty people, and aims for profitability by the end of 2027. *The refusal is the real information. At €60 million, the potential acquirer valued a niche publisher. By refusing, Galeon bets that the sovereignty of health data — HDS-certified hosting in France, beyond the reach of extra-European legislation — will become a decisive selection criterion for public hospitals, and that this sovereignty premium is not yet integrated into the offered price. The €6 million raised from the hospitals themselves — clients who become shareholders — creates a community of interests that complicates any unwanted future acquisition.* This is a form of capital defense as much as growth financing. ## Arlequin AI raises €28 million in Series A: Topology where large models fail **Arlequin AI** closes a **€28 million Series A**, co-led by the Polish fund **OTB Ventures** and **Redalpine**, with the participation of **BPI France**'s Defense Innovation Fund, **Xavier Niel**, and the accelerator **ZEBOX** (CMA CGM). The startup, founded in 2024, develops topological neural networks to detect hidden structures behind money laundering, terrorist financing, and disinformation. Where large language models analyze text and statistical patterns, Arlequin's technology maps networks of relationships — who funds whom, who communicates with whom, which seemingly unrelated entities share a common structure. The HuDEx platform is already commercially operating with governments and large organizations in Europe. *The funding by BPI's Defense Innovation Fund and the presence of CMA CGM (via ZEBOX) are two distinct signals: the first validates sovereign and governmental use, the second points to the fight against fraud in maritime logistics chains — a considerable market. A 50-person startup with 35 engineers and 15 PhD researchers, founded in 2024 and already in commercial deployment, is an execution pace that explains the interest of a fund the size of OTB for such an early-stage asset.* For financial compliance and security players, network topology is a blind spot that traditional tools do not cover — and it is precisely this gap that this fundraising finances. ## Wheere aims for €140 million for a geolocation constellation: Fundraising as a two-step process **Wheere**, a Montpellier-based spacetech specializing in indoor and outdoor satellite geolocation, announces its aim to raise **€140 million** by the end of 2028: €40 million in France in the second half of 2027, then €100 million in the United States. The goal: to orbit 65 satellites and address the mass market from 2030-2032. The company emerges from the International Space Summit with a first commercial contract for its terrestrial technology and a target of €1 million in revenue this year. *What is notable here is the transparency of the financing timeline and geography: the co-founder publicly announces that he is going to San Francisco to seek the missing €100 million after an initial French tranche. This is an admission as much as a strategy — the European space venture market is not yet sufficient to solely finance a constellation, even a modest one. Validation by the ecosystem (CNES, ESA, NASA, SpaceX) at the Paris summit is presented as a necessary prerequisite for fundraising, not a consequence.* For French investors looking at new space, Wheere illustrates the structural challenge: initial tranches are raised in Europe, but subsequent ones almost systematically require a pivot to American capital. ## JL&P raises €22 million in debt and acquires EGEH: Methodical external growth in engineering **JL&P**, a Gironde-based engineering and architecture group based in Floirac, raises **€22 million** in debt from a banking pool (Crédit Agricole, LCL, BNP Paribas, Crédit Mutuel), including €16 million in confirmed credit lines. Following this, the group announces the acquisition of **EGEH**, a specialist in polluted sites and soils, hydrogeology, and water law, with teams in Limoges, Floirac, Nantes, and Île-de-France. Amount not disclosed. JL&P has been supported by **Swen Capital Partners** and **Arkéa Capital** as minority shareholders since 2024. *The operation illustrates a disciplined regional build-up model: debt is raised before it is needed, and the confirmed credit line provides the ability to act quickly on subsequent targets. EGEH brings regulatory expertise (water law, polluted soils) that is becoming increasingly critical as environmental constraints on industrial and real estate projects intensify.* For mid-sized environmental engineering players, the signal is clear: consolidation is underway, and independent firms well-positioned in regulatory niches are attractive targets. ## Accel-KKR acquires Eleco for £207.6 million: The exit premium from the listed market **Accel-KKR** launches a takeover bid for **Eleco**, a British group listed on AIM specializing in software for construction, for **£207.6 million** (~**€243 million**), representing a premium of 74.7% over the last closing price before the announcement and 89.9% over the weighted average of the last six months. The operation is fully financed by Accel-KKR's equity, without debt. *The 75% premium is the figure worth noting. It does not mean that Eleco was undervalued: it means that the listed market applied a structural discount to this type of asset — niche vertical software, low liquidity, the construction market perceived as cyclical. Accel-KKR pays 20 times EBITDA for mission-critical software whose customers do not easily switch solutions. By taking Eleco private, the fund buys the freedom to invest in growth without having to justify a margin trajectory to dispersed shareholders every quarter.* The take-private here is less a conviction about Eleco than an arbitrage between two valuation markets: private equity pays more, but it buys time. ## Main Capital Partners acquires Tribal Group for £189.3 million: Educational software as a sovereign asset **Main Capital Partners**, a software-focused fund managing approximately €12 billion in assets, acquires **Tribal Group**, a British publisher of mission-critical software for international higher education, for approximately **£189.3 million** (~**€221 million**). Tribal was listed on AIM. The operation takes the form of an asset sale requiring shareholder vote. Tribal provides information systems to universities and educational institutions internationally — tools on which institutions rely to manage registrations, results, and regulatory compliance. *Second listed software take-private in one day, same underlying logic: an asset whose value lies in criticality and customer stickiness — a university does not change its information system from one year to the next — but whose listed market cannot adequately value recurrence. Main Capital specializes in B2B software in Europe; for them, Tribal is a building block in a broader thesis of consolidating vertical tools for higher education.* The signal for IT departments of affected institutions: their supplier will now operate under the pressure of a five-to-seven-year valuation horizon, with a growth and margin trajectory to maintain. ## Hardloop: Founders regain majority via a secondary LBO **Hardloop**, an e-commerce platform for outdoor equipment based in Annecy, welcomes **iX Private Equity** to its capital in an operation that allows co-founders Guillaume Richard and Julien Jérémie to increase their stake from 49.6% to nearly 60%. The operation coincides with the exit of 21 historical investors (Seventure, SGPA, Kima Ventures, angels). Amount not disclosed; the company is valued at approximately **€100 million** according to sources. Hardloop exceeds €40 million in revenue with 34% growth in 2026. *The structure is unusual: the founders emerge strengthened from a liquidity event, which is generally the opposite of what happens in a classic secondary LBO. iX Private Equity agreed to finance the liquidity of former investors while allowing the founders to take majority control — a sign that the investment thesis relies entirely on managerial continuity. In a consolidating outdoor market, Hardloop aims for €100 million in revenue in three years: the fund bets that the platform can double without changing its management team.* For entrepreneurs in the secondary LBO phase, this is a negotiation model to remember: continuity can be monetized in terms of governance. ## Francisco Partners and KKR take minority stakes in TeamSystem: "Private IPO" as a substitute exit **Francisco Partners** acquires approximately 10% of **TeamSystem**, an Italian publisher of management software (accounting, payroll, ERP) from **Hellman & Friedman**, while **KKR** and other investors take an additional 5%. The implied valuation of TeamSystem is between €8 billion and €10 billion. H&F retains the rest of its stake, transferred to a new fund. *The operation illustrates a mechanism that is spreading in Europe: the "private IPO," or how a fund returns capital to its LPs without going through public markets. In a context where IPOs are difficult for large software companies — markets have been shaken by fears about the impact of AI on valuations — H&F partially liquidates a €8-10 billion position without exposing TeamSystem to the volatility of a free float. Francisco Partners and KKR enter at a valuation that, at this level, implies a strong conviction about the ability of vertical management software to resist substitution by AI — or to integrate it as a growth driver.* For funds holding similar-sized software assets in Europe, TeamSystem validates that the private secondary market can absorb significant blocks without a major liquidity discount. ## HVR Energy raises €20 million for 75 hydrogen stations in Spain **HVR Energy**, a Spanish operator of hydrogen charging stations, closes a **€20 million Series A**, led by **Sandton** (€15 million), with **Barents Re** (€3 million) and **Langur** (€2 million). The pre-money valuation is €100 million. The goal: 75 stations in Spain by 2030, after a first opening in Coslada in 2023. Hydrogen for heavy mobility remains an infrastructure under construction: European AFIR and RED III regulations create a deployment obligation, but effective commercial demand depends on the speed at which hydrogen vehicle fleets develop. *HVR Energy's real risk is not financial but temporal: building the infrastructure before vehicles arrive is costly, but waiting for vehicles to arrive before building the infrastructure creates a circular blockage. The €20 million serves precisely to take this timing risk — betting that regulation will force demand before capital is exhausted.* For investors in alternative mobility, it's the same equation as with electric charging ten years ago, with a longer risk window. ## HeyDiga raises €5.5 million to automate inbound calls for SMEs **HeyDiga**, a Madrid-based startup founded in July 2025, closes a **€5.5 million seed round** led by **6 Degrees Capital** (London), with **K Fund**, **Italian Founders Fund**, and **Decelera**. The company deploys AI conversational agents on phone, WhatsApp, and chat for small and medium-sized enterprises, and claims 78% of conversations handled without human intervention for 200 clients. Founded fourteen months ago, HeyDiga has already raised approximately €6.5 million in total. Traction is real in specific verticals — automotive, catering — where the volume of inbound calls is high and the need for personalization is limited. *The addressed market is immense but very fragmented: convincing thousands of SMEs to adopt such a tool, one by one, is as much a distribution problem as a product problem.
