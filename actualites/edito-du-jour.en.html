Mediawan acquires Hello Sunshine: Hollywood on sale, but IP remains the real asset

Source: variety.com →

Mediawan is a content production and distribution group founded in Paris, which has built itself over the past ten years by aggregating independent production houses in Europe and, more recently, in the United States. Its American strategy relies on a unique formula: acquiring companies led by recognized talents and integrating them into its capital by bringing these talents in as shareholders — Brad Pitt via Plan B Entertainment in 2022, Peter Chernin via North Road earlier this year, Margot Robbie via LuckyChap International last November. Hello Sunshine, Reese Witherspoon's company, is behind the series Big Little Lies and Lucky, and was built around a model that combines narrative production, social media presence, and a book club. Mediawan is acquiring 100% of it from Candle Media, the vehicle founded by former Disney executives Kevin Mayer and Tom Staggs.

The central fact: Mediawan paid « in the low tens of millions of dollars », according to Variety — a fraction of the valuation of 900 million dollars at which Blackstone-backed Candle Media had acquired a majority stake in 2021. Reese Witherspoon is joining Mediawan's capital, alongside Pitt, Chernin, and the other producers who are already shareholders.

The obvious interpretation is that of a circumstantial bargain: streaming platforms have become more selective, content budgets are contracting, and independent American producers are facing downward pressure on their valuations. Candle Media was selling an asset overvalued at the peak of streaming.

But what is actually happening is more structural: Mediawan is not buying a brand at a low price, it is buying a relationship. Each acquisition in this portfolio follows the same pattern — the talent joins the capital, stays, and produces. This is not a catalog consolidation; it is the construction of a network of creators who have an interest in Mediawan's success. The 2021 valuation was inflated by the promise of a « talent-as-media-brand » model that platforms were funding without limit; the 2026 valuation reflects the reality of a market where IP and the relationship with talent take precedence over social notoriety. Mediawan is buying IP and talent loyalty for a fraction of the price of a bygone era. For a European group that wants to make an impact in Hollywood without the resources of a studio, this is a defensible position: not to produce in volume, but to control relationships with the most sought-after creators, in a market that is refocusing on quality and originality.

Legrand acquires Girtz Industries: American bolt-on in building electrical systems

Legrand, a French group specializing in electrical and digital building infrastructure, announces the acquisition of Girtz Industries, presented as an American specialist. The amount is not disclosed. The operation is part of what Legrand itself describes as its « active and disciplined bolt-on acquisition strategy ».

Available sources do not describe Girtz Industries' precise business beyond the « American specialist » qualifier. Without further context on the target's products, customers, or markets, any detailed strategic interpretation would remain a projection. What is established: Legrand continues to fuel its external growth in the United States, a market where the group is already firmly established and where it regularly seeks to complement its solution portfolio through targeted acquisitions.

Google / RAISE: Free access for European researchers — philanthropy or soft enclosure?

Source: tech-insider.org →

📬 Subscribe to the Developer & IT Infrastructure newsletter

The program RAISE (Resource for AI Science in Europe), launched by the European Commission on October 16, 2026, in Dublin, opens free access to five models of Google DeepMind and Google Cloud — AlphaFold 3, AlphaGenome, AlphaEvolve, AlphaEarth Foundations, and WeatherNext — to 200 European researchers selected by the Commission. This is not an M&A operation in the strict sense; no financial transaction is announced, no amount is circulating. The operation is included here because its logic deserves to be examined.

The surface interpretation: Google contributes to European scientific research, accelerates work in biology, genomics, climatology, and meteorology, and supports the continent's scientific competitiveness.

The less comfortable interpretation: offering free access to tools whose value lies in their use is to create dependence before monetizing it. The 200 researchers who will build their research pipelines on AlphaFold 3 or WeatherNext will work in an environment where Google controls evolution, updates, and future access conditions. Brussels, which has been trying for several years to build digital sovereignty, is here funding the integration of its own researchers into the infrastructure of an American player — at the precise moment when it recognizes, according to Politico, that it has given up on competing head-on with leading American and Chinese models. This is not a criticism of the decision — Google DeepMind's models are among the most powerful available for scientific research — but an invitation to read « generosity » as a positioning strategy: whoever provides the research infrastructure durably influences where that research is deployed, and into what ecosystem its results are integrated.

Hadrian: $40 million for AI to attack before hackers do

Source: businesswire.com →

Hadrian is an offensive security platform based on agentic AI — in other words, autonomous agents that simulate attacks against an organization's information systems to identify vulnerabilities before a real attacker exploits them. The company announces a fundraising of 40 million dollars (~€37M), co-led by investors whose names are not specified in available sources.

The addressed market is proactive cybersecurity: rather than detecting intrusions after the fact, Hadrian seeks to automate the work of the « red team » — the teams that continuously test the resilience of defenses. This type of approach, long reserved for large companies that could afford specialized teams, becomes accessible to less well-resourced organizations once it is automated.

What is interesting about this positioning: the value does not come from detection (a crowded market), but from large-scale, continuous attack simulation. An AI agent that « thinks like an attacker » and operates twenty-four hours a day changes the economics of security testing — not by replacing human experts, but by making their work possible at a frequency and depth that human factors alone could not achieve. For French companies managing critical infrastructure or sensitive data, the model deserves attention: the cost of a major security incident always exceeds that of preventive detection.

Causa Prima: $20 million to automate B2B invoice dispute resolution

Source: ground.news →

📬 Subscribe to the FinTech newsletter

Causa Prima is a fintech incorporated in Delaware that has developed a platform of AI agents capable of autonomously resolving B2B invoice disputes and negotiating early payments between buyers and suppliers. It announces a fundraising of 20 million dollars (~€18M) in seed, with the ambition to expand its operations in the United States and deploy its « agent-to-agent » network.

The problem it addresses is concrete and massive: in B2B commercial relationships, invoicing disputes — amount errors, disagreements on terms, contested deadlines — mobilize entire teams on both sides, slow down payments, and degrade supplier relationships. Causa Prima proposes that two AI agents, one on the buyer's side and one on the supplier's side, directly negotiate resolution without systematic human intervention.

The « agent-to-agent » model is a new architecture in fintech: it is no longer about automating a human task within an organization, but about creating a negotiation protocol between two systems belonging to distinct entities. The question of trust and auditability of these automated exchanges will be crucial for adoption — especially in regulated environments or in sectors where the traceability of commercial agreements is critical. For financial departments and credit managers of large French companies, this is a signal about the direction that accounts receivable automation is taking: no longer just reminders, but negotiation itself.
