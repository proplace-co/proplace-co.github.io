ABB acquires Ziehl-Abegg for $2.2 billion: energy efficiency as a core business

Source: capital.fr →

ABB is the Swedish-Swiss electrification and industrial automation group, present in energy, robotics, and infrastructure. Ziehl-Abegg is a German manufacturer of electric motors, fans, and control systems for industrial applications — building ventilation, data centers, electric vehicles, cooling systems. The company is family-owned, based in Künzelsau, Baden-Württemberg.

ABB is paying $2.2 billion to acquire this asset.

Ziehl-Abegg occupies a high-value technical niche in high-energy-efficiency ventilation — a segment that directly benefits from European regulations on building energy consumption and the explosion in demand for data center cooling. For ABB, which seeks to strengthen its positions in electrification and energy management, the acquisition brings motor and control technology that the group did not possess in-house at this level of specialization.

Ziehl-Abegg is therefore, indirectly, a supplier to AI infrastructure. ABB is acquiring it as this demand accelerates, before the valuation of this positioning is fully visible in market prices. For a French industrialist considering its component suppliers in the value chain of commercial buildings or data centers, this acquisition signals that high-efficiency ventilation equipment will fall within the scope of a large group, with the usual implications for prices, lead times, and availability.

Vår Energi acquires ConocoPhillips' Norwegian assets for $4.4 billion: the North Sea re-fragments

Source: gospodarkamorska.pl →

Vår Energi is a listed Norwegian oil and gas company, majority-owned by Eni and the Norwegian sovereign wealth fund via Equinor and other institutional shareholders. It operates several fields in the North Sea. ConocoPhillips is one of the major American independent oil and gas companies, present in Norway for decades — notably through the operatorship of the Greater Ekofisk complex, one of the oldest and most productive basins in the North Sea.

The transaction, valued at $4.4 billion, transfers ConocoPhillips' entire Norwegian portfolio to Vår Energi, including the operatorship of Greater Ekofisk, as well as certain British infrastructure.

The surface reading: a major American independent divests mature assets to recycle capital towards its strategic priorities — likely American LNG and shale assets, where margins and flexibility are greater. On the buyer's side, Vår Energi consolidates its position in a basin it knows well.

What deserves attention: ConocoPhillips is specifically divesting the operatorship of Ekofisk — meaning the operational responsibility for a complex that requires heavy engineering and a permanent local presence. This type of asset is difficult to value for an American player whose center of gravity is elsewhere; for Vår Energi, anchored in Norway with teams already in place, the integration cost is structurally lower. The acquisition is not just an addition of barrels: it is a consolidation of operational expertise in a basin where proximity to regulators, unions, and local suppliers matters as much as geology. For French capital exposed to offshore energy, this signals that the North Sea is entering a phase of consolidation among regional players — large American groups are arbitrating their geographical portfolios, and European operators are acquiring assets provided they have the critical size to operate them.

Cylib acquires BASF's battery recycling business: a startup takes over from a giant

Source: chemanager-online.com →

📬 Subscribe to the Climate & Energy Tech newsletter

Cylib is a startup founded in Aachen, specializing in lithium-ion battery recycling.

Cylib is acquiring this business, including the Schwarzheide plant, for an undisclosed amount.

The black mass contains lithium, cobalt, nickel, and manganese — strategic raw materials whose supply is geopolitically sensitive. BASF, which had invested in this sector, appears to have decided to focus on other priorities; Cylib thus acquires an operational industrial infrastructure that the startup could not have built alone at this scale and within this timeframe.

What is striking about this operation is the direction of the transfer: a startup is acquiring an industrial line from a large chemical group. This is not the usual inverse. BASF is divesting because battery recycling, at this stage of market maturity, requires specialization and operational agility that large diversified groups struggle to maintain against focused players. For Cylib, it's a considerable industrial shortcut — years of ramp-up avoided. For a French investor or industrialist in the battery value chain, this is a signal that recycling assets will quickly consolidate in the hands of specialists, and that the window to acquire or partner with these players is closing.

The precise details of Agon's product or service are not established in the available sources, beyond the «defense» label.

Their entry into a defense startup in Series A, at a valuation of $450 million, reflects a fundamental shift in European venture: defense has become an acceptable — even sought-after — asset class for funds that structurally avoided it just three years ago. For a French institutional investor considering its exposure to the sector, this is a signal of the normalization of private capital in European defense tech.
