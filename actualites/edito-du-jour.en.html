ACG Metals acquires Nussir copper mine: a bet on strategic European copper

Source: bebeez.eu →

📬 Subscribe to the Construction & PropTech newsletter

ACG Metals Limited (LSE: ACG), listed in London, announces the acquisition of 100% of Blue Moon Norway AS from Blue Moon Metals Inc., including its 94.52% stake in the Nussir copper mine, currently under construction in Norway, for 220 million dollars paid in shares. The transaction creates a combined entity with two long-life assets: the Gediktepe mine in Turkey, scheduled to ramp up in 2027, and Nussir, with first copper concentrate production expected in the first half of 2028.

Nussir is a fully permitted project, designated a strategic European project under the Critical Raw Materials Act. Its expected production between 2029 and 2033 is approximately 18,400 tonnes of copper equivalent per year, bringing the combined production of the new entity to over 50,000 tonnes per year. Approximately $84 million of initial capex has already been spent; $126 million remains to be financed, for which ACG is exploring several options including refinancing existing bonds.

Copper is at the heart of the energy transition — electrical wiring, charging stations, wind turbines, data centers. Nussir's designation as a “strategic European project” is not just an administrative label: it opens access to sovereign funding and European industrial buyers keen to secure their supply outside circuits dominated by non-Western players. For a French investor or industrialist exposed to the electrification value chain, this is the type of asset whose scarcity will increase.

🔮 What ACG might buy next

  • Bluelake Mineral — ACG would seek to strengthen its pipeline of strategic copper projects in Northern Europe, extending its recent acquisition.
  • Aurubis — ACG would complement its mining production and secure the European value chain through its expertise in copper refining and recycling.

Lonza acquires five US sites from Merck KGaA: a trump card in the CDMO war

Source: lonza.com →

📬 Subscribe to the Industrial Tech & Manufacturing newsletter

Lonza, a Swiss contract pharmaceutical manufacturing (CDMO) group, announces the acquisition of five US production sites from Merck KGaA (Darmstadt, Germany) for an enterprise value in the low to mid-hundreds of millions of Swiss francs. The transaction expands Lonza's US footprint from four to nine sites, with a local workforce now exceeding 6,000 people — more than double what it was before the deal.

The acquired sites cover high-growth segments: antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs), viral vectors for cell and gene therapies, and advanced drug delivery technologies. These are precisely the modalities driving demand in oncology and precision medicine, areas where major pharmaceutical companies are massively outsourcing due to a lack of in-house capacity.

By doubling its footprint in this market, Lonza is not just growing — it is making its position structurally more difficult to challenge for anyone looking to compete for a major American pharma tender. For a European laboratory or a mid-sized CDMO observing this move, the message is clear: sector consolidation is accelerating, and the window to build comparable scale is closing.

🔮 What Lonza might buy next

  • Corden Pharma — Lonza would seek to consolidate its CDMO position; this acquisition would strengthen its API and advanced therapies offering.
  • Curia — Lonza would consolidate its American positioning through its expertise in R&D and integrated manufacturing, particularly in advanced therapies.

Terex acquires Keestrack: crushing and screening enter the portfolio

Source: acpresse.fr →

Terex, an American construction equipment manufacturer specializing in lifting and material handling equipment, announces the acquisition of Belgian company Keestrack, a historical manufacturer of aggregate crushing, screening, and recycling machinery. Keestrack will retain its operational autonomy and current management — co-founders Peter and Frederik Hoogendoorn will remain in their positions.

Keestrack holds an established position in the material demolition and recycling segment, with a range of mobile crushers and screens and an existing sales presence in Europe. For Terex, which has its own aggregates business, the acquisition represents a direct entry into the construction material recycling market, which is rapidly expanding due to European regulations on construction and demolition waste.

Maintaining Keestrack as an independent subsidiary is a reasonable integration choice for an asset whose value partly relies on close commercial relationships with European clients — too rapid an integration could risk eroding precisely what the acquirer paid for.

🔮 What Terex might buy next

  • AUMUND Group — Terex would seek to expand its bulk material handling and storage offering, complementing its recent acquisition.
  • Loesche GmbH — Terex would complete its aggregates portfolio through its expertise in vertical mills and turnkey plants.

CrowdStrike swallows Fiddler AI: the “black box” of agents enters the cybersecurity platform

Source: it.investing.com →

CrowdStrike (NASDAQ: CRWD), a US-based publisher of the Falcon cybersecurity platform, signs a definitive agreement to acquire Fiddler AI, specializing in observability and explainability for artificial intelligence agents. The price will be paid primarily in cash, with a portion in equity subject to vesting conditions. Closing remains subject to customary conditions.

Fiddler AI was built to answer a question that companies deploying AI agents don't yet know how to ask: what exactly is this agent doing, and why did it make that decision? The technology allows tracing agent reasoning, detecting behavioral drifts and hallucinations in production, and documenting decisions for regulatory purposes. It will be integrated into the Falcon platform under the name Falcon Observe.

The announcement is made at Fal.Con Europe 2026.

This acquisition says something important about the state of the enterprise AI market: the question is no longer whether AI agents will be deployed, but how to govern them. CrowdStrike, whose core business is trust and visibility into IT systems, logically extends this positioning to autonomous agents. For a French CIO or CISO managing an AI agent deployment, integrating this capability into an already installed security platform — rather than a third-party tool — changes the purchasing equation.

🔮 What CrowdStrike might buy next

  • Anetac — CrowdStrike would seek to extend its AI governance beyond observability, by securing AI and non-human identities, following the acquisition.
  • Koi — CrowdStrike would extend its ability to govern AI agents, complementing Fiddler AI's observability, through its agentic security for endpoints.

HRV Pharma enters Europe with Phatebo: a bridgehead in clinical trial supply

Source: moneycontrol.com →

📬 Subscribe to the Biotech & Pharma newsletter

HRV Pharma, a pharmaceutical group based in Hyderabad (India) specializing in active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs), announces the acquisition of Phatebo GmbH, a German company founded in 2015 in Hilzingen, specializing in the supply of investigational medicinal products, comparators, and ancillary supplies used in clinical trials. Phatebo generated approximately 350 crore rupees in revenue in 2025. The financial terms of the transaction are not disclosed.

This acquisition marks HRV's first operational establishment in finished formulations in Europe. HRV has so far operated on a factory-less model, relying on a network of over 50 FDA and EU-GMP certified manufacturing partners, and serves clients in 55 countries. Phatebo brings an authorized European platform, established GDP systems, and relationships with pharmaceutical sponsors and CROs.

The strategic interest is clear: by combining its API capabilities with a European clinical distribution platform, HRV positions itself across the entire clinical trial supply chain — a market where logistical and regulatory complexity creates a lasting barrier to entry.

Wormsensing raises €9.5 million to industrialize predictive maintenance with vibratory sensors

Source: actutem.com →

Wormsensing, a Grenoble-based company stemming from CEA-Leti's work, develops piezoelectric sensors named Dragonfly designed to measure dynamic deformations directly on industrial equipment. Attached to machines, they detect vibrations and mechanical variations to anticipate wear, friction, and process drifts before they cause failure or production shutdown. The company targets predictive maintenance, process control, and quality control, primarily in the automotive and aerospace industries.

Wormsensing closes a Series A round of 9.5 million euros, led by Orano Venture Fund (managed by Supernova Invest), with participation from the French Tech Seed (Bpifrance, France 2030) fund, the Fonds Régional Avenir Industrie Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes and historical investors Innovacom, KBC Focus Fund and CEA Investissement.

The company already has its own production line (Hyperion, up to two million sensors per year), a portfolio of 50 patents, and a first fundraising of €3.5 million in 2022. The funds will finance international commercial development and the expansion of its offering to digital services associated with the sensors.

The presence of Orano Venture Fund as lead is a sectoral signal: nuclear and high-reliability industries are natural clients for a technology that promises to reduce unplanned machine downtime by 60 to 99%. For a French industrialist evaluating its predictive maintenance options, Wormsensing represents a sovereign alternative to imported solutions.

Viridi (officially ViridiCO2), a spin-off from the University of Southampton founded in 2020, has developed a catalyst enabling factories to transform recycled carbon dioxide into surfactants and other chemical components usable in common products — an alternative to conventional petrochemical processes. The company closes a round of 2.6 million euros (2.2 million pounds sterling), led by Odyssey Ventures, with co-participation fromAlphatron Impact Ventures, historical investors, and TOP Ventures, the corporate arm of Thai oil group Thaioil, which enters as a strategic investor.

The entry of an industrial oil player as a strategic investor at this early stage is a validation of technological credibility — and a gateway to industrial clients who have every interest in diversifying their sources of chemical raw materials before regulatory pressure compels them to do so.

Unitoq raises €155,000 in pre-seed funding to fractionalize European real estate from Riga

Source: seedtable.com →

📬 Subscribe to the Construction & PropTech newsletter

Unitoq, a Latvian proptech founded in 2026 and based in Riga, offers private investors the opportunity to buy fractions of income-generating European real estate properties starting from €100. The company raises 155,000 euros (approximately $174,000) in pre-seed funding from a syndicate of business angels including members of the Latvian Business Angel Network.

Unitoq is still in the waitlist phase for initial access — this funding buys time for regulatory work and a first cohort of properties rather than for growth. The fractional ownership market is crowded, and its main obstacle is not technological: it lies in the cross-border regulatory complexity of protecting private investors and the absence of a liquid secondary market for property fractions. The choice to start from Latvia — an EU-passportable jurisdiction with low licensing costs — is consistent for the first barrier; it solves nothing for the second.
