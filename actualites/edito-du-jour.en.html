Belvedia acquires Acto RH and crosses the 130-agency threshold

Belvedia is a network of business services agencies — including the COPYTOPbrand, active for fifty years in printing, communication, and local consulting in city centers, in daily contact with large groups, SMEs, startups, and liberal professions throughout France. Acto RH is a human resources services structure whose specific activity is not detailed in the available sources. The acquisition, the amount of which was not disclosed, brings the Belvedia network to 130 agencies.

Strategic reading: in local service networks, territorial density is the main asset — it determines commercial capillarity, cost mutualization, and the ability to respond to national calls for tenders. Crossing a symbolic threshold like 130 agencies is not insignificant: it's the kind of size that changes the nature of the interlocutors one can address on the key accounts side.

DYAMEO: verifying in the operating room that the tumor is completely removed

Source: presse.bpifrance.fr →

DYAMEO, a French medtech founded in 2023, develops devices to help surgeons control in real-time, during the operation itself, that resection margins are clear — meaning no tumor cells remain in the surrounding tissues. Today, this confirmation often only comes after the intervention, via delayed anatomopathological analysis, which forces some patients into heavy post-operative treatments or re-intervention.

The solution relies on three combined elements: patented biological reagent capsules that clip onto a probe and react upon contact with cancer cells, an optical fiber probe that the surgeon applies to the surgical specimen, and TempoTouch®, an instant analysis system that displays a readable result in the operating room. The technology integrates into existing practices without modifying the surgical gesture.

DYAMEO announces a fundraising of 3 million euros. Historical investors Medevice Capital and NACO renew their participation; Bpifrance via its Amorçage Industriel fund, OBRATORI and Estellior Capital join the round, alongside French entrepreneurs. The stated objective for 2026 is to obtain CE marking and commercial launch in operating theaters, after a first clinical trial conducted in six centers of excellence in France with results deemed conclusive.

This 3 million euro round is a regulatory threshold-crossing round, not a growth round: the money finances CE marking, the mandatory gateway to the European market. The main risk is no longer scientific — the clinical trial is conclusive — it is operational and commercial: transforming clinical proof into actual adoption in operating theaters where surgeon time is the most constrained asset.

Machenta: teaching a robot in a single demonstration — and raising 37 million euros to prove it

Source: aifundingtracker.com →

📬 Subscribe to the Industrial Tech & Manufacturing newsletter

Most industrial robots learn a task after hundreds, sometimes thousands of demonstrations. Reprogramming an arm for a new operation takes days and mobilizes teams of engineers. Machenta, an Imperial College London spin-out founded in 2025 by Edward Johns — professor of robot learning — and Vitalis Vosylius, has developed an AI model that allows a robot to reproduce a task after a single demonstration, without any real data: training is done entirely in simulation. The same model has already learned packaging, assembly, and tidying tasks on several types of robots, including humanoids.

Machenta has eleven employees.

The surface reading is that of a research bet: an exceptional academic team, a well-posed problem, and leading investors who are betting early. But what deserves attention is the very structure of the model. Training solely on simulated data — without real-world images — is not just a choice of confidentiality: it's a thesis on generalization. If the model holds, it can adapt to any production environment without on-site data recollection, which would erase one of the main hidden costs of robotic deployment in factories.

A technical detail worth noting: documents filed with Companies House recorded preferred shares for approximately £22.7 million at the end of September, a notable discrepancy with the announced $40 million. The reason has not been publicly confirmed. This is not necessarily a negative signal — the filing schedule and effective closing can diverge — but it is a point to verify for anyone considering following the company.

For a French industrialist looking to automate low-volume or high-variability lines — agri-food, cosmetics, equipment — the promise of a robot reprogrammable in one demonstration radically changes the profitability calculation. To be watched closely as soon as the first commercial deployments are documented.
