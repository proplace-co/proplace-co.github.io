Aurelius takes over Resonac's graphite division: the electrode as an industrial transition asset

Source: delano.lu →

Aurelius, an investment fund specializing in the acquisition and transformation of complex businesses, is set to acquire the graphite division of Resonac, a Japanese industrial group. The divested business manufactures graphite electrodes used in electric arc furnaces in steel mills — an essential industrial consumable for steel production via electric arc, a cleaner process than the traditional blast furnace. The division employs approximately 900 people and generated 500 million dollars in revenue in 2025. It operates from sites in A Coruña (Spain), Ridgeville (United States), and Steeg (Austria). The transaction also includes AMI Automation, a specialist in electric furnace optimization. The price was not disclosed. Closing is expected in the second quarter of 2027. The assets and employees of the Omachi site in Japan will remain with Resonac, under a long-term exclusive supply agreement.

This is Aurelius's sixth acquisition for its Opportunities V fund, and the second transaction with Resonac after the acquisition of FIAMM the previous year — confirming an established relationship of trust between the fund and the Japanese group for the divestment of complex international assets.

The graphite electrode for electric furnaces is a discreet but structurally solid asset: demand is driven by the development of recycled steel via electric arc, a process gaining momentum in the European industrial transition. This is not battery graphite — it's the one that melts scrap metal. Aurelius is buying a position in an industrial supply chain with high barriers to entry (complex manufacturing technology, certifications, long-standing customer relationships), with structural demand that steel decarbonization will only accentuate. For French or European capital looking at industrial transition assets, this type of carve-out from a Japanese conglomerate — a profitable asset but non-strategic for the seller, solid for a specialized buyer — is a model to observe.

🔮 What Aurelius might buy next

  • SGL Carbon — Aurelius would seek to diversify its carbon materials portfolio beyond electrodes, thereby strengthening its position in the industrial transition.
  • GrafTech International Ltd. — Aurelius would seek to strengthen its strategic positioning in graphite electrodes post-Resonac acquisition through vertical integration and a global presence.

Global absorbs Alight Media: outdoor advertising becomes a programmatic advertising platform

Source: invidis.de →

Global, a British media and entertainment group, announced on November 9 the acquisition ofAlight Media, a British outdoor advertising network founded in 2019 by Matthew Dearden and Dave Huckerby. The price was not disclosed. Closing is expected in the first quarter of 2027, subject to regulatory approval.

Global has been active in outdoor advertising since 2018 and notably manages advertising spaces for the Transport for London network. The acquisition of Alight geographically complements this existing network.

What distinguishes this deal from a simple panel consolidation is the technological intent: Global wants to deploy its own tools on the Alight network — the Dax programmatic platform, the Global IQ planning and measurement tools, the Adpower self-service system for smaller advertisers. In other words, Global is not buying locations: it is buying inventory that it will transform into programmatically addressable surface, to compete with Google and Meta on the ground of measurement and automation. Outdoor advertising remains one of the few media that cannot be blocked, skipped, or ignored by a feed algorithm. Making this inventory as easy to buy as a digital banner is the strategic gamble of the deal.

🔮 What Global might buy next

  • i-media — Global would seek to expand its digital and programmatic outdoor advertising network into strategic locations across the UK.
  • Ocean Outdoor UK — Global would seek to complement and expand its programmatic inventory with a premium DOOH network in the UK and Europe, capitalizing on the Alight acquisition.

Grupo Tirant acquires elDigesto: consolidating Ibero-American law, one country at a time

The strategy is clear: build comprehensive coverage of Latin America market by market, by acquiring the most relevant local tools to integrate them into a proprietary platform that combines documentary resources, AI, and management.

For a European legal tech player looking at Latin America, Tirant is executing the classic playbook of specialized editorial consolidation in the digital age: acquiring local knowledge (documentary base, network of lawyers, understanding of national law) before large generalist language models commoditize it. The value is not in the AI itself — it is in national legal data and in the trust of local practitioners. This is a combination that cannot be replicated from the outside.

PostScriptum takes control of Brillian: building a European AI integrator through consolidation

Source: epressi.com →

PostScriptum, the family office of AI entrepreneur Peter Sarlin, has acquired a majority stake in Brillian, a Finnish company founded in 2024 by Tarmo Pajunen, Tatu Hautala, and Jussi Järvinen. Brillian delivers end-to-end AI implementation missions — from strategy to operational integration — and has two hubs in Finland, with predominantly international activity (Siemens, Storytel, Carnival Cruise Lines are among its strategic partners). The founders, employees, and existing shareholders retain a significant minority. The amount was not disclosed.

Sarlin's bet is precise: while the race for models concentrates attention and capital, the real value of AI in businesses is created in implementation — the ability to transform a model into a real operational process. Brillian is positioned in this segment, with international credibility from its inception. PostScriptum brings the experience of scaling a European AI player internationally. This is a consolidation thesis: acquire the best European integrators before large consulting firms or American IT service companies structure this market to their advantage.

Gama raises €15M in seed: Europe acquires a supply chain for space solar panels

Source: payloadspace.com →

📬 Subscribe to the Climate & Energy Tech newsletter

Gama, a French startup developing ultra-light deployable solar panels for satellites, has closed a 15 million euro seed round. The round brought together Transition Ventures, Planet A, Draper Associates and Manta Ray. The funds will be used to build a high-rate production facility in the Paris region, capable of producing 100 MW of solar panels annually, and to recruit approximately 60 engineers (mechanical, electrical, software). The first units — called Wings — are expected to be in production next year. Gama signed in September a partnership with American satellite manufacturer Eclipse Space for the supply of deployable panels.

Gama began by developing solar sails for deep space probe propulsion, before pivoting to solar panels for satellites — a market structurally constrained by a small number of global suppliers and lead times that can exceed one year. Gama's value proposition is based on an inverse approach to the market: making panels as thin and light as possible to maximize the collecting surface, then transferring stabilization complexity to the software side — integrated into the client satellites' onboard computers — rather than adding mechanical mass. The cost target is $10 per watt.

What makes this round notable beyond its amount — one of the largest seed rounds for a European space startup according to sources — is what it actually funds: not just another demonstrator, but industrial capacity. 100 MW annually is a factory, not a laboratory. Europe has long produced brilliant space technologies without ever building the supply chains to deploy them at scale. Gama is betting that demand for satellites — LEO constellations, defense satellites, institutional platforms — will grow faster than the global capacity to power them. If this bet is right, the value is not in the panel itself, but in the position of an indispensable supplier of critical infrastructure.

Twin1 raises $5M in seed extension: AI that preserves institutional memory for regulated enterprises

Source: dealroom.co →

📬 Subscribe to the FinTech newsletter

Twin1, a company based in San Mateo and London, develops AI-powered digital twins for enterprise professionals: each employee is associated with an AI agent fed by their emails, meetings, documents, and work systems, with the goal of preserving and sharing institutional knowledge while maintaining confidentiality, permissions, and human control. The company has raised 5 million dollars in a seed extension, bringing its total seed funding to 25 million dollars. The round was led by Curql, a fund backed by over 160 US credit unions. Greyhair Ventures and KittyHawk Frontier participated, as well as angels including Bin Lin (co-founder of Xiaomi), Fan Li (early Google engineer), and Raj Bector (senior partner at McKinsey). Twin1 is already working with Shoreline Credit Union. The funds will finance deployments in credit unions, law firms, and financial institutions, as well as recruitment in San Mateo and London, and the development of governance controls and the « Twin Network » enabling agents to coordinate work across an organization.

The presence of Curql as a lead investor is more than a validation signal: it's a direct distribution channel to over 160 credit unions. In regulated sectors, the primary obstacle to AI adoption is not technological — it's governance, traceability, and compliance. Twin1 has chosen to tackle this problem head-on rather than circumvent it. The real question is whether the promise — capturing knowledge that leaves with employees — stands up to real-world use in organizations where the most valuable knowledge is precisely that which is never written down anywhere.

Mondrio raises $3.75M: AI agents to link B2B pricing strategy and sales execution

Source: briefia.fr →

Mondrio, a Dutch startup founded by Gregor Biljardt and Joep van den Nieuwenhoff, develops an AI-assisted B2B pricing platform articulated in two modules: Pricing Studio, dedicated to developing and optimizing product offers, pricing schemes, and price tiers; and Deal Engine, which integrates pricing intelligence into every transaction — recommendations on quotes, discounts, negotiations, and renewals, with automation of quoting and approval tasks according to predefined rules. The platform continuously analyzes product evolutions, customer behavior, competitive data, and market signals to suggest pricing adjustments. Mondrio has raised 3.75 million dollars. The funds will finance recruitment in AI, engineering, and pricing, technical improvements, and commercial expansion into the United States, Europe, manufacturing, and professional services. Investors are not specified in the available sources.

B2B pricing remains one of the most costly blind spots for industrial and service companies: the gap between the pricing strategy defined in committee and what actually happens in commercial negotiations is often considerable. Mondrio attempts to close this loop in real time.

NeuralSat raises €2.9M in pre-seed: an orbital distributed data and computing network

Source: eu-startups.com →

NeuralSat, a German startup founded in 2026 in Würzburg by Mohd Bilal (CEO, embedded software expertise), Rishi Jaiswal (CTO, mechatronics), and Manohar Karnal (COO, thermal engineering), develops an integrated orbital infrastructure combining high-speed data relays and distributed computing between satellites. The founding observation: the vast majority of satellites are launched without inter-satellite networking capabilities, which prevents resource sharing between spacecraft and their seamless connection with ground systems. NeuralSat targets applications in emergency response, security, and critical missions requiring near real-time. The company has raised 2.9 million euros in pre-seed. The round was led by Capnamic Ventures and Bayern Kapital, with participation from ZOHO.VC, Robin Capital, BAB (Bremen development bank), KIEMIS Holding and Mission E Capital.

NeuralSat is part of a wave of European investments in satellite data infrastructure in 2026, alongside UNIVITY (€27M for space 5G connectivity) and SWISSto12 (€61M Series C) cited in the sectoral context. At this pre-seed stage, the challenge is to validate technical feasibility before raising for constellation construction.
