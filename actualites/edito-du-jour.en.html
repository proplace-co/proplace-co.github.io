Oura acquires Counsel Health: the smart ring wants to become your primary care physician

Source: borskollen.se →

📬 Subscribe to the HealthTech & Digital Health newsletter

Oura, a Finnish company founded in Finland but now headquartered in San Francisco, designs smart rings that continuously measure heart rate, sleep, and physical activity. Until now, the device collected data; it prescribed nothing.

The stated goal is to integrate Counsel Health's services directly into the Oura app, so that a user whose ring detects an anomaly can consult a doctor without leaving the ecosystem. Biometric data becomes a potential prescription.

What the price says about the strategy: Oura, valued at $11 billion at the end of 2025 and whose IPO was postponed last September due to an unfavorable window, is spending the equivalent of more than 5% of its value to acquire not a competitor but a medical capability. This is a bet on regulation as much as on technology — if US health authorities ever validate a care pathway driven by a wearable sensor, Oura will already have the infrastructure. If they don't, this acquisition remains a very expensive option cost.

🔮 What Oura might acquire next

  • Myia Health — Oura could seek to expand its remote monitoring and home care management capabilities, capitalizing on its recent acquisition.
  • Biofourmis — Oura would expand its ability to manage care pathways through its remote monitoring and home care platform.

Germany acquires a €6.6 billion stake in KNDS

Source: realtid.se →

KNDS, a Franco-German group formed from the merger of Krauss-Maffei Wegmann and Nexter, notably manufactures the Leopard 2 tank, deployed in several European armies.

The stated rationale is political as much as industrial: Berlin wanted to have equal weight with Paris in the group's strategic decisions, and avoid being outvoted on export choices or program development.

This move illustrates a logic that goes beyond simple financial investment: European states are re-internalizing control of their defense industries as defense demand becomes structural again. For a defense industrialist or a fund investing in this sector, the signal is that strategic assets in this area tend to be locked up by sovereigns before reaching the market.

🔮 What Germany might acquire next

  • Arquus — Germany could seek to consolidate its position in armored ground vehicles by acquiring this company.
  • MBDA — Germany would complement its ground offering and strengthen vertical integration and European defense sovereignty through their expertise in missile systems.

Raenest acquires Remitise: an FCA license bought, not leased

Source: techpoint.africa →

📬 Subscribe to the FinTech newsletter

Raenest, a financial platform that allows individuals and businesses to send, receive, spend, and manage money internationally, acquires 100% of Remitise, a UK payment company registered with the Financial Conduct Authority. The amount is not disclosed.

Ondas acquires Stemme: a military drone disguised as a civil glider

Ondas, an American group whose Ondas Sentinel division develops military surveillance drones, acquires the entire capital of Stemme, a German motor glider manufacturer based in Strausberg. The price is not disclosed; closing is expected in the first quarter of 2027.

The industrial link is direct: the Ultra drone developed by Ondas Sentinel is built on the airframe of the certified Stemme S12 glider, already used by the US Air Force. Ondas aims for a production rate of ten Ultra aircraft per month by 2028, which requires securing and expanding manufacturing capabilities in Strausberg and Pasewalk, while also creating additional capabilities in the United States. Stemme will continue to sell the S12 to private customers and provide maintenance for its fleet.

Strategic interpretation: by integrating its airframe supplier, Ondas controls the bottleneck in its military production chain. The civil certification of the S12 is a regulatory asset that acquiring a drone startup would not have provided.

🔮 What Ondas might acquire next

  • Skydrone Robotics — Ondas would strengthen its military offering and secure production through its expertise in professional drone systems and control stations.

Vaeridion raises €124 million for a nine-seater electric aircraft

Source: avi-go.com →

Vaeridion, a Munich-based electric aviation startup, is developing the Microliner, a nine-passenger aircraft with a glider-inspired wing, multi-engine propulsion, and patented integrated modular batteries, targeting a range of 400 km in IFR configuration. The company announces total funding of over €124 million, consisting of €30 million in equity (Series A2, led by SHIFT Invest and Invest-NL, with World Fund, Project A, Vsquared Ventures and founder Alexander Rittweger) and a European grant of €94.5 million via the EU Innovation Fund for the CAROLUS project, intended to finance a final assembly line at Oberpfaffenhofen Airport. The signing of the grant agreement is expected in 2027.

Vaeridion claims provisional purchase commitments for over 130 aircraft worth more than €730 million, with ASL Group as the launch customer. KLM, BayernLB, and TrueNoord sit on its market advisory board. The company has completed its preliminary design review.

The funding structure reveals the true state of the sector: 76% of the announced envelope is public money, and the 130 orders are described as provisional agreements by Aviation International News, not firm orders. Battery energy density remains the unresolved technical bottleneck — a professor from Eindhoven University of Technology cited in the sources does not expect a commercial electric aircraft for 9 to 20 passengers before 2035. This round funds the demonstration, not yet the entry into service.

Sparxity: $90 million for chips that only compute what matters

Source: fr.finance.yahoo.com →

Sparxity, a German startup founded in Dresden, develops “sparse-native” computing systems: its chips and architectures are designed to activate only the parts of an AI model that are actually useful for each computation, rather than running the entire network continuously. The company closes a Series A round that brings its cumulative funding to $90 million. The round is led by ICM HPQC Fund, with participation from Lockheed Martin Ventures, GlobalFoundries, GS Futures, Cloudberry Ventures, TGFS and theEIC Fund, as well as business angels including Richard Socher and Nancy Xu.

Sparxity relies on three chip manufacturing cycles (“tapeouts”), over 70,000 chips produced, and 12 racks deployed at more than 30 customers, including supercomputing centers. Its revenue quadrupled between 2024 and 2025. The funding is intended to accelerate the development of specialized chips aiming for up to 44 times faster inference on ultra-sparse models.

The presence of Lockheed Martin Ventures and GlobalFoundries (semiconductor foundry) in the round is not insignificant: it signals that dynamic sparsity interests defense applications as much as commercial cloud, and that the manufacturing chain is already under discussion. For an industrial player looking to reduce its computing energy bill, Sparxity offers an architectural alternative to buying additional GPUs.

Tendrils raises €26 million to reinvent processor architecture

Source: eu-startups.com →

📬 Subscribe to The Physical World newsletter

Tendrils, a semiconductor startup founded in 2025 in Cambridge by Nils Cremer, is developing a new general-purpose processor architecture designed to run thousands of cores in parallel. The approach is based on “interaction nets,” a computational model Cremer studied at ETH Zurich. The company raises €26 million (approximately $30 million) in a Seed funding round, led by Benchmark with participation from Visionaries Club, the UK Sovereign AI Fund, FR8, Antimatter, Cloudberry VC, Conviction, Embassy Ventures and SV Angels. Jack Altman, partner at Benchmark, joins the board of directors.

Co-founder T6 developed Vine, an open-source programming language built around interaction nets; Hugo McNally contributed to OpenTitan at lowRISC.

This round comes in a context of several hundred million euros raised in 2026 by alternative computing architectures in Europe (OLIX, Optalysys, Callosum, Openchip, EUCLYD). The presence of the UK Sovereign AI Fund alongside Benchmark — a rare combination of public capital and top-tier American venture — signals that sovereignty over computing architectures is now an issue of industrial policy, not just market.

triPica raises €12.4M to modernize billing for energy and telecom providers

Source: shareware.fr →

📬 Subscribe to the B2B Software & Cloud newsletter

triPica, a Parisian software publisher founded in 2016, develops a cloud platform for customer relationship management and billing for energy suppliers and telecom operators. Its clients include Bouygues Telecom, Primeo Energie, Gaz Européen, Digicel, and CelcomDigi. The company raises €12.4 million from TiLT Capital Partners, an energy transition fund linked to Siparex. The funds will finance expansion in Germany and Italy, the development of the telecom offering, and the integration of AI into billing.

triPica's thesis is that the energy transition multiplies contract types — dynamic tariffs, self-consumption, electric vehicle charging — that historical billing systems of energy providers, designed for stable regulated tariffs, cannot quickly absorb. The promise is to reduce the time to launch a new tariff offer from several months to a few weeks.

The investor says as much as the amount: TiLT Capital is an energy transition fund, not a generalist SaaS fund. The fact that billing software is financed by an energy infrastructure fund confirms that the software layer has become a real bottleneck in the transition — and a strategic asset for those building the new energy offering.

Switchpoint Bio raises €4.75M to read epigenomic marks in blood

Source: eu-startups.com →

Switchpoint Bio, a startup founded in 2025 in Helsinki and Cambridge (Massachusetts) by Samuel Myllykangas and Jason Buenrostro — a Harvard researcher, director of the epigenomics program at the Broad Institute, and inventor of ATAC-seq — develops an epigenomic profiling platform from blood samples. The goal is to provide drug developers with a tool to measure how a disease and treatment modify human biology at the level of circulating immune cells, repeatedly and at scale. The company raises €4.75 million in pre-Seed funding, of which €4.48 million comes from Treehouse Delmar Investments.

Myllykangas previously founded and developed Blueprint Genetics, acquired by Quest Diagnostics in 2020. The founding team also includes experts in bioinformatics and computational biology.

The value proposition is precise: not to diagnose a disease, but to measure what changes in a patient's biology before, during, and after treatment — information that clinical trials currently struggle to capture systematically. If the platform delivers on its promise of scale, it targets pharmaceutical companies looking to understand why some patients respond and others do not.
