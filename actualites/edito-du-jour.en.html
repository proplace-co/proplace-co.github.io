Valsoft acquires Chalco and enters product content management for luxury

Source: valsoftcorp.com →

📬 Subscribe to the B2B Software & Cloud newsletter

Chalco is an Italian software platform that manages the complete lifecycle of product content and information — from design to e-commerce, including marketing, B2B and B2C sales, and digital archiving. Developed within Hyphen-Group for over twenty-five years, it serves luxury and retail brands in Italy and Switzerland. Valsoft Corporation, a Canadian group specializing in the acquisition and long-term development of vertical market software, acquired 100% of Chalco through its entity TAG Software Group, via a new structure, Chalco Technologies Srl. Hyphen-Group, rebranded as Hyphen-Integration, remains the exclusive partner for Chalco's distribution, consulting, and implementation in Italy and Switzerland. Stefano Righetti, who led Hyphen-Group, will now head Chalco Technologies.

The immediate takeaway: Valsoft adds a twenty-first vertical to its portfolio and enters a market it considers to be experiencing strong growth in the AI era.

What deserves attention is the exit structure: Hyphen-Group sells its product but retains exclusive distribution rights in its historical markets. The buyer acquires the intellectual property and the capacity for international expansion; the seller retains its local customer base. This is a surgical separation between technological assets and the commercial network — an arrangement that allows Valsoft to push Chalco into other geographies without destabilizing the existing customer base, and for Hyphen to monetize an asset it could not internationalize alone.

🔮 What Valsoft could buy next

  • Afineo — Valsoft could seek to expand its PIM/DAM offering with a no-code and AI platform, strengthening its luxury and retail positioning.
  • Pimland — Their platform unifies PLM, PIM, SRM, and DPP with AI.

Huisman acquires Future Production: offshore maintenance finds its integrator

Source: ocean-energyresources.com →

📬 Subscribe to the Climate & Energy Tech newsletter

Future Production is a Norwegian company of about forty people, based in Kristiansand, that designs and delivers modular solutions for the maintenance, modification, and operation of offshore platforms, as well as for renewable energies. Its model is deliberately lean: design and engineering are internalized, manufacturing is outsourced to a network of qualified suppliers. Since 1999, it has completed over 650 deliveries on more than 90 platforms worldwide. Its exiting shareholder is Norvestor, a Norwegian private equity fund. The acquirer is Huisman, a Dutch family-owned industrial group active in the design and manufacturing of heavy equipment for offshore and energy.

The surface reading: a classic bolt-on acquisition, a fund exits after supporting growth, an industrial player integrates complementary capabilities.

What's more interesting is the timing. Future Production maintained its teams through several years of depression in the rig market — a costly short-term decision that preserved rare expertise just as activity is picking up again. Huisman, which is developing its client relationships in Norway, is buying precisely this rarity: not physical assets (there are almost none), but engineering expertise and a network of contacts in recovering markets. For a long-term oriented family industrial acquirer, this is exactly the type of asset that cannot be quickly rebuilt — and which gains value in an upward cycle.

🔮 What Huisman could buy next

  • STAPEM Offshore — Huisman could seek to expand its expertise in offshore maintenance and renewable energies, capitalizing on the engineering acquired with Future Production.
  • Imeca — Huisman could seek to complement its modular offering post-acquisition of Future Production with their expertise in heavy and integrated marine handling systems.

Amplifon–GN Hearing: green light from Brussels, closing before end of 2026

Source: firstonline.info →

Amplifon, an Italian group specializing in hearing aid distribution and fitting, has obtained authorization from the European Commission for the acquisition of the hearing division of GN (a Danish group active in audio and communications). This approval completes the regulatory green lights, following those already obtained in Australia and the United States. Closing is expected before the end of the year, once the final formalities are completed. Preparations for operational integration are underway.

This is a procedural step — regulatory validation — rather than a new strategic event. What is nevertheless worth noting: simultaneously obtaining European, American, and Australian green lights for an audiology operation signals that competition authorities did not deem the merger likely to close off the market — a useful signal for players monitoring the consolidation of this sector.

🔮 What Amplifon could buy next

  • Audika Group — Amplifon could seek to consolidate its leadership position in hearing aid distribution post-GN Hearing, by strengthening its network of clinics.

Michiels Foods acquires Billiebubs: Belgian infant nutrition consolidates

Source: nieuwsblad.be →

Michiels Foods, a family business based in Zulte (East Flanders) specializing in food, is acquiring Billiebubs, a Kortrijk-based brand focused on breakfast products and snacks for babies and young children. The stated objective is to expand Michiels' activities into the consumer market.

Available sources do not detail Michiels Foods' exact product ranges or Billiebubs' distribution channels. The logic is clear: a regional food company seeking to build its own brand in a segment with high perceived value — infant nutrition — rather than remaining solely focused on undifferentiated products. The baby food market combines high regulatory requirements and strong parental loyalty: difficult to enter from scratch, more accessible through the acquisition of an established brand.

🔮 What Michiels Foods could buy next

  • Ready to Grow — Michiels Foods could seek to expand its infant nutrition offering with fresh, organic, and refrigerated products, complementing the acquisition of Billiebubs.
  • Ma Petite Cuillère — Michiels Foods could seek to complement its snacks with their positioning on homemade and organic meals for children.

Granit automates administrative tasks for healthcare professionals — and raises €5M after eight months

Source: entreprisma.fr →

📬 Subscribe to the HealthTech & Digital Health newsletter

Granit develops an administrative management automation tool for healthcare professionals: practices, clinics, and care facilities. The company uses artificial intelligence to reduce the administrative burden on these structures. Founded in March 2026, it already claims 250 equipped facilities. It announces a €5M.

The obvious takeaway: a startup gaining speed very quickly in a market known for its administrative friction, and raising funds to accelerate.

What deserves close attention is the timing: eight months of existence, 250 clients, a significant seed round. In healthcare, convincing 250 facilities in less than a year — a sector known for slow adoption, demanding compliance, and conservative habits — is a signal of real, not cosmetic, traction. The real test will be retention: healthcare administration is a field where tools are adopted quickly and uninstalled even faster if integration with existing business software is imperfect. For a French investor or industrial partner in digital health, Granit is worth watching precisely because the initial adoption speed says something — but does not yet say whether the product will last.
