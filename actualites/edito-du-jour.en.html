CVC acquires Recordati: Europe's largest LBO of 2026, financed without a dominant bank

Source: investorsinhealthcare.com →

Recordati is an Italian pharmaceutical group listed on Euronext Milan, specializing in rare diseases and specialty medicines. CVC Capital Partners, leading a consortium including GBL, is launching a public tender offer to delist all Recordati shares, structured via the holding company Respighi HoldCo.

The secured financing totals €7.58 billion, making it the largest LBO announced in Europe in 2026. The architecture is unusual in its size and composition: approximately €6.05 billion in senior secured debt in fixed and variable rate bonds, split into five tranches (including two denominated in dollars), an €850 million revolving credit facility, and a €1.53 billion PIK tranche issued by a separate entity (Respighi Investments). Twenty-four banks acted as initial underwriters. The first settlement of the offer took place on November 2; the second is scheduled for November 17.

The obvious interpretation: CVC is betting on the resilience of rare disease revenues, whose regulatory barriers and administered prices protect margins, to justify massive leverage.

What deserves attention is the structure itself: the absence of a classic bank term loan at the core of the arrangement — replaced by a bond stack — signals that the European credit market has sufficient depth to finance a « single B » at this scale without relying on a single lead bank. For an industrial acquirer or a sovereign fund monitoring the European specialized pharma market, the signal is twofold: liquidity exists for defensive assets, and the delisting of a player of this size mechanically reduces the pool of available listed targets.

Crédit Agricole — Banco BPM: Italy's third-largest bank takes shape from Paris

Source: pymnts.com →

📬 Subscribe to the FinTech newsletter

Crédit Agricole SA proposes to merge its Italian banking operations — grouped under Crédit Agricole Italia — with Banco BPM SpA, in which it already holds a 29.6% stake.

The proposed terms include an exchange ratio incorporating a 20% premium on Banco BPM's valuation, along with an extraordinary dividend of €1 billion paid to Banco BPM shareholders. Crédit Agricole estimates annual pre-tax synergies at approximately €800 million and projects an increase of around 24% in Banco BPM's earnings per share from 2027. Crédit Agricole's stake in the combined entity would rise to approximately 45%. Banco BPM would retain its listing in Milan, its Italian identity, and its CEO Giuseppe Castagna.

Strategic interpretation: Crédit Agricole cannot become number one in France — its mutualist model structurally prevents it. Italy has been its priority expansion territory for several years. By increasing its stake from 29.6% to 45% in a combined entity. The market reaction — Banco BPM up more than 9%, Crédit Agricole slightly down — reflects exactly this asymmetry: the target's shareholders capture the premium, while the acquirer's shareholders question the price of external growth.

  • Credem Banca — Its strong regional presence and multichannel model would complement its expansion in Italy, consolidating its position after Banco BPM.

Sapian acquires Aérolia, Servitec, and EC Assainissement: three acquisitions, one territorial network

Source: lagazettefrance.fr →

Sapian is a French group providing building hygiene services and protection of living spaces, founded 45 years ago and acquired from ISS by Weinberg Capital Partners in 2019. It operates in four business lines: air hygiene, pest control, sanitation, and fire protection. It has over 1,500 employees and forty agencies serving approximately 48,000 clients.

Sapian announces three simultaneous acquisitions: Aérolia (Radinghem-en-Weppes, Nord), specializing in air hygiene, industrial cleaning, and disinfection for industry, healthcare, agri-food, and local authorities; Servitec (South-West), specializing in air hygiene and fire protection; EC Assainissement (Ain), a historical player in wastewater purification and treatment. The combined revenue of the three targets exceeds €10 million.

The logic is that of a regional consolidator in an acceleration phase: each acquisition densifies a business in a geography, without changing the model. The next external growth operation dated back to 2024; by making three simultaneous acquisitions, Sapian signals that it is entering a more active phase — which, for a seller of technical services SMEs in France, deserves attention.

Warner Music France absorbs Délicieuse Musique: Bordeaux electronic catalog joins the major

Source: musicbusinessworldwide.com →

📬 Subscribe to the Media & Entertainment newsletter

Délicieuse Musique is a group of independent labels founded in 2012 in Bordeaux by Mathieu Léglise and Gautier de Bosredon. It manages ten imprints — including Délicieuse Records, Nice Guys, Noire & Blanche, Egoplanet — and represents over 1,200 artists in electronic genres (chill, lounge, deep house, jazz house) and indie pop. Its past signings include Lost Frequencies, Ofenbach, Bakermat, and Kungs.

Warner Music France acquires 100% of Délicieuse Musique. Financial terms are not disclosed. The company will continue to operate autonomously from Bordeaux, under the direction of its co-founders. The associated publishing house, GLM Editions, remains independent. The operation is described by Warner Music France as the first step in a strategy of partnerships with the French independent sector.

Strategic interpretation: Warner is not acquiring Délicieuse for its current revenues — it is acquiring its talent identification pipeline. An independent Bordeaux label that signed Lost Frequencies and Ofenbach before their international breakthrough possesses something that streaming algorithms cannot replicate: a trusted network with emerging artists who would refuse to sign directly with a major. By acquiring the independent wrapper while attaching WMG's global distribution and synchronization resources, Warner obtains access to this pipeline without destroying it — provided it keeps its promise of autonomy.

🔮 What Warner Music France could buy next

  • Nowadays Records — It could seek to expand its French electronic talent pipeline, capitalizing on the success of Délicieuse Musique and its model.
  • Roy Music — Its expertise in music supervision and diverse catalog would complement its talent pipeline, extending its presence beyond electronic music.

Lagardère Travel Retail enters Munich: the tenth German airport

Lagardère Travel Retail operates retail and catering outlets in airports. Before this operation, it was present in nine German airports with over 200 points of sale. Aichinger Gastro GmbH, founded in 2003 and managed by Elena and Markus Aichinger, operates six catering outlets in the secured area of Munich Airport, offering coffee specialties and Asian cuisine concepts.

Lagardère Travel Retail acquires Aichinger Gastro, extending its presence to ten German airports. Existing concepts will be maintained. Markus Aichinger will remain involved in an advisory role.

Vivify acquires imin: connecting school sports facilities to booking platforms

Source: businesscloud.co.uk →

📬 Subscribe to the Media & Entertainment newsletter

Vivify, a Manchester-based technology company, helps over 6,000 UK schools open their sports facilities (halls, pitches) to local communities outside school hours, founded in 2020. imin, based in London and founded in 2014, co-created OpenActive, the open data standard for physical activities supported by Sport England. Its technology allows users to discover and book sports activities by aggregating information available on different platforms (Decathlon, LTA Play, Every Body Moves by ParalympicsGB).

Vivify acquires imin for undisclosed terms. The combined entity will offer five solutions: slot management and instant payment, intelligent planning, marketplace, data analytics, and school facility management.

Strategic interpretation: Vivify owns the physical infrastructure (agreements with schools); imin owns the digital distribution layer (data feeds and integrations with booking platforms). The acquisition is a classic vertical integration: without imin, Vivify remains a slot manager; with imin, it becomes an entry point for anyone seeking an accessible sports activity in their community — which fundamentally changes the value proposition for local authorities and leisure operators.
