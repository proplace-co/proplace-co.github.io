Newborn Town acquires Softonic for its 100 million users

Source: expansion.com →

Softonic, a software download platform founded in Barcelona in 1997, with 100 million monthly users and a presence in Europe, Southeast Asia, and Latin America, has been fully acquired by Hong Kong-based group Newborn Town, listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange and specializing in interactive entertainment, mobile social networks, and games. The main shareholder, Michael Levit, held 61% of the capital among 42 shareholders. The current management, Fiona Garvey, remains in place; the brand, the Barcelona headquarters, and the European regulatory framework are preserved.

Newborn Town brings its artificial intelligence systems and its experience in scaling mobile applications — a portfolio that includes social apps Mico, YoHo, TopTop, and games like Alice's Dream.

What is at stake here is more precise than a simple traffic acquisition: Softonic possesses an established Western audience and a distribution infrastructure that Newborn Town could not build from scratch without encountering the regulatory and trust barriers specific to European markets. For an Asian group looking to distribute its games and applications in Europe, buying 100 million monthly users with a local brand for €40 to €50 million is a reasonably priced entry point.

🔮 What Newborn Town might acquire next

  • Soft112 — Newborn Town might seek to consolidate its presence in Europe and diversify its software catalog for its 100 million users.
  • EPTIMUM — Newborn Town might seek to complement its acquisition by strengthening its European presence.

Expedia acquires Kiwi.com to establish itself in European flight travel

Source: skift.com →

Kiwi generated €227 million in revenue in 2025. The price was not disclosed. The transaction is expected to close in the first half of 2027.

According to Expedia, the combination of Kiwi's technology with Expedia's scale will strengthen the Expedia brand and its B2B business in the air segment.

Strategic interpretation: the timing is not insignificant — the maturity of the convertible bond in June 2027 creates liquidity pressure for Kiwi's shareholders. Expedia is acquiring complex flight search technology and a European base in a context where its positioning in the air segment remains weaker than in accommodation. For a French or European travel operator, this means that Expedia is equipping itself with a more sophisticated flight distribution tool, capable of serving B2B partners beyond its own brand.

🔮 What Expedia might acquire next

  • Kombo — Expedia might seek to extend its multimodal search technology in Europe, capitalizing on the expertise of the target in flight travel.
  • Rail Europe — Expedia might seek to complement its post-Kiwi air offering, strengthening its multimodal positioning in Europe.

Intermarché takes control of Chronodrive

Source: lesechos.fr →

Auchan Retail has announced that it has entered into exclusive negotiations for the transfer of control of Chronodrive to Groupement Mousquetaires (Intermarché). The amount was not disclosed.

The Mousquetaires had already taken over Auchan supermarkets under franchise; this operation extends the movement by adding a pure-player drive network. For Auchan, which has been in difficulty for several years, the sale of Chronodrive is part of a gradual refocusing.

What is emerging is the recomposition of French food retail through absorption: Intermarché is methodically consolidating the assets of a weakened competitor, acquiring both locations and expertise in the autonomous drive format, a growing segment. For any player in food distribution or last-mile logistics, the gradual disappearance of Auchan's assets into the orbit of the Mousquetaires redraws the competitive map.

Alterra acquires Pryme's European development and establishes itself in Amsterdam

Source: google.com →

Alterra, an American company specializing in the chemical recycling of post-consumer plastics, whose technology has been operational for over five years at its Akron (Ohio) plant, announced the acquisition of Pryme N.V.'s development business, as well as the associated rights to an industrial site in the port of Amsterdam. Concurrently, Alterra raised new capital from Taranis and its existing shareholders Potenza Capital and Infinity 2 Capital. The amounts were not disclosed.

The acquisition brings an experienced European development team and a potential construction site in Amsterdam. Alterra has also identified a second opportunity in the Rotterdam region. Pryme N.V. continues to exist as an independent entity.

The European regulatory context is favorable: the regulation on packaging and packaging waste imposes increasing thresholds for recycled content for 2030 and 2040, creating structural demand for recycled raw materials.

Strategic interpretation: Alterra is importing into Europe an industrial model already commercially validated in the United States, relying on existing land rights and a local team already in place rather than starting from scratch. The proximity of major Dutch industrial ports is not insignificant — these are the entry points for plastic flows and potential petrochemical customer bases.

🔮 What Alterra might acquire next

  • Plastic Energy — Alterra might seek to extend its European footprint and chemical recycling capacity, capitalizing on its recent establishment in the Netherlands.
  • Xycle — Alterra might seek to strengthen its post-Pryme expansion, particularly in the Netherlands.

Believe takes full control of Lili Louise Musique

Source: musicbusinessworldwide.com →

The relationship between the two entities dates back to 2018. Founder Vincent Frèrebeau remains at the head of the group and retains his artistic and strategic management autonomy. The amount was not disclosed.

Lili Louise Musique encompasses several activities: tôt ou tard (label), Zouave (concert production), LLM (music publishing), Guess What (innovative projects), and stakes in the concert company Uni-t. The whole covers recording rights, copyrights, concerts, and artist development.

What Believe is building here is a complete vertical integration around French-speaking artists: from creation to concerts, including publishing and digital distribution. By fully absorbing a structure that already managed several links in the chain, Believe internalizes revenues that were partially external, while preserving the brand and culture that make tôt ou tard attractive to artists.

Believe enters China through catalogs

Source: karlobag.eu →

Believe also announced the acquisition of the catalog of independent Chinese label Tianyue Huayin — the group's first catalog acquisition in Greater China, where it has operated since 2016. Tianyue Huayin remains an independent entity; only its catalog is being sold. The amount was not disclosed.

The Chinese recorded music market generated $1.9 billion in revenue in 2025, growing by 20.1% year-on-year — the strongest growth among the top twenty global markets according to the IFPI.

Strategic interpretation: acquiring a local catalog rather than a simple license means establishing a long-term presence in a market with structural growth. For Believe, celebrating its tenth anniversary in Greater China, this is the transition from a distributor to a rights holder — a more defensive and profitable business model over time.

Ziacom acquires Geniova's aligner segment

Source: capital-riesgo.es →

Ziacom, a Spanish dental medical technology group based in Pinto (Madrid), which designs and manufactures dental implants, biomaterials, and clear aligners under the brand RealAligner, has acquired the conventional aligner business unit of Geniova Technologies. Geniova Technologies, renamed Specialist Dental Technologies, continues its other activities under the Specialist brand. The operation does not involve the acquisition of Geniova as a company. The amount was not disclosed.

Ziacom has been supported by the fund Suma Capital since 2022 and expects to close 2026 with approximately €25 million in revenue and 280 employees. The group is opening new production facilities in Pinto before the end of the year.

Strategic interpretation: rather than building a RealAligner customer base from scratch, Ziacom is acquiring an existing clientele of dental professionals already converted to conventional aligners — immediate commercial acceleration in a segment where brand recognition and practitioner trust are the real assets.

Boato Group acquires Nardini's assets in judicial liquidation

Source: ntplusdiritto.ilsole24ore.com →

Boato Group, a growing Italian industrial group through external acquisitions, has acquired the business assets of Nardini, a company founded in 1952 specializing in the design, construction, and installation of machines and complete lines for the production of bituminous membranes, through an auction process as part of Nardini's judicial liquidation. The amount was not disclosed.

Boato Group had already integrated C.M.M., Menestrina, and benefited from Nextalia's (Nextalia Credit Solutions fund) entry into its holding company last July.

In judicial liquidation proceedings, the acquirer can recover quality industrial assets — brand, machinery, know-how — under conditions that the free market would not allow. For Boato Group, this is an extension of technical skills in a niche segment with an established international clientele.

Holidu consolidates Spanish rural tourism

Citrosol acquires Janssen PMP's global post-harvest fungicide portfolio

Source: empresaexterior.com →

Citrosol (Productos Citrosol S.A.), a Valencian company specializing in post-harvest technologies and treatments for fruits and vegetables, founded in 1994 and employing 144 people, has formalized the acquisition of Janssen PMP's global post-harvest fungicide portfolio, a crop protection subsidiary of Janssen Pharmaceutica ( Johnson & Johnsongroup). The operation transfers the brands FUNGAFLOR®, FUNGAZIL®, PENBOTEC®, and PHILABUSTER®, as well as patents, intellectual property, know-how, and regulatory registrations in dozens of markets. The amount was not disclosed.

These products cover not only citrus fruits — Citrosol's historical specialty — but also pome fruits, cherries, blueberries, stone fruits, and bananas, a high-volume global fruit.

Citrosol is making a qualitative leap here: from a position as a citrus specialist, it is becoming a broad-portfolio player in global post-harvest protection. The acquisition of regulatory registrations in dozens of countries is particularly valuable — obtaining these authorizations from scratch would take years and tens of millions of euros.
