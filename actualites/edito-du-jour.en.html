SickKids and Microsoft Canada: the hospital as a cloud experimentation ground

Source: news.microsoft.com →

📬 Subscribe to the HealthTech & Digital Health newsletter

The Hospital for Sick Children (SickKids), a leading Canadian pediatric hospital based in Toronto, is partnering for five years with Microsoft Canada to migrate its clinical systems to Azure and deploy AI tools on its medical data. The agreement covers electronic health records, advanced analytics, medical needs forecasting, and assisted diagnosis. Microsoft commits in return to providing Azure credits, mobilizing technology partners, and training SickKids staff on AI tools.

No financial amount is communicated. This is a strategic partnership, not a capital transaction.

This type of agreement is less a software sale than a strategic positioning in the critical infrastructure of a leading institution: five years of structured pediatric clinical data on Azure is also a research asset that Microsoft will value well beyond the contract.

Stagwell and Palantir: corporate marketing enters the era of ontologies

Source: fr.investing.com →

📬 Subscribe to the Horizontal & Productivity SaaS newsletter

Stagwell (NASDAQ: STGW) is a marketing and communications group present in over 40 countries, which develops a suite of technology products under the Machine brand — Machine OS, The Knowledge Machine, The Audience Machine, The Media Machine. Palantir Technologies (NASDAQ: PLTR) provides AI software platforms to enterprise and government clients, notably through its data ontologies and agent framework.

The two companies are expanding a partnership formed in November 2025: Palantir is integrating its technological building blocks across Stagwell's entire Machine portfolio, and Stagwell, in turn, acts as a marketing transformation partner for Palantir's clients. The focus is on data sovereignty — clients retain their data in their own systems, without migration to a closed ecosystem.

Strategic interpretation: Palantir has long sold its technology to states and large industrial companies; corporate marketing represents a much larger volume market, but one historically resistant to the complexity of data engineering tools. Stagwell plays the role of translator and distributor. What is at stake here is not so much a product alliance as a bet by Palantir on its ability to move downmarket without diluting its value proposition — Stagwell takes the integration risk, Palantir takes the reputation risk.

Wendel: 3 billion raised in nine months, transformation into a third-party asset manager accelerates

Source: globenewswire.com →

Wendel (Euronext Paris) publishes its third-quarter 2026 results. The group, historically an investment holding company for its own account, has for several years been transforming into a third-party asset manager via Wendel Investment Managers. The Q3 figures illustrate the scale of the pivot: €48.7 billion in assets under management in private equity, private debt, and secondaries; over €3.0 billion raised in the first nine months of 2026, including $2.0 billion in private credit and €1.3 billion in secondaries. Management fees reached €344.0 million over nine months, up +41.3% compared to the previous year — an increase that includes the full-year effect of the acquisition of Monroe Capital and the acquisition of Committed Advisors in April 2026. Organic growth at constant scope and exchange rates was +10%.

Regarding its own portfolio (Wendel Principal Investments): the sale of Stahl is finalized, generating €1.14 billion in liquidity received as of October 1, at a price 20% above the revalued net asset value published before the announcement. The sale process forIHS is underway, with an anticipated closing by the end of 2026. Furthermore, Wendel is injecting €50 million into Globeducate to support its external growth. The revalued net asset value per share was €167.0 as of September 30, 2026, up +5.1% over the quarter.

What this publication reveals: third-party asset management activities now represent approximately one-third of the gross revalued assets excluding cash — Wendel's model increasingly resembles that of a listed asset management company and less and less that of an industrial conglomerate. The sale of Stahl at 20% above NAV is not a detail: it retroactively validates the group's valuation discipline, which is precisely what a third-party asset manager needs to raise funds from demanding institutional investors.
