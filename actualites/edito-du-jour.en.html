FedEx Acquires InPost: When the Last Mile Is Worth More Than the Parcel

Source: tvpworld.com →

InPost is the Polish operator of automated parcel lockers and out-of-home delivery that has transformed e-commerce logistics in Central and Western Europe—its tens of thousands of collection points allow for delivery without time constraints, at a significantly lower cost than home delivery. FedEx, leading a consortium, is finalizing its acquisition for 7.8 billion euros.

The immediate interpretation is that of an American express giant catching up on the European e-commerce market, where out-of-home delivery has significantly outpaced the American door-to-door model.

What's worth noting: InPost is not a carrier; it's an infrastructure of contact points. Its lockers are dense, capillary physical assets, impossible to replicate quickly—they resemble a network of telecom towers more than a fleet of trucks. FedEx isn't buying more trucks; it's buying a territorial network whose value grows with each new online merchant that connects to it. For a French retail or distribution player looking to reduce delivery costs in Central Europe, this change of control could mean a renegotiation of pricing terms in the medium term—the logic of a proprietary network is rarely favorable to captive customers.

CTP Acquires SLP in Vietnam for $500 Million: Logistics Real Estate as a Bet on Industrial Reshoring

Source: vietnam.vn →

CTP is a European developer and operator of logistics and industrial parks, listed in Amsterdam, active primarily in Central and Eastern Europe. SLP (Strategic Logistics Park) is a logistics park developer in Vietnam.

In just a few years, Vietnam has become one of the preferred destinations for industrial reshoring from China—electronics, textiles, automotive components—driven by Sino-American trade tensions and the search for more resilient supply chains. Quality logistics and industrial parks are scarce there, and demand from multinational companies setting up operations is strong.

CTP is exporting its model—developing, owning, and operating industrial parks long-term rather than reselling them—to a geography where this model barely exists yet. This is a bet on the permanence of the trend towards diversification of global production chains, and not on a short cycle. For a logistics real estate investor or an industrialist looking to establish production capacity in Southeast Asia, this signals that quality assets in this region are beginning to be capitalized at European levels.

EDF and Utilita: Exceeding Six Million Accounts in the UK

Source: economiematin.fr →

EDF operates in the UK as an energy supplier to individuals and businesses, under the EDF Energy brand. Utilita is a British energy supplier specializing in prepayment meters, a segment that primarily serves low-income households. EDF intends to acquire Utilita to cross the threshold of six million energy accounts in the UK—the amount has not been disclosed.

Strategic interpretation: The prepayment meter is a structurally distinct segment of the standard residential market—different customer base, different billing infrastructure, specific regulations. For EDF, the interest is not just volumetric: it's also access to a customer base whose relationship with energy is more granular, which gains value in a market where demand flexibility (curtailment, load management) becomes an asset in itself.

Swietelsky Acquires VIKING-Rail: Discreet Consolidation of Danish Rail Freight

Source: railmarket.com →

Swietelsky is an Austrian construction and railway engineering group, present in many European countries. VIKING-Rail is a Danish rail freight operator. The acquisition is announced without a disclosed amount.

Rail freight in Northern Europe is undergoing gradual consolidation, driven by regulatory pressure for modal shift (road to rail) and network liberalization. For Swietelsky, integrating a freight operator gives it a position on the downstream link of the railway chain, beyond just infrastructure construction and maintenance—a logic of moving upmarket in the sector's added value.

Portugal Takes 13.7% Stake in REN: A State Securing Its Energy Network

REN (Redes Energéticas Nacionais) is the operator of the electricity and natural gas transmission network in Portugal. The Portuguese state is acquiring a stake of 13.7 % in REN—amount undisclosed.

This is less a financial decision than a signal of sovereignty over an infrastructure whose criticality has been rediscovered.

Schroeder van der Kolk Takes Over Big Bazar: The Second Life of a Dutch Discounter

Source: voorburgsdagblad.nl →

Big Bazar is a Dutch discount retail chain. Schroeder van der Kolk, a company based in The Hague, is taking control—amount undisclosed.

The details of the operation and the precise profile of the acquirer are not available from the sources. What stands out is the fact that a discount brand is changing hands in the Netherlands, in a retail segment that remains under pressure between hard-discount food retailers and online commerce.

AlpSemi Raises €17M to Accelerate the Transformation of Smart Grids

Source: ipsilon-ip.com →

AlpSemi is an industrial startup founded and based in Grenoble, developing power semiconductors for smart grids. The power semiconductor sector is the critical link between intermittent renewable electricity generation and its efficient transmission or storage—without high-performance power conversion components, the energy transition faces significant physical losses. AlpSemi is raising 17 million euros from Yotta Capital, a French fund.

Grenoble is one of the few European ecosystems where public research in microelectronics (CEA-Leti, STMicroelectronics) and industrial startups coexist with sufficient density to produce teams capable of designing components of this level of complexity.

What makes this fundraising interesting beyond its amount: power semiconductors are currently one of the least visible but most structural bottlenecks in the energy transition. Demand for components capable of managing variable electrical flows (wind, solar, storage) is exploding, and European dependence on Asian suppliers in this segment is real. A Grenoble-based startup developing these components in Europe addresses a concrete industrial tension, not a technological trend.

Vetaion Raises €5.7M in Pre-Seed to Automate Control Cabinet Production

Source: sesamers.com →

Vetaion is a Munich-based startup developing robots to automate the manufacturing of electrical control cabinets—these cabinets are the equipment that controls and protects industrial installations, and their production today remains largely manual, fragmented among hundreds of small integrators. Vetaion is raising 5.7 million euros in a pre-seed round co-led by Visionaries Tomorrow and UVC Partners.

The control cabinet is a highly customized product—each industrial project requires a specific version—which has long made its automation difficult. This is precisely the barrier that Vetaion seeks to overcome with robotics.

For an industrial investor or an electrical integrator, this is an operation to watch: if Vetaion succeeds in automating this process, it is not just selling robots—it is redefining the economic model of an entire segment of industrial subcontracting that employs tens of thousands of technicians in Europe.
