EQT announces Stockbridge acquisition and closes quarter with Coller Capital

Source: finanznachrichten.de →

EQT, the Swedish alternative asset manager, announces the acquisition of Stockbridge, a US real estate manager with approximately $17 billion in fee-generating assets under management, as part of the development of its Real Estate line. The transaction will be financed in cash. Concurrently, EQT confirms the closing, as of August 31, of its merger with Coller Capital, a private equity secondary market specialist, which now operates under the name Coller EQT. This merger added €34 billion to EQT's assets under management, bringing the total to €354 billion.

Strategic interpretation: EQT is methodically building a multi-strategy platform — private equity, infrastructure, real estate, secondaries — capable of meeting broad institutional mandates. The acquisition of Stockbridge completes the real estate range; the integration of Coller opens access to the secondary market, a rapidly growing segment as LPs seek liquidity on their portfolios. The logic is that of a manager selling diversification and access, not just performance by strategy.

Leonardo-Iveco Defence: Brussels validates the recomposition of Italian land defense

Source: reuters.com →

Leonardo, the Milan-listed defense and aerospace conglomerate, is acquiring the defense arm ofIveco Group, a specialist in land military vehicles — armored vehicles, command vehicles, tactical logistics. The agreement was announced at the end of July 2025 for €1.7 billion ; the European Commission has just given its green light.

The obvious interpretation: Leonardo strengthens its position in land systems, a segment where it was historically less present than in air and naval. Iveco Defence brings an established industrial base, contracts with the Italian army, and relationships with several allied armies.

What makes the operation structurally interesting is the timing: the post-2022 European rearmament has dramatically re-evaluated land defense assets. Iveco Group, whose core business remains civilian trucks, held an asset whose value had just risen — and for which it was not best placed to capture the growth. Leonardo, on the other hand, is exactly the acquirer that can integrate these capabilities into more complex systems (connected vehicles, embedded electronics, NATO interoperability). Brussels' green light on an operation of this size in defense, without apparent major conditions, also says something about the European political appetite for integrated continental champions.

Lufthansa wins TAP Air Portugal: geography trumped price

Source: businesstravelnews.com →

TAP Air Portugal is the Portuguese national airline, with a hub in Lisbon and a dense network to Brazil and Lusophone Africa — an Atlantic bridge few European airlines possess. Lufthansa Group has just been designated as the acquirer of a 44.9% stake for up to €1.2 billion, beating Air France-KLM which had offered €1.04 billion with a conditional supplement.

The Portuguese government retains 50.1% and reserves 5% for employees. Lufthansa will take over executive and operational management. Prime Minister Luís Montenegro presented the operation as the first phase of a privatization that could go further — subject to parliamentary support.

The obvious interpretation: Lufthansa pays more than Air France-KLM and wins. It integrates an existing Star Alliance partner, consolidates its footprint in the South Atlantic, and adds Lisbon as a secondary hub to its network.

But the real question posed by this operation lies elsewhere. Air France-KLM was the logical buyer: same SkyTeam alliance, geographical proximity, complementary Atlantic network. If Lufthansa won despite a higher price, it's because Lisbon was worth more to them — precisely because they didn't have comparable access to the South Atlantic. When an asset is worth more to buyer B than to buyer A, it's often because B fills a structural gap, not because they overpay. For Air France-KLM, the loss of TAP closes a window on an Atlantic corridor that it cannot recreate from Paris or Amsterdam with the same density of Lusophone traffic.

Crédit Agricole acquires IKB: a French bank enters the German Mittelstand

Source: fstech.co.uk →

📬 Subscribe to the Construction & PropTech newsletter

IKB Deutsche Industriebank is a century-old German commercial bank, specializing in financing large German SMEs and mid-caps — the Mittelstand — through loans, capital market solutions, and advisory services. It reported a net profit of €61 million for 2025 and a loan book of €8.2 billion. Its current shareholder is Lone Star Funds, an American private equity fund. Crédit Agricole, France's second-largest bank, has signed an agreement to acquire control as part of its ACT 2028 strategic plan — Germany is designated a priority market. The price is not disclosed. The closing is expected in the second half of 2027.

The operation is more straightforward than it appears. Crédit Agricole is not building a presence in Germany from scratch: it is acquiring a hundred years of relationships with the German industrial fabric, a network of contacts in the segment of companies that export, invest, and need structured financing. The Mittelstand is notoriously difficult for a foreign player to penetrate — banking relationships there take a long time to build and are loyal once established. Lone Star, which acquired IKB after its difficulties related to subprime mortgages in 2008, is making a clean exit here after cleaning up the balance sheet. For Crédit Agricole, this is a customer acquisition as much as a balance sheet acquisition — and a positioning in the financing of Germany's industrial transition at a time when investment needs are considerable.

NUBURU acquires Tekne: a defense platform is built at a low price in Italy

Source: stocktitan.net →

NUBURU, a listed American company repositioning itself as a dual-use defense and security platform, is finalizing the acquisition of a majority stake (70%) in Tekne S.p.A., an Italian defense manufacturer founded in 1990, specializing in electronic warfare, special vehicles, and defense mobility, with approximately 185 people across three sites in Italy (Ortona, Poggiofiorito, Guastalla).

The economics of the transaction are unusual. NUBURU paid €5.2 million for the additional 67.1% that gives it control — €1.2 million at signing, €4 million deferred until March 2027 — plus an earn-out capped at €20 million, payable in cash from 2028 based on 5% of annual revenues, with a guaranteed minimum of €2 million/year. Furthermore, approximately €29.7 million in shareholder loans and receivables were written off, converted into permanent equity of Tekne. Tekne reports approximately $93 million in residual value on its production backlog as of September 14, 2026 — a preliminary and unaudited figure.

This arrangement is worth examining. NUBURU gains control of a defense manufacturer with a $93 million production backlog for an initial outlay of €1.2 million. The bulk of the value transferred is the write-off of internal debts — a balance sheet operation that creates equity without fresh cash. It's a form of acquisition through restructuring: the former shareholders cede control in exchange for a clean slate and a future earn-out. For NUBURU, which is rebuilding after a difficult period (approximately $16.75 million in debt repaid in the preceding 90 days according to sources), this is a way to enter the NATO defense market in Italy with a minimal entry ticket. The symmetrical risk: the backlog value is preliminary and unaudited — this is the figure that will need to be validated.

Hy24 acquires Hynamics from EDF: when the public operator hands over to the specialized fund

Source: esgtoday.com →

Hynamics, anEDF subsidiary founded in 2019, produces and distributes low-carbon hydrogen for heavy mobility and industry, with projects in France and the United Kingdom. Hy24, a joint venture between Ardian and FiveT Hydrogen, is acquiring it via its Clean Hydrogen Infrastructure Fund — a €2 billionfund, one of the largest exclusively dedicated to the hydrogen value chain. The divestment is being made from EDF Pulse Ventures, the group's venture capital arm. The transaction is expected to close in the first quarter of 2027, subject to regulatory approvals. The amount is not disclosed.

This move illustrates a recurring logic in energy transition assets: large public or semi-public operators have incubated niche subsidiaries in still-immature technologies; having reached a stage of development that requires a dedicated, patient, and specialized shareholder, these subsidiaries are divested to thematic infrastructure funds that can scale them without the constraints of a group balance sheet. EDF refocuses, Hy24 consolidates. For French hydrogen players, the signal is twofold: the technology is mature enough to interest a €2 billion fund, but not yet mature enough to remain in an electricity company's portfolio.

Penske Automotive goes private: global automotive distributor returns to private ownership

Source: investors.penskeautomotive.com →

📬 Subscribe to the Mobility & Transportation newsletter

Penske Automotive Group is one of the world's largest automotive and commercial truck distributors, with operations in the United States, Germany, Italy, Japan, and Australia. Penske Corporation and Mitsui & Co., already its main shareholders, have agreed to take it private via a merger at $212 per share, valuing the company at approximately $16.3 billion in enterprise value. Following the transaction, Penske Corporation will own approximately 64% and Mitsui 36%. The special committee of independent directors unanimously recommended the transaction.

The delisting of an automotive distributor of this size by its own majority shareholders is a signal about the stock market's perception of the sector. Listed markets undervalue distributors with narrow margins in an uncertain cycle (electric transition, manufacturer pressure, volume volatility). By going private, Penske and Mitsui free themselves from quarterly reporting requirements and can manage the network's transformation — towards electric, towards services — at their own pace, without the pressure of a stock price. The modest premium (approximately 9% over the last price) suggests that the market had already factored in the available value; this is a governance operation as much as a financial one.

Paprec crosses €5 billion and acquires Marius Pedersen in Czech Republic and Slovakia

Source: paprec.com →

📬 Subscribe to the Climate & Energy Tech newsletter

Paprec, a French family group founded in 1994, operates in recycling, waste management, and green energy production. It simultaneously announces crossing the €5 billion revenue mark expected by the end of 2026 and the signing of an agreement to acquire Marius Pedersen 's activities in the Czech Republic and Slovakia — just weeks after announcing the acquisition of Brantner Green Solutions, present in five Central European countries. The group will soon reach nearly 30,000 employees and over 800 sites in 15 countries, with approximately €2 billion in revenues generated outside France.

Paprec is methodically building a European recycling platform starting from a dominant position in France. The logic is both industrial and regulatory: European directives on waste and the circular economy create homogeneous pressure on Central European countries that still have ground to make up — which generates organic growth for an operator entering now. By adding Marius Pedersen (Czech Republic, Slovakia) to Brantner (five countries), Paprec gains critical mass in Central Europe in a few months. For a family group, this is a remarkable change of scale — and a model of internationalization through the acquisition of entrenched local operators rather than by greenfield development.

21 Invest sells Energreen to Ambienta and reinvests: a PE exit that isn't an exit

Source: 21invest.com →

📬 Subscribe to the Climate & Energy Tech newsletter

Energreen, founded in 1999 and based in Pojana Maggiore (Vicenza), designs and manufactures self-propelled and remote-controlled machines for vegetation management — a segment that is gradually replacing traditional tractors with dedicated, more productive, and less energy-consuming equipment. 21 Invest, a European fund founded by Alessandro Benetton, is selling its stake to Ambienta, a European asset manager focused on environmental sustainability. A particularity: 21 Invest is reinvesting in the next phase alongside Ambienta and founder Daniele Fraron.

The global market for vegetation management machines is estimated at approximately €4 billion; dedicated machines (like Energreen's) represent about one-fifth of the market but constitute its fastest-growing segment, at double-digit rates.

21 Invest's reinvestment is the most interesting signal of this operation. A fund that sells and then reacquires a fraction alongside the new shareholder says two things: it believes in the trajectory but recognizes that Ambienta — with its environmental positioning and specialized networks — is better placed to lead the next phase. It's a handover, not an exit. For Ambienta, Energreen ticks both boxes of its investment framework (resource efficiency and pollution reduction) in a market where climate regulatory pressure — fire risks, infrastructure roadside management — creates growing structural demand.

Fraikin passes to Asterion: a commercial mobility infrastructure changes hands

Source: prnewswire.com →

📬 Subscribe to the Industrial Tech & Manufacturing newsletter

Fraikin is a pan-European commercial vehicle lessor — trucks, utility vehicles, specialized fleets — with approximately €1 billion in revenue and a fleet whose gross book value reaches approximately €3 billion. Its current shareholders — Benefit Street Partners, Värde Partners and Canyon Partners — have signed an exclusive agreement for its sale to Asterion Industrial Partners, a European mid-market infrastructure fund. The closing is expected in the first quarter of 2027. The price is not disclosed.

Under Asterion, Fraikin has three priorities: investment in the fleet and European platform, transition to low-emission vehicles, and digitalization of fleet management offerings.

Fraikin is an infrastructure asset disguised as a lessor: long-term contracts, immobilized fleet, captive industrial clients, high barriers to entry. This profile — recurring revenues, capital intensity, long horizon — is exactly what an infrastructure fund like Asterion values more than a credit fund or a generalist private equity fund. The change of hands has a cyclical logic: credit funds (BSP, Värde, Canyon) restructured and stabilized; an infrastructure fund takes over to invest in the fleet's energy transition, which requires patient capex. For Fraikin's French clients — many in logistics and construction — operational continuity is the priority; the change of shareholder should not alter commercial relationships.

Solvera: a building engineering firm built around AI from day one

Source: eu-startups.com →

📬 Subscribe to the Construction & PropTech newsletter

Solvera, founded in July 2026 in Oslo by Arif Mirza, Pål Rasmussen, and Thorvald Thorsnes, does not sell software to building engineers: it positions itself as the engineering firm itself, re-engineered around AI. Specifically, it handles end-to-end engineering missions — starting with mechanical, electrical, and plumbing (MEP) systems — by combining experienced engineers and proprietary AI agents. Clients pay for the delivered result, not for licenses or consulting hours. The company announces a pre-seed round of €5.62 million, led by Seedcamp, with participation from Construct Venture, Inception Fund, Momentum, Obos Ventures, PSV Tech, Sondo, and The Nordic Web Ventures, as well as business angels including Spacemaker founders Anders Kvåle and Håvard Haukeland.

What distinguishes Solvera from dozens of AI tools for construction is the business model as much as the technology. By billing for results rather than time or software, Solvera aligns with its clients' interests and can scale its agents without proportionally increasing its costs. The system learns from each project and reuses that judgment across the firm — something an organization of thousands of consultants billing by the hour cannot do without friction. The presence of Spacemaker founders (acquired by Autodesk) in the round is a signal of sectoral credibility: they have already built and sold AI for architectural design.
