KKR and Telecom Italia: When regulatory arbitration comes back like a boomerang

Source: tech-insider.org →

📬 Subscribe to the Retail & E-commerce Tech newsletter

KKR, the American private equity fund, acquired in 2024 NetCo, the fixed network subsidiary of Telecom Italia, for a value of up to 22 billion euros — the copper cable and fiber connecting millions of Italian homes and businesses, the infrastructure that all operators in the country must rely on to reach their own customers. The European Commission has just sent KKR a formal statement of objections — the step preceding a potential fine — on the grounds that the fund allegedly provided incorrect or misleading information during the review of the case in 2024, particularly regarding the terms of wholesale access contracts already signed with competing operators: these contracts allow other players to lease network capacity to sell their own internet and telephony offers, without building their own infrastructure. The central question is whether these access conditions should or should not remain applicable after the change of ownership.

KKR disputes the facts and reiterates that the 2024 decision remains legally valid. The statement of objections is not a judgment.

What is at stake here goes beyond the KKR-TIM case: a national fixed network, once privatized, becomes a toll road. Whoever controls the tariff conditions for access effectively controls the competitive structure of an entire country's telecom market. If the Commission establishes that the access terms communicated during the acquisition did not reflect the contractual reality, the entire logic of these large infrastructure divestitures to financial funds will be questioned — not on principle, but on the transparency of commitments. For an acquirer or investor in European infrastructure, the message is clear: regulators are no longer content to validate at closing; they are looking backward.

EQT in pole position for Qiagen: Molecular biology as a defensive asset

Source: ground.news →

Qiagen is a Dutch-German company specializing in molecular tests — it provides kits and instruments for extracting, purifying, and analyzing DNA and RNA in clinical, pharmaceutical, and research laboratories worldwide. Its name became public during the pandemic, but its activity has been structurally embedded in the diagnostic and molecular biology value chain for decades. EQT, the Swedish private equity fund, is reportedly in advanced negotiations to acquire Qiagen in an LBO valued at over 10 billion dollars, according to Bloomberg. KKR has also reportedly submitted an offer.

Strategic interpretation: Qiagen is a rare asset — a global platform for diagnostic consumables with a recurring revenue base (kits are replenished with each analysis), established regulatory positions, and exposure to pharmaceutical, clinical, and academic markets simultaneously. For a fund the size of EQT, this is precisely the type of defensive asset — non-cyclical, with high intellectual property intensity — that justifies a high control premium. The competition between EQT and KKR on this deal says something about the market: large life sciences platforms with recurring revenues have become as rare as physical infrastructure, and funds are now treating them with the same logic of accumulation.

🔮 What EQT might buy next

  • Eurobio Scientific FR — The acquirer seeks to strengthen their European positioning in in-vitro and molecular diagnostics, thus complementing the acquisition of Qiagen.
  • Molbio Diagnostics — Their portable Truenat PCR platform would extend their global reach in molecular diagnostics, capitalizing on the acquisition of Qiagen for the markets.

Lundi Matin forced to acquire Dispeo: When justice compels an acquirer

Source: lejournaldesentreprises.com →

Lundi Matin is a commercial management and logistics software publisher that reported 18 million euros in revenue in 2024 and employed 200 people. Dispeo was a logistics provider specializing in e-commerce, placed into receivership in July 2024 and then liquidation in March 2025. In February 2025, the court selected Lundi Matin to acquire the Beauvais site — 221 employees — as part of a divestment plan, while Bigblue acquired the Guichainville site. The deed of transfer was never signed. The Marseille Commercial Court therefore ordered Lundi Matin on October 12, 2026, to finalize the acquisition within ten days, under penalty of 1,000 euros per day of delay; failing signature within a month, the judgment itself will serve as the deed of transfer. The Beauvais site currently employs 140 people.

A judicial acquisition not finalized for over a year, with the seller's liquidation occurring in the interim, creates an unprecedented situation: the acquirer is bound by a commitment it never formalized, for an asset whose context has changed. The judicial constraint transforms what should have been a strategic acquisition into a legal obligation — with the execution risks this implies for the 140 employees still in post.

Morgan Stanley Capital Partners Acquires The Rapid Group

Source: eatonvance.com →

The Rapid Group (TRG) is a provider of inspection, repair, and maintenance services for rotating equipment — pumps, motors, generators — for municipal, commercial, and industrial clients in the Northeastern United States (New York, New Jersey, Connecticut, Massachusetts, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island). Founded over fifty years ago, the company serves over 1,800 clients and relies on technical expertise in emergency maintenance of critical infrastructure, particularly water and wastewater systems — among the oldest in the United States. Morgan Stanley Capital Partners (MSCP), Morgan Stanley Investment Management's middle market buyout team, announces the acquisition of TRG. The amount was not disclosed.

Strategic interpretation: TRG operates in an unglamorous but structurally growing segment — the maintenance of aging infrastructure in a dense region, with a captive clientele (municipalities cannot choose not to maintain their pumps) and barriers to entry built on fifty years of local reputation. For MSCP, which has already invested in infrastructure services companies, the acquisition of TRG follows a regional consolidation logic: a dense platform in the Northeast, followed by geographical and capacity expansion through complementary acquisitions.

Data I/O Doubles in Size with Acquisition of V-TEK and A&J Programming

Source: stocktitan.net →

Data I/O (Nasdaq-listed) specializes in semiconductor programming — its systems enable firmware to be loaded into chips before their integration into finished products, particularly in the automotive sector. The company has just finalized the acquisition of V-TEK and A&J Programming for approximately 23 million dollars (20 million in cash, 3 million in Data I/O shares). V-TEK brings semiconductor handling, testing, and packaging capabilities, as well as programming services. Data I/O anticipates doubling its revenue and reducing its dependence on automotive industry cycles. Mitch Jacobs, President of V-TEK, becomes Senior Vice President of Data I/O.

The interest of the operation lies in diversification: Data I/O was focused on automotive, a transitional and cyclical sector. By adding service and packaging capabilities, the company is moving towards a more recurring model — Programming-as-a-Service — and expanding its industrial customer base.

Legora: From 0 to 200 million ARR in 24 months, and 550 million to keep going

Source: lemondedudroit.fr →

Legora builds an artificial intelligence assistant for law firms and corporate legal departments — its flagship product, Legora aOS, is described as an agentic operating system where legal professionals work alongside AI agents for research, drafting, case management, and legal workflow management. The company closes a Series E of 550 million dollars led by Atreides Management, with participation from new institutional investors — Baron Capital, Capital Research, Fidelity Management & Research Company, Capital Group, Glade Brook Capital Partners — and all of its historical investors: Accel, Benchmark, Bessemer Venture Partners, General Catalyst, ICONIQ, Insight Venture Partners, Redpoint Ventures, Salesforce Ventures and Sands Capital. The post-money valuation reaches 8.8 billion dollars, compared to 5.6 billion in the previous round.

The growth rate is worth noting: Legora went from 1 to 100 million dollars in annual recurring revenue in less than eighteen months, then reached 200 million in less than six additional months. For an enterprise software publisher selling directly — without an indirect distribution channel — this pace is indeed rare. The capital will be used for North American expansion (offices in New York, Denver, Chicago, Houston, San Francisco, Toronto, and Mexico City), development in Europe and Asia-Pacific, and investment in new products.

What distinguishes Legora in the crowded landscape of AI tools for legal professionals is less the product itself than the speed at which it has established itself in organizations known for their conservatism. Law firms and legal departments have long purchasing cycles, high confidentiality requirements, and a culture of prudence. That Legora achieved 200 million ARR by selling directly to these clients says something about the reality of adoption — and explains why long-term institutional investors like Fidelity or Capital Group, who don't play the classic venture game, joined this round.

Pigment: The French financial planning platform aiming to compete with Oracle and Anaplan

Source: lesechos.fr →

Pigment is a financial and operational planning platform founded in 2019, which enables finance, HR, and operations teams to build forecasting models, simulate scenarios, and manage company performance in real time. Its direct competitors are the major American platforms in the sector — Anaplan (acquired by Thoma Bravo), Adaptive Insights (Workday), Planful. The company closes a Series E of 250 million dollars led by the Scaleup Europe Fund, with Nvidia participating in the round. The valuation is not disclosed in available sources, but Les Echos indicates that it has increased compared to the previous round.

Nvidia's presence in the funding round is not anecdotal: it signals that Pigment integrates or plans to integrate intensive computing capabilities into its planning models — AI-assisted financial planning requires large-scale inference on sensitive enterprise data. For Pigment, it is also a signal of credibility in the American market, where the battle against established players is fought as much on technology as on the reputation of partners. Concretely, for a French CFO evaluating planning tools: Pigment is now one of the rare European players with the financial capacity and technological partnerships to stand up to American platforms on their own turf.

Dream Labs: 90 million for the brain of robots

Source: automate.org →

Dream Labs emerges from stealth mode, announcing a 90 million dollar seed round, co-led by B Capital and Kindred Ventures. The company is developing Dream-1, a foundational model trained on human interactions, designed to control any two-armed robotic system equipped with grippers. Within twenty-four hours of its launch, the model was reportedly used to control 31 types of robots it had never encountered during training. The funds will primarily be used to finance compute to expand the task library and increase the model's scale.

A 90 million dollar seed round for a company just emerging from stealth mode indicates where the competition in robotics is concentrated: not on metal and motors, but on general-purpose control software. Whoever builds the foundational model that all robot manufacturers will want to use captures a position analogous to that of an operating system — a royalty on every deployment. This is the bet that B Capital and Kindred are funding here.

Eka Robotics: 150 million for haptic robotic hands

Eka Robotics develops robotic hands equipped with a “force action model” — a system that transmits tactile information about what the robot is manipulating to the control model, allowing for finer dexterity than purely visual approaches. The company raises 150 million dollars in a Series A, co-led by Andreessen Horowitz and F-Prime. This round also provides insight into how a16z intends to use its 1.1 billion dollar hardware fund.

Dexterity remains the main bottleneck in industrial robotics: a robot that can see but not feel breaks fragile parts or misses precise assemblies. Force feedback — transmitting what the machine touches to it — is the technical problem that robotic hand manufacturers have been trying to solve for years. Eka bets that vertical integration (hand + force model) creates lasting differentiation.

ExponTech: Storage as the bottleneck for AI inference

Source: dealroom.co →

📬 Subscribe to the Data & Analytics newsletter

ExponTech (华瑞指数云) is a Chinese distributed data storage startup that develops data management products for enterprise IT infrastructure, with a focus on domestic technology. It raises over 13.8 million dollars in a new round, co-led by Congbi Qiushi Investment with the participation of a public industrial fund. The capital is directed towards WQS, a KV Cache storage product designed specifically for large language model inference — the phase where an AI model generates its responses, which requires ultra-fast access to in-memory data. WQS is in production testing phase.

AI inference is the real mass market: training a model is done once, inference runs continuously for every query from every user. KV Cache storage is one of the concrete bottlenecks of this phase — and that's precisely where ExponTech is positioning its bet. In the context of Sino-American technological rivalry, the fact that a public industrial fund participates in the round signals that this computing infrastructure is considered strategic beyond just commercial stakes.

120ft: Microfactories for robots, no humans in the loop

120ft is a Swedish startup that designs autonomous microfactories — production units the size of three standard shipping containers, operated entirely by humanoid robots, with no human presence in the workspace. The funds will be used for software and hardware development with a view to commercial deployment.

This is a seed round, consistent with the company's stage. The angle is interesting — isolating robots in human-free spaces bypasses some of the safety and certification constraints that hinder deployment in mixed environments — but it remains to be demonstrated at scale.

EstateMin: AI for inheritance law firms, 1 million euros to establish in the United States

Biogenera: A Bolognese biotech on crowdfunding, possibly heading for Euronext

Source: dealroom.co →

📬 Subscribe to the FinTech newsletter

Biogenera is an Italian biotech based near Bologna that develops anti-gene drugs based on its patented MyGenera platform. Its lead candidate, BGA002, targets aggressive pediatric cancers linked to MYCN amplification and has orphan drug status with the FDA and EMA — but the compound remains preclinical, with no human data. The company raises 667,469 euros via the crowdfunding platform CrowdFundMe, exceeding its minimum target of 249,984 euros, at a pre-money valuation of 90 million euros. The campaign closes on October 26. These funds will cover the regulatory work needed to launch a Phase I study at Padua Hospital in early 2027 — but completing the full Phase I would cost approximately 2.5 million euros, and total clinical development is estimated at 35 million.

Biogenera returns to crowdfunding for the third time (after 2.87 million in 2021 and 1.58 million in 2024), each time with an IPO announced as imminent — an aborted SPAC merger in 2020, unfulfilled Euronext Paris projects in 2021, 2022, and 2024. The amount raised today barely covers the first regulatory step of a program that requires thirty-five times more. For an investor evaluating this case: the science may be serious, but the funding path to the clinic remains entirely open.

Momentum Technologies / LifePods: Survival capsules, 400,000 euros in preparation

Source: lindependant.fr →

Momentum Technologies is a four-employee company based in Toulouges (Pyrénées-Orientales) that manufactures survival capsules under the LifePods brand — an anti-ballistic version resistant to military-grade weapon fire (B01, ICC 500 certified) and a version resistant to climatic hazards such as floods and tsunamis (W01). Its director, Cédric Choffat, is preparing a fundraising round of 400,000 euros for 4% of the capital, at an announced valuation of 10 million euros, with the support of BPI and the Foster fund from the Occitanie region. The B01 has attracted interest in the United States for hurricane protection; a salesperson has been hired to target the Japanese market.

This is a seed round for a company in the industrial incubation phase, with certifications obtained and markets to explore. The fundraising is not yet closed according to available information.
