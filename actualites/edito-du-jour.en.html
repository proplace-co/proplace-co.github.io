Carrefour acquires Codifrance: what the group is really buying is not stores

Source: infonet.fr →

Carrefour announces the acquisition of Codifrance, a subsidiary of the Belgian group Colruyt Group, for an enterprise value of 152 million euros, with closing expected in the first half of 2027, subject to regulatory approvals.

Codifrance is not a network of integrated stores. Founded in 1969, the company is an affiliation and local food distribution operator: it supplies approximately 3,100 independent clients — retailers who remain owners of their points of sale — and covers nearly three-quarters of French territory. What Carrefour is buying is a networking model, a logistical infrastructure, and expertise in animating networks of independent retailers.

The obvious interpretation: Carrefour is consolidating its proximity segment, consistent with its Carrefour 2030 strategic plan which explicitly identifies this format as a growth axis. The group already operates over 1,500 Proxi stores in France; Codifrance expands the base of affiliated retailers and densifies territorial coverage.

What deserves attention is the exact nature of the acquired asset: Carrefour does not inherit any leases or any proprietary business goodwill — it acquires a contract of trust with 3,100 independent businesses. The value lies in the relationship, logistics, and the ability to renew these affiliation contracts. Integration will therefore be less a matter of real estate than of network management: retaining Codifrance's teams, maintaining service quality for affiliates, and preventing leaks to other central purchasing bodies. For a group the size of Carrefour, the risk is not financial — it is cultural.

Brioche Pasquier establishes itself on the American East Coast

Brioche Pasquier, an agri-food group based in Cholet (Maine-et-Loire), acquired at the end of 2025 90% of the capital ofAnthony & Sons Bakery, a family bakery founded in 1984 by Anthony Dattolo, located in Denville, New Jersey, with a fully automated 6,000 m² production site. The goodwill recorded in consolidation amounts to 57 million euros — the only financial figure communicated by the group regarding this operation.

With Anthony & Sons, the group is now positioning itself on the East Coast, near New York, in traditional bakery — a different profile from Galaxy Desserts, which is more focused on premium desserts.

Strategic interpretation: two American locations, two coasts, two product profiles — Brioche Pasquier is patiently building an industrial presence in the United States without immediately going through national mass distribution. The automated production tool in New Jersey is a real industrial asset, not just a brand. For a French mid-sized family business, this is a way to test and establish itself before expanding.

Wirquin acquires a stake in Etteliot: an industrial player takes a position in waterless construction site sanitation

Source: agence-api.ouest-france.fr →

Wirquin, a Nantes-based mid-sized company specializing in sanitary equipment (1,000 employees, approximately 100 million euros in revenue, based in Carquefou), has acquired a minority stake inEtteliot, a Nantes startup with six employees founded in 2020. The amount is not disclosed and is described as modest.

Etteliot has developed an autonomous toilet system that processes urine without water or chemicals. The company currently installs its devices at the top of construction cranes to improve sanitary conditions on construction sites — a segment where access constraints and the absence of a water network make conventional solutions impractical.

This rapprochement illustrates a simple industrial logic: Wirquin, whose core business is precisely sanitary equipment, is taking an option on a technology complementary to its own, without water or chemicals, where its usual products cannot go. The stake remains symbolic in amount, but it creates a capital link that can facilitate future integration or acquisition if the technology proves itself on a large scale. For Etteliot, an industrial shareholder from the same sector is often worth more than their check: it provides access to distribution networks, commercial credibility, and production expertise.

Inovia Capital Fund VI: Quebec capital structures its bet on nascent AI

Source: dealroom.co →

📬 Subscribe to the FinTech newsletter

The fund will be deployed over three years, with tickets ranging from $1 million to $10 million for pre-revenue companies, targeting 25 to 30 startups in North America and Europe.

The targeted sectors are cybersecurity, healthcare, and computing infrastructure — areas where AI is still in an early adoption phase but mature enough to generate measurable economic impact. The fund is denominated in US dollars, a deliberate choice to attract international teams. Among the subscribers: La Caisse de dépôt et placement du Québec, the Business Development Bank of Canada, Investissement Québec, the Fonds de solidarité FTQ, CIBC and Fondaction.

This sixth fund is smaller than the fifth ($325 million in 2022), which reflects a repositioning towards earlier stages rather than a retreat in ambition. According to Inovia, AI-native startups captured 40% of the value of software deals in Canada in 2025.

The decision to reduce the fund size to focus on pre-revenue is a bet on upstream selection: by entering earlier, Inovia aims for lower entry valuations in a sector where growth rounds are now dominated by vehicles of a billion dollars or more — like Radical Ventures, cited in the source, which raises at this level for more mature companies. The two funds do not compete; they cover different stages of the same cycle.

1KOMMA5°: when the home energy manager becomes a virtual power plant

Source: esgtoday.com →

📬 Subscribe to the Climate & Energy Tech newsletter

1KOMMA5°, a Hamburg-based startup founded in 2021, sells integrated clean energy solutions for individuals: solar panels, storage batteries, heat pumps, air conditioners, and electric vehicle charging stations. Its central product is Heartbeat AI, a platform that connects and controls all these installed customer devices, optimizes their consumption, and resells their electricity on energy markets on their behalf.

The company announces a fundraising of over 70 million euros at a pre-money valuation of 1.35 billion dollars — up 35% compared to its July 2025 round. The round was led by b2venture and includes participation from existing investors (CalSTRS, eCapital, G2 Venture Partners, Eurazeo) and a new entrant, Marunouchi Innovation Partners, a Japanese fund specializing in growth-stage cleantech. The CFO specifies that the round was oversubscribed, that the hardware core of the business is profitable and generates positive cash flow, and that the company remains debt-free.

The funds are earmarked for an investment of over 100 million euros in the Heartbeat AI platform in 2027, targeting AI development, software capabilities, and speed to market.

What distinguishes this round from classic cleantech fundraisings is the explicit separation between hardware and software: the hardware is profitable, the software is the bet. 1KOMMA5° is not raising to survive — it is raising to transform a base of installed equipment into a European-scale energy data infrastructure. Heartbeat AI is not a comfort product; it is an attempt to become the operating system for residential energy. For an industrial investor or a French energy company, the question is not whether this model works in Germany — it is who will play this role in France.

Vessla: AI agents for managing Nordic buildings

Source: sesamers.com →

📬 Subscribe to the Construction & PropTech newsletter

Vessla, a Stockholm-based startup founded in 2026, is building an agentic operating system for property management: AI agents that overlay an existing property manager's software, execute end-to-end workflows — maintenance requests, vendor invoices, lease renewals — and only escalate decisions that require human judgment. The company is already working with some of the largest commercial and residential property owners and operators in the Nordics.

Vessla announces a pre-seed round of 3.3 million dollars (approximately 3 million euros), led by Seedcamp, with participation fromInception Fund, Tiny VC, and business angels including Tandem Health founder Lukas Saari and Voi co-founder Fredrik Hjelm. The round was more than twice oversubscribed according to the company.

The founding team brings together Orman Gazvini (ex-McKinsey, ex-Pophouse), Kasper Janehag (ex-NASA Mars 2020, ex-QuantumBlack), and Arash Dabiri (ex-Andon Labs, ex-QuantumBlack). The funds are earmarked for platform development.

Real estate is one of Europe's most fragmented and manual sectors: thousands of systems that don't communicate, recurring processes never automated due to a lack of an orchestration layer. This is exactly the kind of terrain where current AI agents show their value — not by replacing business software, but by connecting existing ones. Seedcamp's presence at this stage, for a company only a few months old, signals less a conviction about the product than a conviction about the team and the timing.

Plinto: four Peakon alumni continuously track invoicing errors

Source: sesamers.com →

Plinto, a Copenhagen-based startup founded in early 2025, sells AI agents that connect to a company's ERPs, banking tools, and collaboration platforms to continuously verify every transaction — invoices, payments, accounting entries — against contracts, budgets, and internal rules. When a discrepancy is detected (price deviating from an agreement, duplicate under another supplier name), the agent investigates, gathers evidence, contacts the supplier for a credit note, or prepares a correction for validation. Integrations primarily target the Nordic and European markets: Microsoft Business Central, Xero, Visma on the ERP side, major Scandinavian banks and over 2,700 others on the payments side, Slack and Teams for follow-ups. Customer data is processed and stored in Europe.

The company announces a pre-seed round of 2.9 million euros, co-led by People Ventures and Emblem, with business angels. The funds are earmarked for product development, enterprise customer acquisition, and initial expansion outside Denmark.

The founding team — Nermina Suljevic, Jakob Nielsen, Sebastian Rehnby, and CTO Andreas Garnæs — are all alumni of Peakon, the employee engagement platform acquired by Workday in 2021.

The addressed market is large and painful: invoicing errors in low-margin companies silently accumulate until the monthly check, often after the money has left the account. Plinto's positioning is less "new accounting software" than "a permanent vigilance layer over what already exists" — a proposition that financial departments immediately understand, especially during periods of cost pressure. For a French mid-sized company or group working with Nordic suppliers, this is also a signal: this type of tool will become widespread, and finance teams that do not prepare for it will be behind.
