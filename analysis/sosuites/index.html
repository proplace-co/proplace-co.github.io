Market DEFINITION

The market for digital marketing and social media advertising campaign management services for content creators and influencers in France, driven by net advertiser investments by national and local advertisers in influencer marketing. ➜ The buyer is a French national or local advertiser — or the agency acting for them — purchasing creator identification, campaign orchestration and performance measurement for influencer campaigns, and the job being hired is to convert a marketing budget into measurable reach and conversion through creators.

The structural friction is that 42% of national advertisers still find influencer marketing complex to activate, and 65% now route through an agency or intermediary to identify creators (versus 37% in 2022), which means the market is growing but the operational layer between advertiser and creator remains fragmented and expensive.[?] The value chain runs from creators (upstream supply) through platforms and agencies (midstream orchestration) to advertisers (downstream demand), and the profit pool today concentrates in the platforms and the largest agencies, not in the tooling layer that SOSUITES occupies.

Our Market THESIS

Net influencer-marketing investments in France grew from 323 M€ in 2022 to 587 M€ in 2025, a +81% expansion in three years, and the share of total digital spend captured by influence rose from 3.6% to 5.2% over the same period — that is the structural break, and it is already irreversible.[1][2][3] The competitive data provided does not allow us to assert that incumbent campaign-management platforms are structurally unable to respond to a collaborative-video format, because no source in the input data documents their product roadmaps or their technical constraints.

The attack vector that is specific to this moment is the combination of a 5-second collaborative format and a Stripe-based per-episode payout rail: no incumbent social platform pays co-authors per episode, and no incumbent campaign-management tool produces content, so a new entrant can occupy the intersection of creation and settlement before either side moves. The window is open because generative-video inference costs have only recently fallen to consumer scale, and it will close when either a generative-video provider (Runway, Pika, Luma) or a distribution platform (TikTok, Meta) ships a native collaborative multi-author format with built-in payouts — a realistic 18-to-24-month horizon.

Company Deep Dive

Mind map — what SOSUITES does

SOSUITES is a B2C platform that transforms a photo into a 5-second collaborative video series, where the community writes the continuation. It allows content creators and aspiring showrunners to co-write short, serialized stories, with a unique revenue-sharing promise where authors and producers receive one-third of the net profits per episode.

The solution uses AI to generate 5-second video clips with voice-overs and subtitles, and stands out with its collaborative continuation mechanism and per-episode remuneration system via Stripe. The business model is freemium with subscriptions (Creator, Studio, Pro) and episode purchases at €5.99, which trigger revenue sharing. The promotional kit (posters, trailers) aims to empower creator-led distribution.

The company initially targets French-speaking social users and micro-creators, leveraging ease of mobile production and financial incentives against established platforms like TikTok and Instagram. Although B2B potential exists via the Pro plan for brands, SOSUITES is primarily positioned as a social entertainment and collaborative content creation platform.

Prospective synthesis

SOSUITES reveals the emergence of collaborative and remunerative content formats as an engagement lever against established social platforms. The challenge lies in the profitability of micro-transactions and the ability to build a loyal network of authors and producers before giants imitate the model. The rapidly growing influencer marketing market in France shows a growing need for intermediation and solutions for creators.

Product

Core Solution

SOSUITES provides a platform for turning photos into 5-second, AI-enhanced video clips.[27] Users build collaborative series where each subsequent contributor adds to the story. The product differentiates itself through its mechanical revenue-sharing ledger and collaborative sequel format rather than the proprietary nature of the AI technology.

Feature Encyclopedia

Business Model

Business Model Analysis

The company utilizes a freemium SaaS model integrated with a revenue-sharing marketplace. Revenue is generated through tiered subscriptions and individual episode purchases, which trigger a transparent distribution of net revenue among the platform, the writers, and the producers after expenses.

Revenue Streams & Pricing Tiers

The platform offers four distinct levels: Free (0€/day for 5 clips), Creator (8.99€/month for 60 clips), Studio (24.99€/month for 200 clips), and Pro (79.99€/month for 800 clips).[?] Individual episodes are priced at 5.99€, of which a significant portion (4.34€) is allocated toward VAT, production costs, and fees prior to the revenue split.[?]

KEY COMPETITIVE ADVANTAGES

  • The 5-second collaborative sequel format is a genuinely novel content primitive: it is short enough to be produced on a phone in under a minute, which removes the production barrier that keeps most users from creating serialised content on TikTok or Reels.

  • The Stripe-based payout rail is already shipped, which means the hardest operational piece of any creator-economy product (getting money to many small contributors) is solved before scale, not after.
  • The promotional kit (posters, trailers, comics) turns each series into a self-contained marketing asset, which lowers the customer-acquisition cost per new writer because existing showrunners do the distribution work.

🧱 MOAT : WEAK

SOSUITES's only real moat mechanism is the accumulation of writers and producers inside a specific series — once a series has 20 or 30 recurring contributors who have each earned a payout, the switching cost is not the tool but the social and financial relationship with the other contributors, and that threshold is probably reached at a few hundred active series rather than a few thousand.

The compounding dynamic is that each new episode adds a new writer to the payout ledger, which increases the number of people with a financial reason to promote the next episode, which increases distribution, which attracts more writers — a genuine feedback loop, but one that only accelerates if per-episode economics stay positive. There is no meaningful secondary layer of defensibility: no proprietary model, no exclusive data, no regulatory certification and no brand equity that would protect margins if Runway, Pika or TikTok shipped a comparable collaborative format, which is the single most likely competitive event over the next 24 months.

ASYMMETRIC WAGER

The Bull Case

If SOSUITES crosses roughly 1 000 active series with recurring paying writers, the payout ledger itself becomes the product — a portable, verifiable record of who co-authored what and who is owed what — and at that point the company is no longer a video tool but the settlement layer for collaborative creative work, which is a position that generative-video incumbents cannot easily occupy because their business model is per-seat software, not per-episode royalties.

The Bear Case

The company has already bet that a 5.99€ episode price can support a 1/3-1/3-1/3 revenue split after 4.34€ of VAT, filming and fees, and if that gross margin (roughly 1.65€ per episode) cannot cover AI inference plus Stripe payout costs at scale, the entire revenue-share promise collapses into a subsidy the company cannot fund — the earliest visible signal would be a quiet removal of the episode-purchase option or a reduction of the writer/producer share within 12 to 18 months.
